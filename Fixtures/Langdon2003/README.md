# Langdon's corrugated blast wall panels under pulse pressure

`panels.json` holds the pulse-pressure tests of G. S. Langdon, *Failure of Corrugated Panels and
Supports under Blast Loading: Experimental, Analytical and Numerical Studies*, PhD thesis,
University of Liverpool, 2003 (doi:10.17638/03167700). The thesis is open to download for
non-commercial research, its copyright kept by its author, so only numbers are kept here: those of
its tables and text. The thesis itself is on StudioData (`bombcad/profiled-sheeting/langdon-2003/`,
with its `SOURCE.md`).

**The panels.** Quarter-scale offshore blast walls of 2 mm 316L stainless steel, 880 mm wide and
915 mm between their connections, corrugated in a deep-trough trapezoidal profile of 220 mm pitch
and 40.5 mm depth between the flanges' centrelines: one flange 85 mm flat (95 mm to where the webs'
centrelines meet it), the other 35 mm (45 mm), the webs 50 mm across (Table 7-1 and Fig. 5-7).
Table 7-2 gives the section's properties per corrugation. The panels were loaded in their design
("positive") direction, which puts the wide flange in tension at mid-span, and the other way
("negative"), where the wide flange buckles in compression (a class 4 section that way, class 1
the other).

**The connections.** Top and bottom, each panel was welded to a "flexible" angle of 3 mm, itself
welded to a 125 × 75 × 12 "rigid" angle bolted to the rig. The flexible length, from the toe of the
rigid angle to the end of the flexible one, was 60, 120 or 180 mm (Fig. 4-4). The sides were free,
guided by side plates that kept the corrugations from flattening.

**The steel.** Tables 3-2 to 3-4: 0.2% proof stress, ultimate strength and elongation from static
tests, the true stress and strain at rupture, and Cowper–Symonds coefficients fitted to the proof
stress over 0.0015 to 5.9 /s, for the 2 and 3 mm sheet in both directions.

**The tests.** Table 5-1, all 33 rows: the peak pressure (barg), the time to it and the duration of
the idealised triangular pulse (ms), the peak deflection at mid-span of the central corrugation
where the LVDT stayed on (mm), and the permanent deflection (mm). Where the table gives two rise
times (the chambers' blow-down measured two ways) or a range of permanent deflection, both are kept
as written. Read from a scan of the table; the numbers were checked against the text of
Section 5.4 where it repeats them.
