# Ground motion under a 1 t surface burst

`ground-motion.json` holds what C. Xia, L. Chen, R. Xu, M. Cao, D. Chen and Q. Fang measured
0.5 m below the ground at 21 and 42 m from 1 t of TNT on the ground ("Experimental and
numerical studies on ground shock generated by large equivalent surface explosions", *Applied
Sciences* 12 (2022) 7987, doi:10.3390/app12167987, CC BY 4.0). It has the test's set-up as the
paper gives it, the peak accelerations printed on its Figures 5a and 6a, the three components'
acceleration histories read off those figures, and their integrals. The user's copy of the
paper is on `$BOMBCAD_DATA/data/XiaChen2022-GroundShock/`; only these derived
numbers are kept here, with citation. `blastbench groundmotion` compares the ground shock with
them (see [Ground shock](../../docs/ground-shock.md#against-measured-ground-motion)).

**How.** The figures are vector drawings in the PDF. Each curve's stroked segments
(`pdftocairo -svg`), inside the plot and outside the legend, are mapped through least-squares
lines through the axes' major tick marks; the residuals are under 0.03 ms and 0.006 m/s². The
dashed and dash-dotted curves are joined linearly across their gaps, and the histories here are
resampled every 0.25 ms. The scripts and the raw readings are on StudioData,
`ground-motion/digitise/` (`xia_digitise.py`, `make_fixture.py`, `xia-raw.json`).

**Checks.** The digitised extremes match the values printed on the figures within 0.2 m/s² at
21 m (X's trough reads −5.62 against −5.43, Z's crest +1.49 against +1.58, where a dash-dot gap
falls) and within 0.04 m/s² at 42 m. The paper's own ratios between components and ranges
(for example, X's crest falling 88% from 21 to 42 m) are reproduced to their last digit by the
printed peaks.

**What the numbers can and cannot carry.**

- Z is vertical, taken as positive upwards; the downward peaks are its negative extremes.
- The integrated velocities remove each record's pre-event offset (0.14 to 0.18 m/s² at 21 m,
  under 0.01 at 42 m); the bounds take it removed and not. At 21 m the vertical peaks at
  0.009–0.012 m/s downward and the horizontal at 0.03 (X) and 0.045 m/s (Y); at 42 m, about
  0.001 vertical and 0.003–0.004 horizontal. The horizontal speed, √(v_x² + v_y²), peaks at
  0.054–0.058 m/s at 21 m and 0.0040–0.0042 m/s at 42 m (`integratedHorizontalSpeed`).
- The velocity sensor at 21 m (Figure 7) clipped at about 0.085 m/s for about 60 ms, more than
  the integrated accelerations reach in any component. Its component is not stated. The two
  records disagree, and which is right cannot be told from the paper.
- The records' time zero is not stated (first motion at about 12 ms at 21 m and 5 ms at 42 m;
  the velocity record's at about 290 ms), so arrival times are not compared.
- The soil is not measured: the seismic speed, 457 m/s, is the authors' fit of UFC
  3-340-01's direct-induced relation to the two peak accelerations, and the density, 2,050
  kg/m³, their numerical model's, from tests on another sand.
