**In the Quarry Face and Ridge cut windows the rock's joints have broken edges, the quarry floor and the talus at the face's foot run on without seams, and the outcrops' and crags' ends no longer stand as thin lips or square slabs.** Close in, a joint's crack gapes wider along stretches of it, where its lips are spalled in chips of paler, fresher rock bevelled into the crack, and it is darker in places and filled paler in others; short cracks lie between the joints, so the face past the strip's left end is no longer plain; a crag's joints vary the same way. The floor's grit, gravel and cobbles each fade out at their own distance, so the soft band where all the detail faded together is gone; the talus banked at the face's foot meets the face in a smooth line and runs out into the floor with no edge. On a ridge cut the soil's stones vary in size, shape and how deeply they are sunk, and soil meets rock in a crisp, wandering edge; rock thrown far by the blast keeps its turf only in ragged patches on what was its top. An outcrop's layers stand flush where it sinks into the slope, and a crag's beds curve back into the hill as they run out. Drawing only: the blasted rock, its pieces and how they move are unchanged.
