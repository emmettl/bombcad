**In the Ridge cut window and its clips, the firings' dust now merges into one plume that rises, leans with a light wind and lingers as a thinning haze, instead of an even row of puffs that had gone by 7 seconds.** The columns over the firings now spread toward each other and differ in strength, timing and height, so they rise unevenly and run together into one cloud along the crest; the low columns on the near flank are fainter and fed more slowly over a wider area, so they no longer stand as dense round balls. The cloud then thins rather than vanishing: its thick parts fall out within a few seconds, and what is left drifts slowly away over the far flank and clears over the next ten to fifteen seconds, with a light haze settling over the heap. The thin veil of dust that runs with the rock is no longer fed by blocks that have nearly stopped, by blocks near the opening view's camera, or once the rock has settled, so the lump of dust that stood by the camera at 7 seconds is gone. The rock's motion is unchanged, the draw while the ridge fires is within about 0.3 ms of before, and the Quarry Face is unchanged. After the firings the lingering haze adds about 3 ms to a frame's draw in the opening view, and more with the camera close in. Illustrative, like the rest of the window: dust for the eye, not a model of real dust, a dust hazard or a blast-design aid ([docs/ridge-cut.md](../../ridge-cut.md), "Stage 3k").
