**In the Ridge cut and Quarry Face windows, rocks on a ridge's flanks no longer draw as dark hollow shells, outcrops no longer lie on the slope as thin slabs, and the quarry floor's gravel is crushed, angular rock.** About a fifth of the outcrop pieces on the flanks were drawn inside out (their meshes whole, their normals pointing into the rock), so they were lit from behind and read as dark brown cupped shells; their outward side is now taken from how each piece is swept along the slope. An outcrop that would show only a thin slab or lip above the slope is tipped back so it stands as a block, and a piece's ends sink over a short way rather than running down to a point. The floor's stones are faceted, sharp-edged and of many sizes and tones, rather than smooth pebbles. Drawing only: the blasted rock, its pieces and how they move are unchanged, and a frame costs about the same. Illustrative, like the rest of the window ([docs/hillside-blast.md](../../hillside-blast.md), "Stage 3p").
