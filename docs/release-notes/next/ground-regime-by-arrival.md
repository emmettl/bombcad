**Ground points know when the ground's own wave outruns the blast:** each point now takes the time
the ground's wave from the charge first reaches it through the soil's layers (the direct wave, or
the head wave along a deeper, faster layer such as saturated soil under a water table), and is
labelled outrun wherever that comes before the air, however fast the air's front still is. The
ground shock chart names the followed point's regime, in orange with the lead in milliseconds where
it is outrun; `--ground-results` gives `groundArrival`. Under Prairie Flat's 500 t the label finds
the outrunning limit between 400 and 560 ft, where Murrell found it at about 560 ft; the front's
speed alone never did ([Prairie Flat](../../ground-shock.md#against-prairie-flat)). *Illustrative:*
against Prairie Flat the column and the estimate are within a factor of two at under half the
gauges inside that limit (too low near the surface), and the half-space carries the motion ahead of
the blast past it within a factor of two.
