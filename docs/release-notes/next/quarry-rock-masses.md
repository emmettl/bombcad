**In the Quarry Face and Ridge cut windows the outcrops on a ridge's flanks no longer read as rows from above, a crag's ends step down into the slope, the cut's margin loses its green plates after the blast, and the quarry floor is gravel.** An outcrop is drawn in short pieces, each a little offset and turned against the next, with lone blocks among them, so from above the flanks show scattered rock rather than lines along the beds; a crag's beds run out a step at a time where it goes under the slope, its ends settle into the slope in broken blocks, and talus lies at its ends. On a ridge cut the render-only rock round the cut, once the blast has laid it bare, holds its turf only in torn mats back from the rim, its sides facing the cut are bare rock, and rock that heaved without being thrown has its turf split along the heave. The quarry floor is covered in gravel of the beds' rock, with fines between, its tone the same near and far, and the talus at the face's foot is graded into it in colour. A joint's chipped lips are separate scoops that catch the light on one side of the crack and are shaded on the other, rather than a pale line beside it. Drawing only: the blasted rock, its pieces and how they move are unchanged. Illustrative, like the rest of the window ([docs/hillside-blast.md](../../hillside-blast.md), "Stage 3o").
