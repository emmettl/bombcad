**In the Quarry Face window and its clips, the dust now has the ridge cut's look: billowing, sunlit dust with no dark grain, the firings' dust running together into one bank along the face, then drifting along the bench and thinning; and in the Ridge cut the plume no longer hides the rock running down the near flank.** The Quarry Face's dust was still the old one, speckled where it was thin, dark and grainy underneath, and its later firings stood as a row of round puffs. It now uses the same dust as the Ridge cut, lit a paler cream, with its own wind (along the strip, away from the camera) and its own firings: each firing's dust billows out of the face and off the bench's top within half a second, unevenly, and reaches its neighbours, so the strip's dust rises as one bank the length of the face, stands and drifts along the bench, and thins to a haze over it by about 20 seconds, while the trench in front of the new face clears. In the Ridge cut, the low dust over the near flank at 3.6 to 4.5 seconds is now thinner and broken, so the rock running down shows through it under the same plume, and the lingering haze is cheaper to draw: about 2 ms less a frame with the camera close in on the crest, and about 1 ms less over the whole event in the opening view. The rock's motion is unchanged in both scenes, and both draw as before while the rock fires. Illustrative, like the rest of the window: dust for the eye, not a model of real dust, a dust hazard or a blast-design aid ([docs/hillside-blast.md](../../hillside-blast.md) and [docs/ridge-cut.md](../../ridge-cut.md), "Stage 3l").
