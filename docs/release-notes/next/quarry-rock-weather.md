**In the ridge cut and Quarry Face scenes, crags look weathered, outcrops end in blocks and gravel looks crushed.** A ridge's crags have rounded edges and corners, uneven tops and beds that thicken and thin, and their foot is partly buried in scree, with stones banked along it, so the slope no longer meets their layers in straight lines. Outcrop pieces end broad instead of narrowing to a point or a hooked tail in plan, and thin upright plates are gone. The quarry floor's gravel and the ridge's scree and soil are cut by planes at random instead of a top facet and a ring of sides, the smallest grains are angular chips, and their tone stays close to the fines. A chipped crack lip turned away from the sun no longer reads as a dark hole.
