Eleventh build of BombCAD, an interactive blast simulator for Macs.

- macOS 15 or later, Apple silicon (arm64), Metal GPU
- Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, with the ticket stapled: it opens without
  Gatekeeper warnings
- Version 0.7.1, build 16, from commit COMMIT

**Install with Homebrew:** `brew install emmettl/tap/bombcad` installs the app and links its
headless runner as `bombcad`.

**The main changes**, each in full below:
- **Charges on sloping ground now get the mapped start**, 0.7.0's known limitation: on Beirut's
  terrain the impulse is about 9% higher on 4 m cells, and the same at every refinement within 2%.
- **The quick estimate's façade loads refitted** on runs with that start, refined to 2 m: lower
  behind buildings close to a charge among them (1.2 times the runs' peaks there, from 1.7), and
  concrete walls ×1.15 in place of ×1.3.
- **Learned field model 2:** the option now gives the peak at every storey's façades, and its walls
  pass the calibration test. Still an option, off by default, and illustrative.
- **Run on a Mac nearby, faster and fuller:** frames on a sealed socket of their own (40–45 MB/s
  over Wi-Fi, two and a half to six times as many frames shown), **Pause**, **Resume** and **Rerun
  with Edits**, and the fragments, thermal radiation, ground points and cloud run there too.
- **Structures past failure no longer blow up:** no more corners thrown at kilometres a second, and
  rubble no longer rings at the joints or pumps energy into a collapsing building. **Structures
  step faster**, the same to the bit (1,227 to 1,370 steps a second on a 225,000-element building).
- **The crater grows in the replay** over its formation time, carried on past the run's end.
- **A first iPad viewer, a preview:** it replays the runs a Mac nearby keeps. Tried in the iPad
  Simulator only.
- **Macs that share work, and the iPad viewer, all need 0.7.1** (worker protocol 18).

## New

**Pause a run on a Mac nearby, edit the scene, and run it again with the edits.** Pause (or
Space) holds the run there between batches, to the same result once resumed; while it is paused
the sidebar unlocks, and **Rerun with Edits** stops it and sends the scene as it is now over the
same connection, naming the files already sent rather than sending them again. The connection
stays open five minutes after a run comes back, so Run after an edit does the same. A run sent
elsewhere now also runs the project's fragments, thermal radiation, ground points and cloud there,
their results kept with the run, and vents the envelopes' glazing as a run here does (before, it
did neither); the viewport still draws only the blast and the structures. See
[Running the blast on a Mac nearby](../run-comparison.md#running-the-blast-on-a-mac-nearby).

**A replay shows the crater growing, and the USD export animates it.** On earth or rock, a run's
replay now digs each crater over the time it takes to form, from flat ground at t = 0: its bowl
widens and deepens, and its lip rises last. Scrubbing back fills it in again. The time is
Holsapple's gravity-regime formation time, 0.8 √(V^(1/3) / g) of the crater's volume (0.33 s for
100 kg of TNT on alluvium), and the bowl grows as the point-source power law t^(μ/(1+μ)); the
standing is illustrative. Most runs end long before then, so the replay carries on past the run's
last frame in a crater tail, the air and structures held, while the crater finishes forming in
real time; the bar marks it ("Crater forming, run ended at 4.0 ms") and the slider shows where the
run ends. Nothing more is stored, so older recordings get the tail too. The USD's crater patch
grows the same way over the run's frames, then the cloud's, then a tail of its own where needed;
before, it was flat until the last frame. See [how it grows](../cratering.md#how-it-grows).

**A first iPad viewer, a preview: it replays the runs a Mac keeps, scrubbed from its first frame.**
BombCAD now builds for iPadOS as a viewer, from the source (`Scripts/build-ipad-viewer.sh`; it is
not part of this download). It pairs with a Mac sharing its GPU as another Mac would, lists the
kept runs with recordings in the projects that Mac shares (`BombCAD serve --share <project>`, or
the saved projects open in the app while it shares), fetches one over the session's sealed socket
and replays it with the app's renderer, its vent panels, envelope colours, thermal paint and cloud
as Replay shows them; frames scrubbed to before they arrive are sent next. Tap a building to
outline it and see its damage at the frame shown, what its surfaces took and the run's standing for
both. Tried in the iPad Simulator on 23 October 2026: the made-up district's recording, 233 frames
and 252 MB, came whole in 0.7 s over loopback, its first frame on screen after 0.45 s, a frame
shown in 5 ms; over the Wi-Fi measured on 22 October that is about six seconds.
`Scripts/build-ipad-viewer.sh --device` signs it for a real iPad with the team's development
certificate, and it then keeps its pairings in the Keychain, as the Mac does
([the spatial viewer](../spatial-viewer.md)).

**Tools and the record.**

- **A third code on the open benchmark: Clawpack.** Clawpack 5.14.0 ran the two decks, the free
  field as a 2-D axisymmetric surface burst with a mesh study from 0.5 m to 16 mm and the room in
  3-D on the deck's own mesh. It scores 0.96 on the free field and 0.60 on the room, as pyro2 does
  (0.7.0's notes give pyro2's run), and its impulses and arrivals converge to pyro2's, its peaks
  1–7% below. BombCAD's own 0.5 m control scores 0.50 on the free field
  ([a third code](../open-benchmark.md#a-third-code-clawpack-22-october-2026)).
- **The Linux lane and the PCs' disks** (for developers). The pre-push check's Linux lane no
  longer counts a PC that answers but cannot write its staging folder, so its suites run on the
  Mac instead. The PCs' training and sweep folders are kept in bounds: old datasets, jobs and
  stages are pruned once their results are safely back, and a send that would leave a PC's disk
  under 50 GB (or 15% of a smaller disk) is refused
  ([retention and free space](../linux.md#retention-and-free-space-23-october-2026)).
- **The CUDA training-data worker** (for developers). On the corrected terrain start, the flat and
  slope pilots pass every target in all three channels, and Beirut's failures shrink to one
  first-phase impulse; bulk generation stays held. Opt-in diagnostics behind that work trace the
  native timestep's inputs (`field-export --trace-clock-inputs`,
  `--precise-step-selector-diagnostic`), the raw cell inputs behind first-phase pressure samples,
  the GPU's sweep updates (`acoustic-phase --trace-updates`) and the source cells on the host, and
  a compensated 1-D sweep is tried as a research candidate. None changes a solver default or the
  rules for accepting data ([the CUDA backend](../cuda-backend.md#current-acceptance--22-october-2026)).

## Changed

**A run on a Mac nearby shows two and a half to six times as many frames over Wi-Fi.** A session's
frames and results now go on a plain socket beside its encrypted connection, sealed with keys
from the pairing (AES-GCM, a fresh key each way each session), since macOS's network framework
carried frames at 4 to 7 MB/s where the same Wi-Fi carries 40. Measured with the mini watching the
Studio on 22 October 2026: 40 to 45 MB/s back and 34 to 38 out, and the city block at every 2nd
cell showed 99 to 161 of 171 frames instead of 30 to 55. If the socket cannot be opened the session
goes on the encrypted connection as before; `BombCAD nearby probe <Mac>` measures both. See
[Macs nearby](../distributed-computing.md#the-bulk-on-a-socket-and-the-thin-clients-controls-22-october-2026).

**Quick estimate: façade loads refitted, and lower behind buildings close to a charge among
them.** Beirut's ten near boxes refined to 2 m, behind 0.7.0's ×1.3 for concrete walls, had
started their charge as the balloon (below); rerun on the mapped start, they judged the walls the
same. The surrogate's terms are then refitted on BombCAD's runs after the terrain start fix, on air
refined to 2 m, with five of Beirut's near boxes among them. Close in, behind buildings, the loads
now fall the narrower the charge's surroundings: against Beirut's charges among buildings those
façades read 1.2 times the runs' peaks, where they read 1.7, and behind the grain silos 1.06 of the
2 m runs (1.09 before). Across Beirut's full runs, building sides whose glass state agrees with the
run's go from 75–84% to 78–84%, and the glass chances score better (blown out, Brier skill 0.84
from 0.81). The new terms carry most of the walls' old load factor, so concrete walls' chances now
take ×1.15 in place of ×1.3: skill 0.61 from 0.59, still within each tenth of the runs. Roofs (skill
0.74, their middle chances 0.11–0.18 low) and stone are unchanged, and the field model's walls keep
their factors (the peak ×1.15, the impulse ×0.95). The hybrid far field's share of sides broken at
0.2 kt beyond 1.5 km now comes within 0.06 of the full run's (it fell 0.16–0.28 short); at 1.1 kt
it reads 1.12–1.19 times the full run's peaks there (below). See
[the first look](../surrogate-first-look.md#adopted-23-october-2026).

**Learned field model 2: the peak at every storey.** The quick estimate's learned field option
(still off by default) now gives the peak at every storey's façades, not just the ground floor's.
Field model 1's network still does the ground floor, and a second network, trained on BombCAD's own
runs at more heights, does the storeys above. The chances draw each network's own scatter. Roofs
and the impulse stay on the surrogate. Judged against Beirut's runs refined to 2 m, a city it never
saw:

- glass blown out by building side scores Brier skill 0.44 against field model 1's 0.35, and 0.43
  against 0.32 above the ground floor;
- concrete walls damaged moderately or worse score 0.656 against field model 1's 0.654, and both
  pass the tenths test, on the refitted façade terms above (on the terms before, field model 2's
  walls missed it by one tenth);
- roofs are unchanged.

It is illustrative. The two files, 31.4 MB together, download from the `field-model-2` release only
when you click Download. An install that has field model 1 keeps working as before, and is offered
the second file (15.7 MB) under "A newer model is available". **Check on this Mac**, or
`BombCAD field-model check` without a window, runs the networks on a built-in 5 km district. It
reports the GPU, the times, the peak memory and whether the model fits. A plan too large for the
GPU is refused with a message and left to the surrogate, never run. See
[learned models](../learned-models.md#field-model-2-in-the-app-23-october-2026).

**Structures step faster, with the same results to the bit.** A concrete element far from
cracking no longer works out its principal strains, which only a crack needs, and a body whose
faces carry no load (run on its own, or settling under its own weight before the blast) gets an
element kernel without the faces' loads. On the 225,000-element building alone that is 1,227 to
1,370 steps a second; most of what the structure lost since 3 October was the code of rarely
taken paths, not their work ([performance](../performance.md#structure-step-cost)).

**Macs that share work all need 0.7.1 (worker protocol 18).** A worker, a Mac watching another's
run, a Mac running one for another and the iPad viewer must be on the same version; an older one is
turned away when it connects.

## Fixed

**Charges on sloping ground now get the mapped start.** A charge sitting on terrain was always
started as a rough sphere of hot gas, instead of the accurate one-dimensional solution mapped onto
the grid that flat ground gets. Its loads then depended on how fine the cells were: on Beirut's
district the blast's impulse read 14–17% low on finely refined air. The ground under the charge is
now treated as the mirror it is, along its own slope. A charge on a gentle slope reads as on flat
ground, and Beirut's impulse rises about 9% on 4 m cells, the same at every refinement within 2%.
This was the known limitation noted for 0.7.0. Runs of charges on terrain made before it read a
lower impulse; flat ground is unchanged.

**Structures past failure no longer blow up.** A concrete element crushed or sheared far past
failure could outrun the structure's fixed time step and ring unstably, throwing its corners at
kilometres a second within a millisecond. On the large-panel block, a wall's failed elements
opposite the charge put two hundred times the blast's energy into the building at 0.6 s, and a
flank wall's at 14 km/s. Such elements are now removed, or left as their bars, as soon as they pass
the step's reach. Bare elements so distorted lose their hourglass control. A cracked concrete
element squeezed out of shape could also read as crushed solid the moment it was lightly pressed
from every side, and throw a corner at a kilometre a second; its crushing now counts from the shape
it had when pressed, as block masonry's already did. Runs past failure, the whole-event runs
especially, no longer carry those events. Elements that never get that far are unchanged.

**Rubble no longer blows a run up at the joints between parts.** With failed concrete kept as
rubble particles (an option), a joint whose node had lost its concrete to a particle stayed as
stiff as for the whole node and rang far beyond the step: on the large-panel block a node was
driven to 18 km/s at 0.62 s. A joint now bears in proportion to the mass its nodes have left;
without rubble nothing changes ([whole-event simulation](../whole-event.md)).

**Rubble no longer pumps energy into a collapsing building.** A failed element's rubble sat
caged by its corners. As the building settled they closed in on it, and contact pushed them out
again every step. Nodes left with only grams of steel took less push than they gave. On the
large-panel block the two together put a sixth again of the blast's energy into the building by
1.5 s, and threw a few nodes at hundreds of metres a second. Rubble now crushes rather than
springs back, and two nodes push each other equally. Runs without rubble are unchanged.

**Thermal output over terrain no longer stalls a district run.** Each sight line from a receiver
to the fireball looked up the terrain's highest point by scanning every one of its nodes, so on a
district's heightfield the thermal march spent nearly all its time there and a run with thermal
receivers made no progress for over an hour. The highest point is now found once. Results are
unchanged, bit for bit.

**Pairing two Macs leaves both paired or neither.** A sharing Mac whose user chose Allow after the
other Mac had stopped waiting (five minutes) kept the pairing and said it was paired, with a Mac
that had kept nothing; now it keeps the key only once the other Mac is known to be still there,
and that Mac only once the sharing Mac has kept it (emmettl/bombcad-work#76). See
[Macs nearby](../distributed-computing.md#macs-nearby-without-ssh).

**Interface fixes from a look at 0.7.0's new panels.** In **Envelope building damage**, Colour by
sits on a row of its own with shorter names, so "Chance walls damaged" and the others are no longer
cut short, and what the longer names said is shown beneath; the walls' and roofs' colourings now
show their legend; the line under the glass's chances no longer says walls and roofs are given
none. A building's rows read "Façade 3" and "Façade 3 glass" in place of two rows both called
"Facade 3.0", a rotation that rounds to nought reads 0.00°, not -0.00°, and its construction and
chances lines sit at the left edge instead of centred. The close calls' line says "its" for one
building. The Glass picker reads "As designed (E1300)", which fits. A failed download of the
learned field model no longer ends its reason with two full stops. On a Mac nearby, the lost
connection's third button is **Stop**, as elsewhere, and the run's name is quoted in "“On Studio”
came back from Studio". Show counts in **People in the scene** sits at the left edge, and reads
"72 people placed, evening (5 pm)" ([the interface review](../native-interface-review.md#since-060-23-october-2026)).

## Known limitations

**The learned field model is illustrative, and off by default.** Field model 2's walls now pass
the calibration test on the refitted façade terms, but it gives only the façades' peaks: the
impulse and the roofs stay on the surrogate. It was trained on BombCAD's own runs of made-up
districts and judged on one real city's, Beirut's, and it needs a download before it can be turned
on ([learned models](../learned-models.md#field-model-2-in-the-app-23-october-2026)).

**The 2 m judge boxes' outer ring was cut short.** Beirut's ten near boxes refined to 2 m, which
judge the walls' factors, the refit and the field models, were run to 3 s, before the blast
reached their outer ring: beyond about 1.2 km at 1.5 kt (0.9 km at 150 t) their strips' histories
are cut short or empty, and the far bands' judgements, glass beyond 1 km most of all, lean on them.
They are being rerun to their corners
([learned models](../learned-models.md#the-models-scatter-measured-on-2-m-runs-23-october-2026)).

**The hybrid far field reads high beyond 1.5 km at 1.1 kt.** On the refitted façade terms its peaks
there are 1.12–1.19 times the full run's (0.95–1.19 across the yields judged, from 0.76–1.04 before),
on the safe side: the surrogate's own over-read at that yield, which the old trend had cancelled by
chance. A trend that would raise the match outward is now held, which had read them 1.2–1.3
([the hybrid far field](../hybrid-far-field.md#on-the-refitted-surrogate-23-october-2026)).

**People in the scene is illustrative and relative.** Its models come from other events and
hazards, and on Beirut 2020 its counts read 4 to 12 times the injuries and deaths recorded, so the
counts are behind Show counts with that warning; read it for where people would be hurt and for
the change against a baseline, not for numbers ([people in the scene](../people-in-the-scene.md)).

**A run on a Mac nearby draws only the blast and the structures as it goes.** The fragments, the
thermal radiation, the ground points and the cloud now run there and come back with the run, but
the fragments' dots, the thermal paint, the ground points and the envelopes' damage colours are not
sent, so they are not drawn during the run or in its recording. **Continue from here** is not
offered for a run elsewhere. The Mac sharing the run needs a signed build, such as this release
([running on a Mac nearby](../run-comparison.md#running-the-blast-on-a-mac-nearby)).

**The iPad viewer has been tried in the Simulator only.** It is not yet measured on a real iPad:
its Wi-Fi, its GPU drawing the field, and its memory with a 250 MB recording
([the spatial viewer](../spatial-viewer.md)).

**Whole-event runs are not offered.** The large-panel block carried past its collapse with rubble
reaches 3.4 s, but its energy budget stops closing from about 1.5 s, and hourglass control does
54–61% of the elements' work, so collapse stays illustrative and the run is not offered in the app
([whole-event simulation](../whole-event.md)).

**The quick estimate's walls and roofs are still estimates.** They are not to be relied on for a
building's level; stone walls' chances read high by up to 0.18, roofs' middle chances low, and
failure gets no chance ([walls and roofs](../glass-probability.md#walls-and-roofs)).

**Barriers and buildings in front still barely change the quick estimate's loads** (as in 0.7.0;
[protective design](../protective-design.md#stage-1-shielding-as-a-factor-stopped)).

**Full Keyboard Access and VoiceOver have not been checked on a real screen.** The labels and
keyboard paths were reviewed offscreen, which cannot test focus order or speech
([native interface review](../native-interface-review.md)).
