Sixth build of BombCAD, an interactive blast simulator for Macs.

- macOS 15 or later, Apple silicon (arm64), Metal GPU
- Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, with the ticket stapled: it opens without
  Gatekeeper warnings
- Version 0.4.0, build 10, from commit COMMIT

**Fixed first: 0.3.0 crashes on New Document,** or whenever it first builds a structure's
solver. A resource bundle of the contact search it takes from ContinuumKit was never copied into
the app. Every bundle the binary links is now packaged. The release runs a small project from
inside the packaged app before signing and again before archiving, and draws a new document's
window offscreen while switching its source between a charge and a gas cloud. Replace 0.3.0 with
this build.

What's new since 0.3.0:

- **Defaults changed.** Results from this build differ from 0.3.0's for the same project:
  - **The structures' outline in the air** is counted by each element's overlap with the air's
    cells, with a little hysteresis, not at the elements' centres. Mendonça's slabs' first peaks
    move by −7% to +2%. Hupfauf's SN142 and SN131 far faces become 77 and 27 m/s (61 and 22
    before), within the spread of their roundings. Keys's brick debris moves by −1% to +3%. The
    rest of the case matrix is unchanged or moves under 0.1 mm. The app's "Outline structures by
    overlap" turns it off, and saved runs record which was used.
  - **Gauges inside a solid cell** (a structure moved into it) read the nearest fluid cell beside
    it, at the finest level, instead of the solid cell's stale state. Across air cells of 24 to
    26 mm, which move the face through a cell, the spread of Mendonça's centre gauge's reflected
    peak falls from 32% to 16%, and of Wang's slab's gauge impulses from 46% to 5%. On the validated
    runs only Mendonça's gauges move (corner impulses by 1–1.5%); Wang's, Alsubaei's, the chamber's
    and the close-in slab's read the same, bit for bit.
  - **Default steel ruptures at 15% strain over the 100 mm window,** a typical bar's plastic
    elongation at fracture (12% before). Across the record only Reynolds's slab HSCV4 depends on
    it: it breaks below 14% and holds at 14% and above (at 15%, 161 mm against 182 measured). The
    value was set after blind test 3 was revealed, with that slab in view, so it is calibration and
    does not change the test's score (below). Saved projects keep their steel's values.
  - **Hot air** finds its temperature in one Newton step. Results move in the last bits, no
    printed validation figure changed, and runs on the recommended air take 1.05–1.08 times less.
  - **The advisor turns on adaptive substeps** where they pay: each refined level takes only the
    substeps its own waves need. They are on for refined runs in the open and the street, not
    close in. The open blast's peaks move up 1–2.5%, towards Kingery–Bulmash; impulses and
    arrival times move under 1%. They are off unless the advisor or the user (Sharpen shocks ▸
    Adaptive substeps) turns them on.
- **New: damage for envelope buildings,** one way, in the app and headless. The air still sees
  each building rigid and whole. The display is fully tested in code but has been looked at only in
  offscreen captures, not yet on a real display.
  - **What it is.** Each wall panel, roof and pane is a single-degree-of-freedom system after UFC
    3-340-02, driven by the load the run records on it. It matches UFC's worked examples within 1%.
  - **What isn't covered,** as the standing says first:
    - freestanding (cantilever) walls;
    - any comparison with measurement, for concrete, brick or glass;
    - frame action: each panel is judged alone;
    - everything is compared with the detailed model on one mesh only.
  - **Glass, an approximation.** Panes break or not, and when. Against the same buildings free to
    respond in the detailed model, it breaks all 45 panes those break, a median 1.7 ms early, and
    7 more under light loads, wrong on the safe side.
  - **Concrete, an approximation.** Panel rotation is given as a range between fixed and simply
    supported edges, judged by ASCE/SEI 59-11's limits for single-reinforced slabs. The detailed
    walls facing the charge lie between the two bounds; the fixed bound alone would read low (a
    median 0.59 of the detailed rotation), the unsafe side, so read the range's worse end. Roofs,
    and walls beside a failing one, go further than both, so a building with a panel past 2° is
    flagged as beyond the one-way model.
  - **Brick, illustrative.** Cracked or fallen, without arching; no citable limits were found.
  - **The inside, illustrative.** The pressure through broken panes, from a simple fill model, is
    0.87–2.23 times the detailed buildings' inside gauges. A breached concrete wall lets no air in.
  - **Construction.** Each envelope is built of concrete or brick. This is read from an IFC
    import's material layers or a map building's `building:material` tag; otherwise it is assumed,
    and the standing says how many were. It can be changed in the editor, and is saved.
  - **Shown** in the sidebar's Envelope building damage section, with a legend. In the view, each
    building is coloured by its level, in colours chosen to stay apart under common colour-vision
    deficiencies. "Export damage…" writes it all, and so does `BombCAD run … --envelope-damage
    <file.json>`.
- **New: soft ground under debris.** In the app: Run ▸ Terrain ▸ Debris lands on: Rigid ground
  (the default) or Soil, a Winkler bed. On Keys's wall WR1A, soil brings the debris's mean reach
  from 2.10 to 1.36 m, against 0.23 m measured: still six times too far.
- **Faster.**
  - Envelope buildings' surface records skip still air: the neighbourhood's run takes 0.81 times as
    long (19% less); the street's is unchanged. Recording each component's history for its damage
    adds 7–10%.
  - Terrain's cut cells cost less: the ridge's extra cost over a staircase falls from 25% to 15% a
    step, the slope's from 20% to 13%.
  - Adaptive substeps (above): refined runs take 1.19 times less on open ground refined once,
    1.31–1.33 times less refined twice, and 1.07 in the street.
- **Fixed.**
  - New Document (above).
  - **Removing a gas cloud** (Source ▸ Charge) stopped the app, where its section read the cloud
    after it had gone.
  - **Glazing.** A pane or door inside a wall's opening was cut away with the opening, so it was
    missing from the air and from the structure. It is now kept, and IFC windows are glazed unless
    they say otherwise.
  - **Refined air.** A step of zero length, at the end of a batch cut short by its time limit, could
    fill the air beside a moving structure with NaN.
  - **Terrain.** A whole air cell beside a cliff or a plateau read another column's wall, and a
    closed box over such ground gained 0.24% of its mass in 200 steps.
- **The record.**
  - **Bands on the shipped air.** Each error band holds only on the air it was found on. Five
    comparisons were rerun on the shipped air. The scene harnesses and benchmarks now start from
    it; validate and the chamber had been left on the ideal gas.
  - **0.3.0's open-air figures were afterburning's alone.** On the shipped air, on 0.25 m cells
    from 1 m/kg^(1/3), against Kingery–Bulmash:
    - incident peaks 74–84%;
    - incident impulse 94–100%;
    - reflected impulse 93–113%.
  - **The internal-explosion chamber's roof** rises 50 mm and is left 27 mm up on the shipped air,
    against 95 mm left measured.
  - **Blind test 3: Reynolds's high-strength slabs** in the blast load simulator, predicted on
    v0.3.0 before the measurements were unsealed. *Worse* by its rule, on one slab: HSCV4 broke
    through in the model (its bars ruptured) where the test held at 182 mm. That error is on the
    safe side. The other peaks are 0.77–1.06. No blast face crushed in the model, where four of the
    tests' did, which is on the unsafe side.
  - **Blind test 2's caveats** (Mendonça's slabs; the verdict stands, *worse*).
    - The meters that the four-to-ten-times gap was measured against likely include the supports'
      movement and a lift-off.
    - The model's slab responds as its own load says.
    - That load's suction came from the frozen build's copying faces, which are no longer the
      default. Even on the corrected load, no bending slab reaches the readings: what remains lies
      in the meters or in the test's load, outside the slab's bending.
    - Both collapses were still missed.
  - **Reynolds's normal-strength slabs RSCR4–6,** compared openly in the contest slab's rig: peaks
    0.91–1.06, all standing as tested. The thesis gives no pressure history for them, so each
    took the shape of the nearest high-strength shot. The contest's own record scaled down instead
    gives 0.56–0.68, so the load's shape decides the agreement.
  - **Vented gas deflagrations** on far-field faces: FM Global's six tests read 0.9–1.8 lit at the
    back wall and 0.4–0.6 in the middle, a geometric mean of 0.76 (0.65 before).

Known gaps, as the standing says, those on the unsafe side (predicting too little damage) first:

- **Collapses are missed.** Collapse has been compared once, blind, and both collapses were missed.
- **Punching under impact** is not predicted: the cone forms but cannot slide.
- **Slabs are too strong.** Statically the model's slab holds about twice its yield line. Wu's slabs under small charges reach
  40–70% of the measured peaks. The load, the supports, the bars and their bond, cracked
  concrete's strength and stiffness, hourglass control, arching, an embedded crack and the mesh have each been ruled out as the cause, which is
  not found. Two comparisons once counted here are now doubtful:
  - Mendonça's 2.0 m slabs: the model responds as its load says, so the gap lies in the meters or
    the load;
  - the contest slab's lower shot, 79–88%: on a measured shot's shape instead of a scaled one it
    reads 97%, though that shape is another shot's.
- **No blast face crushes:** none in the model where four of blind test 3's tests crushed.
- **Close-in charges do too little damage:** slabs a third to a half as far down as measured, too
  little spall, never holed by default.
- **Shear is too strong** (4–11% on fine meshes, 32% on 12 elements), and short beams struck to a
  shear failure fail by the wrong mechanism.
- **The internal-explosion chamber's roof is too stiff**, left under a third as far up as measured.
- **Peak pressures read low** unless the air is refined (incident peaks 74–84% on 0.25 m cells).
- **Debris** off slabs under contact charges is held back rather than thrown. Brick walls' debris
  lands too far in the open (the safe side) but half as far as measured in a shock tube.
- **Structural results do not converge** as the mesh is refined. The bands hold only on the meshes
  they were derived on.
- **After failure,** results are one draw among roundings of the arithmetic. A beam's moment after
  failure moves by up to a quarter, and debris speeds by about ±10%.
- **A crushed bare element can throw nodes under contact charges.** Under Hupfauf's SN144 charge,
  an element whose concrete is gone and whose bars remain, squeezed flat, threw nodes at about
  2,000 m/s in two of six runs. Its hourglass control is the cause. Two options stop it, but
  neither is a default: both send another slab's debris two to three times further from its test.
- **Illustrative effects:** the fireball radiates about a fifth of the charge's energy, against
  4–7% measured for TNT, and vented gas peaks are low in the room's middle.

`docs/validation.md`, `docs/blind-tests.md` and `docs/validation-review.md` give the details.

Download `BombCAD-0.4.0-macos-arm64.zip`, expand it and move `BombCAD.app` to Applications. To check
the download:

```
shasum -a 256 -c BombCAD-0.4.0-macos-arm64.zip.sha256
```

It is a study of the numerics, the physics and the performance of blast simulation, not a design
tool, and nothing in it should be used to judge the safety of a real structure. MIT licence.
