Eighth build of BombCAD, an interactive blast simulator for Macs.

- macOS 15 or later, Apple silicon (arm64), Metal GPU
- Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, with the ticket stapled
- Version 0.6.0, build _, from commit _

**Install with Homebrew:** `brew install emmettl/tap/bombcad` installs the app and links its
headless runner as `bombcad` ([releasing](../releasing.md#after-publishing-the-homebrew-cask)).

What you can now do, each with its [standing](../standing.md) (how far the result has been
compared with measurement):

## New

**Materials look like what they are.** Concrete, steel, profiled sheeting, glass, timber, brick,
blockwork and terrain are drawn in their own restrained colours in the viewport, in snapshots and
in a watched run, and failed elements are dark grey rubble that stays distinct from every stage of
damage under the three common colour-vision deficiencies ([using the
app](../using-the-app.md#the-view-and-the-sidebar)).

**Buildings from architectural floor plans.** A new guide takes a 2D plan through FreeCAD's BIM
workbench to IFC and into BombCAD as shells and panes, with a worked two-storey house in
`Samples/Plans`; the IFC importer now reads a wall's layers by their material and keeps a cavity
wall's second leaf as mass ([from architectural plans](../using-the-app.md#from-architectural-plans)).

**A first look at a district's envelope damage in seconds.** Simulation ▸ Quick Estimate of
Envelope Damage (headless, `BombCAD run --surrogate <file.json>`) predicts each component's load
from where it stands and judges it with the run's own damage model: about half a minute for
Beirut's 724,512 components, against 25–28 minutes for a run ([first
look](../surrogate-first-look.md)). *Approximation for glass:* each building side's glass state
agrees with Beirut's full runs for 75–84% of sides; walls and roofs are not to be relied on, and it
is always shown as an estimate, never as a run.

**A city in minutes (headless).** `scenebench district-hybrid` runs the air in 3-D only out to a
hand-over radius and takes each façade's load beyond it from the surrogate, matched to the near
run: on Beirut, handing over at 1 km, the near run took 135–335 s against the full run's
1,604–2,464 s on the CI Mac mini ([a city in minutes](../hybrid-far-field.md)). The share of
building sides with some glass broken comes within 0.03 of the full run's to 1.5 km at every
yield; it is not in the app yet.

**Infer the charge from what was seen.** `scenebench infer-charge` takes the shock's arrival times
at observers and gives the TNT yield and the place, optionally a height and clock offset, each with
a credible interval, in about a second ([charge inference](../charge-inference.md)).
*Approximation:* from Rigby et al.'s observers it finds Beirut at 0.50–0.54 kt (90%: about
0.36–0.85 kt) with Warehouse 12 within 25–32 m; broken glass can be added but biases the yield
low, so it is illustrative and off by default.

**File ▸ New District offers refined air, and over a city it now pays.** The advisor chooses
between uniform cells and a coarser base refined where the shock is, taking refinement where it is
finer at the façades and no dearer, and refinement now stops at a ceiling over the ground
(`SolverConfiguration.refinementCeiling`). Over Beirut's 1 km box, 8 m cells refined once to 4 m
kept the 4 m cells' glass and arrivals in 0.44 of their run time, with façade peaks 2–12% lower
([Beirut](../beirut-2020.md#what-cell-size-the-glass-and-the-damage-need), [using the
app](../using-the-app.md#a-district-from-a-map)).

**Easier by keyboard and VoiceOver.** Menu items now reach what only toolbar and panel buttons
did (File ▸ Import Model… ⇧⌘I, Export Results as CSV…; Simulation ▸ Keep Run… ⌘K, Compare Runs…
⇧⌘K, Parameter Sweep…), fields and buttons say which they are, sheets open in their first field,
and Tab no longer stops on the 3D view ([native interface
review](../native-interface-review.md#on-the-real-screen-labels-and-keyboard)).

### Structures

**Steel connections.** A steel beam that stops at a column, a through member or a shell now meets
it through a hinge that is plastic at the connection's moment and lets go at its fracture turn:
welded unreinforced flanges, reduced beam sections, fin plates and bolted end plates ([steel
connections](../shell-model.md#steel-connections)). *A check, not a validation:* under a column's
removal the NIST TN 1669 and Weigand and Berman assemblies fail 5–27% past the measured drop; the
welded and fin-plate joints, from slow tests, may be too ductile under blast.

- **The office stands on fin plates, and collapses.** Under the kerbside 1 t its front
  ground-storey columns are lost; 334 of its 480 fin plates fracture by 1 s and 428 by 3 s, when
  beams freed at both ends have dropped about two storeys and the side façade peels away
  ([office](../office-tower.md#on-fin-plates)). *Illustrative:* compared with nothing measured.
- **The transport hub's portal joints are bolted extended end plates;** none of its 46 fractures.

**Footings on a slope.** Footings stay level, stepped into the slope, and take their burial,
earth pressures and bearing capacity from the terrain round them, with a new sample, "Two-storey
frame on a hillside" ([structural model](../structural-model.md#footings),
[validation](../validation.md#footings-on-a-slope)). Judged on closed forms only.

**Steel strips buckling under a sudden end load** are a benchmark (`blastbench kenny`): against
Kenny's drop-hammer tests the peak buckle's final deflection is within half to twice of measured
in 13 of 16 series with the default bows ([buckling](../shell-model.md#buckling)). The slenderest
steel strips read 2.5–3.7 times too far, and the buckle lies at mid-length where the tests' lay
near the clamp.

### New, as options

- **Structures' outlines as cut cells** (`structureCutCells`, off): a free wall off the lattice
  takes its impulse within 2.6% of its lattice values, but its peak force overshoots by 12%, so
  it stays off ([structural model](../structural-model.md)).
- **Freestanding objects beside a deformable structure** (stage 1, the experimental object
  drivers on the coarse grid): each keeps its own cells while the other moves; contact between
  them and the app's runs come later ([freestanding
  objects](../freestanding-objects.md#limitations-and-future-work)).

## Changed

**Every charge starts from a finely resolved burst,** not only a lone one: each is solved in one
dimension and mapped to its own radius, and a charge on a reflecting face is mirrored. Two 10 kg
bursts 30 m apart now read 98–115% of Kingery–Bulmash within the mapped radius, where the balloon
gave 52–69%; a lone charge in the open is unchanged to the bit.

**Structures on the terrain:** the clamp and the ground's connection hold a base within half an
element of the terrain under it, and loose nodes, shell nodes and rubble land on the terrain.
Scenes without terrain are unchanged.

**An envelope's load history starts at 500 Pa at the component,** not when the air around it
wakes a tile ahead of the shock, which had opened Beirut's windows 0.13–0.35 s early. Options saved
before keep the old rule.

**Freestanding objects' runs are about twice as fast in crowded scenes, and repeat to the bit.**
The coupling to refined air now remaps only the cells that change, side by side: the car park's
0.5 s run took 771 s against 1,417 s on the CI Mac mini ([freestanding
objects](../freestanding-objects.md#populated-scenes)). A box in the corner of an L of others
is no longer closed by its own remap (the furnished room's pieces move up to 5 mm differently).

### The record

- **Beirut against three more sources** ([Beirut](../beirut-2020.md)): arrivals at 0.94–1.06 of
  Stennett et al.'s six observers' times; the grain silos shade the peaks as Denny et al.'s tests
  measured close in; the JRC's simulation matches Kingery–Bulmash with a charge tuned to it.
- **The slab, wall and Keys comparisons start from the one-dimensional burst,** as the app does,
  at the open faces' clearance; Chiquito's P7 and Keys's debris moved most, and no standing moved.
  Keys's debris lies too far out in the open (the safe side) and half as far as measured in the
  shock tube (the unsafe side).
- **The error bands rechecked on today's defaults** ([the model's
  spread](../validation.md#the-models-spread-from-uncertain-inputs)): the close-in gauges spread
  7%, Wu's slab band is now 0.90–1.19 of the measured peak, and a wall's reflected impulse, 11%
  above Kingery–Bulmash on the mapped start, no longer falls inside the model's spread.

## Fixed

- **Parameter sweeps and ensembles no longer keep every run's memory** (0.5.0's known limitation):
  each run builds its solver in its own autorelease pool, so six solvers built in turn hold 0–7 MB
  where they held 2.8–3.2 GB, and reading every cell's state no longer piles up memory (a digest's
  peak fell from 445 MB to 327 MB). Results are unchanged.
- **The thermal record starts at detonation again on the fine start** (0.5.0's known limitation):
  the fireball before the grid's clock comes from the one-dimensional solution. Against Dial Pack
  the first radiation maximum is still a quarter of the measured, so thermal radiation stays
  illustrative ([thermal radiation](../thermal-radiation.md#before-the-grids-clock)).
- **On the fine start, events are timed from the detonation again,** not from the hand-over:
  fragment holes and close-in breaches no longer open a few milliseconds late, envelope load
  histories are no longer shifted, and the exposure plane's arrivals no longer step at the mapped
  radius (0.5.0's known limitation; commit `2337523`, which has no fragment of its own).
- **Broken panes open the refined air where they are:** on refined air with venting, the two
  highest pane cells now open and the corner cell at the origin no longer does.
- **A concrete or masonry shell keeps its own rule through the thickness beside timber,** where any
  timber in a model put every shell on timber's rule ([shell model](../shell-model.md)). A joint's
  friction exponent must now be below 1.
- **A structure on footings settles under its own weight before the blast,** where its footing
  joints chattered at rest and it started unloaded; the frame on a hillside settles in 0.18 s, and
  Alsubaei's walls on footings move 226 → 228 mm and 356 → 362 mm
  ([footings](../structural-model.md#footings)).
- **A structure standing on cut terrain no longer goes non-finite** as the blast arrives: cut
  cells less than half air under it are solid for the run.
- **Refined air beside a building no longer loses pressure when its patches are released.**
  Terrain-only scenes are unchanged.
- **A refined run that runs out of memory while its pools grow carries on as it was,** refining no
  more than its pools hold.
- **A Mac nearby that stops answering no longer holds a sweep or a pairing for ever:** connecting
  must finish within 20 seconds and each message of the first exchange within 30, and a session
  opened straight after pairing reaches the right listener ([distributed
  computing](../distributed-computing.md)).

## Known limitations

- **Reflected peaks at a wall fall short where cells are coarse against the charge:** at
  1 m/kg^(1/3) a peak read beside a wall is 38–40% of Kingery–Bulmash's with 19–20 cells across
  W^(1/3), and 62–69% with 37–50 ([air-blast
  model](../air-blast-model.md#reflected-peaks-at-a-wall)). The gauges' wall read
  (`gaugesReadWallPressure`, `--wall-read`, off) brings peaks to 69–96% but overshoots close-in
  impulse by 19–43% on 0.02 m cells, so it is for peaks alone.
- **The quick estimate and the city in minutes are for glass, not structure:** walls and roofs
  take the surrogate's standing, which says to run the scene; at 0.2 kt the hybrid's share of
  glass broken falls 0.13–0.25 short beyond 1.5 km.
- **Refined air takes the ground as a staircase of whole cells,** and a tower above the refinement
  ceiling takes its upper floors' load on the coarse cells.
- **Restarting a run with shells** still starts it with the springs' and fasteners' state from
  the run before (0.5.0's known limitation; no fix found on main). Close the run and reopen the
  project to start clean.
- **The thermal radiation stays illustrative,** its first maximum a quarter of Dial Pack's.
- **Collapse that has not been compared reads as the concrete band:** the large-panel street
  preset's damage stands as an approximation, though the one blind test of collapse missed it.
- **Steel connections' fracture turns come from slow tests** for the welded and fin-plate joints,
  which may be too ductile under blast.

tool, and nothing in it should be used to judge the safety of a real structure. MIT licence.
