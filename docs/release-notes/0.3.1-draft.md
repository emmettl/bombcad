- macOS 15 or later, Apple silicon (arm64), Metal GPU
- Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, with the ticket stapled: it opens without
  Gatekeeper warnings

What's new since 0.3.0:

- **Fixes.**
  - **New Document no longer crashes** (found after 0.3.0 crashed on New Document).
    [PENDING: in its final check in the release session.] The app reads its resource bundles
    from the build itself rather than a fixed list. `make app-smoke`, which launches the
    packaged app and builds a solver, is now part of release-smoke and of the release, so a
    missing bundle fails the build rather than the first new document.
  - **0.3.0's open-air figures were afterburning's alone.** On the shipped air (afterburning,
    hot air and far-field faces), on 0.25 m cells from 1 m/kg^(1/3), the figures against
    Kingery–Bulmash are:
    - incident peaks 74–84%;
    - incident impulse 94–100%;
    - reflected impulse 93–113%.

    0.3.0's notes gave 80–86%, 96–101% and 98–115%.
  - **Every scene harness and benchmark now starts from the shipped air.** `--air`, `--no-afterburn` and `--copy-faces` choose otherwise.
    - `validate` and `chamber` had kept the ideal gas and copied faces after the redesign.
    - The numerics studies, `digest`, `contact-profile` and the deflagration stay on the bare
      core by choice.
    - The blind tests' harnesses (Hrynyk, Mendonça) are unchanged.
    - On the shipped air, the anchorage wall at 10 m reads 27–28 mm against 22 mm measured.
  - **Terrain no longer leaks mass at cliffs.** Cut cells: a whole cell beside the terrain's
    solid cells (over a plateau lying on cells' faces, or at a cliff) read another column's wall.
    It now mirrors across its own face, as the staircase does. A closed box over such a plateau
    gained 0.24% of its mass in 200 steps; now it holds to round-off. Runs over smooth terrain
    are unchanged (72 terrain digests).
- **Faster.**
  - **Hot air** finds its temperature in one Newton step from a fitted first guess, where it took
    six searches of two steps each: the recommended air's cell update costs 1.37 times the ideal
    gas's instead of 1.50. Whole runs on it take 1.05–1.08 times less time (1.02–1.07 refined).
    Results move only in the last bits; no printed validation figure changed.
  - **Terrain's cut cells** skip their work above the cut band and wake their tiles only when
    needed. Bit-identical; the ridge's extra cost over a staircase falls from 25% to 15% a step,
    the slope's from 20% to 13%.
  - **Adaptive substeps through the advisor.** [PENDING: in the refinement session.] The advisor
    turns them on for refined runs where they gain: 1.19 times faster on open ground refined
    once, 1.31–1.33 twice, 1.07 in the street. Peaks rise by up to 2.5%, towards
    Kingery–Bulmash.
- **The app.**
  - **Soft ground under debris**: Run > Terrain > Debris lands on: Rigid ground (the default) or
    Soil, a Winkler bed of 10–150 MN/m³ (50 by default, the footings' medium dense sand).
    Projects save it, headless runs take `--ground rigid|soil|<MN/m3>`, and the standing names
    it with its one comparison. On Keys's walls in the open, mean debris reach goes from 2.10 to
    1.36 m against 0.23 m measured, so rigid stays the default.
  - **Error bands say which air they were found on.**
    - Since afterburning, hot air and far-field faces became the default, a band derived on the
      ideal gas says so or is withheld.
    - Five comparisons were rerun on today's air on 16 October:
      - close in, the finest air's peaks are within 7% and impulses 3–5% high;
      - Church's clouds keep their band;
      - Wu's 1.6 kg slab is at 0.91–1.19;
      - Hupfauf's debris band is back at 0.88–1.82;
      - the chamber's wall pressures are 1.0–1.6.
    - The burning tables on the shipped air,
      and the chamber on it: walls 0.9–1.6 times measured, roof 50 mm up at its peak and left
      27 mm up.
- **Blind test 3 is prepared and sealed.** Reynolds's high-strength slabs in the blast load
  simulator: six panels, one shot each, in the contest slab's own rig. The inputs are extracted
  and the measurements sealed. It is frozen at v0.3.0 (b524a9d), so it judges 0.3.0's defaults,
  not this build's.

Known gaps, as the standing says. Several err on the unsafe side, predicting too little damage:

- **Blind tests.** Two tests have been predicted and scored, both on defaults frozen before
  0.3.0's changes to the strain rate, the air and the sleeping air.
  - Hrynyk's slabs struck repeatedly by a weight: *between*. The first impacts were 15–29% too
    flexible as calibrated, later ones too stiff, and none of the three punching failures was
    predicted.
  - Mendonça's slabs under an open-air charge: *worse*. Both collapses were missed, and the
    blast's bending was four to ten times the model's.
  - A third, Reynolds's, is sealed and awaits its predictions.
- **Slabs under blasts are too stiff and too strong** short of the contest's load: Mendonça's,
  Wu's under small charges at 40–70%, and the contest slab's lower shot at 79–88%. Since 0.3.0:
  - **Not the mesh:** Mendonça's first peaks do not move with it. On 8, 16 and 24 elements
    through they stay under 10% apart and at a fifth of the measured.
  - **Static resistance:** statically, the model's slab holds 2.4 times its yield line uncracked
    and 1.9 times it fully cracked. So part of the excess is in the concrete or the element
    before any rate or inertia.
  - **Ruled out** remain the load, the supports, the bars and their bond, cracked concrete,
    hourglass control, arching and an embedded crack. The cause is not found.
- **Punching under impact** is not predicted: the cone forms but cannot slide.
- **Structural results do not converge** as the mesh is refined; the bands hold only on the
  meshes they were derived on.
- **After failure**, results are one draw among roundings of the arithmetic: a beam's moment after
  failure moves by up to a quarter, debris speeds by about ±10%.
- Close-in charges do too little damage: slabs a third to a half as far down as measured, too
  little spall, never holed by default.
- Shear is too strong (4–11% on fine meshes, 32% on 12 elements), and short beams struck to a
  shear failure fail by the wrong mechanism.
- **The internal-explosion chamber's roof** is too stiff. On the shipped air it rises 50 mm and is left 27 mm up, against 95 mm left measured: under a
  third.
- Debris is held back rather than thrown; collapse has been compared once, blind, and both
  collapses were missed.
- The fireball's radiation, on today's air, radiates about a fifth of the charge's energy, against
  4–7% measured for TNT; vented gas peaks are too low; both are illustrative.
- Peak pressures read low unless the air is refined.

`docs/validation.md`, `docs/blind-tests.md` and `docs/validation-review.md` give the details.

Download `BombCAD-0.3.1-macos-arm64.zip`, expand it and move `BombCAD.app` to Applications. To
check the download:

```
shasum -a 256 -c BombCAD-0.3.1-macos-arm64.zip.sha256
```

It is a study of the numerics, the physics and the performance of blast simulation, not a design
tool, and nothing in it should be used to judge the safety of a real structure. MIT licence.
