Fifth build of BombCAD, an interactive blast simulator for Macs.

- macOS 15 or later, Apple silicon (arm64), Metal GPU
- Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, with the ticket stapled: it opens without Gatekeeper warnings
- Version 0.3.0, build 7, from commit b524a9d

What's new since 0.2.0:

- **Defaults changed.** Results from this build differ from 0.2.0's for the same project:
  - **The air**: afterburning, hot air and far-field open faces for every run (the app's "Afterburning and hot air" turns the first two off). On 0.25 m cells from 1 m/kg^(1/3), incident impulse in the open moves from 78–86% of Kingery–Bulmash to 96–101% (afterburning's burning time was fitted to it), and impulse on walls from 91–102% to 98–115%; closer in, at 0.75 m/kg^(1/3), incident impulse reads a fifth to a third high. The structures compared move by under a fifth either way, towards the tests for some and away for others (the heaviest close-in charges' slabs now 110–196 mm down against 340 and 510 mm measured). Runs take about 1.1–2 times as long (the least when refined) and a quarter more memory.
  - **Concrete under fast loading**: the strain rate averaged over 100 µs, so results no longer change with the time step (they still change with the mesh). Beams broken by an impact follow the window's length steeply instead: Ando's moved by up to two thirds when it became the default.
  - **Footings on sand** give back the sand's crushing under the heel when they rock, a law fitted on one test.
  - **Gas deflagrations** wrinkle the flame by its cellular instability from a measured onset radius, and the ignition point now always lights exactly one cell.
  - **Sleeping air** (the air frozen once the blast has passed) keeps following the structure's outline as it moves. Before, a slab landing while the air slept was driven down by the frozen outline; that made the peaks in blind test 2 that seemed to agree. The sleeping air still feels no new flow and loose debris feels no air while it sleeps: keep the air awake for anything thrown.
  - **Refinement** places one coarse cell of margin round the shock instead of two: a fifth fewer refined blocks, peaks moved under 1.3%.
  - **Terrain** slopes are cut cells on every run, uniform or refined, rather than steps of whole cells.
  - **Contact** comes from ContinuumKit 0.1.0-alpha.25.
- **The app.**
  - **IFC import**: reinforcement read from the file; meshes repaired and touching members tied; slabs and walls on the ground or footings rest on it; a gravity check says what stands, what deflects and what falls (12 of the 19 buildings of a test corpus now stand).
  - **Error bands**: each structural band holds only on the mesh it was derived on and is withheld on others; the contest slab's band is withheld within 1.5 m/kg^(1/3) of a charge, where the blind open-air slabs bent far more; the debris band widens by its spread across roundings of the arithmetic (±10%).
  - **The advisor**'s costs are re-timed on a quiet Mac (within 0.6–1.4 times), and close in it weighs a uniform grid where refinement would fill the domain.
  - Layout fixes across the sidebar's sections and editors.
  - From the command line only: `BombCAD ensemble` runs each case under several roundings of the arithmetic; soft ground for debris is an option of the debris benches.

Known gaps, as the standing says. Several err on the unsafe side, predicting too little damage:

- **Blind tests.** Two tests were predicted on frozen defaults before their measurements were unsealed; both were frozen before this build's changes to the strain rate, the air and the sleeping air, and judge those earlier defaults. Hrynyk's slabs struck repeatedly by a weight: *between*, the first impacts 15–29% too flexible as calibrated, later ones too stiff, and none of the three punching failures predicted. Mendonça's slabs under an open-air charge: *worse*, both collapses missed and the blast's bending four to ten times the model's.
- **Slabs under blasts are too stiff and too strong short of the contest's load**: Mendonça's (above), Wu's under small charges at 40–70%, and the contest slab's lower shot at 79–88%. The load, the supports, the bars and their bond, cracked concrete's strength and stiffness, hourglass control and arching were each ruled out, and an embedded crack was tried without effect; the cause is not found.
- **Punching under impact** is not predicted: the cone forms but cannot slide.
- **Structural results do not converge** as the mesh is refined; the bands hold only on the meshes they were derived on.
- **After failure**, results are one draw among roundings of the arithmetic: a beam's moment after failure moves by up to a quarter, debris speeds by about ±10%.
- Close-in charges do too little damage: slabs a third to a half as far down as measured, too little spall, never holed by default.
- Shear is too strong (4–11% on fine meshes, 32% on 12 elements), and short beams struck to a shear failure fail by the wrong mechanism.
- The internal-explosion chamber's roof is too stiff: left a quarter as far up as measured.
- Debris is held back rather than thrown; collapse has been compared once, blind, and both collapses were missed.
- The fireball's radiation, on the new air, radiates about a fifth of the charge's energy, against 4–7% measured for TNT; vented gas peaks are too low; both are illustrative.
- Peak pressures read low unless the air is refined.

`docs/validation.md`, `docs/blind-tests.md` and `docs/validation-review.md` give the details.

Download `BombCAD-0.3.0-macos-arm64.zip`, expand it and move `BombCAD.app` to Applications. To check the download:

```
shasum -a 256 -c BombCAD-0.3.0-macos-arm64.zip.sha256
```

It is a study of the numerics, the physics and the performance of blast simulation, not a design tool, and nothing in it should be used to judge the safety of a real structure. MIT licence.
