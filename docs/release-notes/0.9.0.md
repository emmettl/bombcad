Seventeenth build of BombCAD, an interactive blast simulator for Macs.

- macOS 15 or later, Apple silicon (arm64), Metal GPU
- Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, with the ticket stapled: it opens without
  Gatekeeper warnings
- Version 0.9.0, build 22, from commit COMMIT

**Install with Homebrew:** `brew install emmettl/tap/bombcad` installs the app and links its
headless runner as `bombcad`.

**The main changes**, each in full below:
- **Best for a Budget:** give a budget and the app finds the barrier walls, the film or laminated
  glass building by building, and the stand-off that leave the fewest people hurt, and the full
  estimate judges its best plans. The dollar costs are illustrative, editable in the sheet.
- **Protective design, live:** put up a barrier wall and drag it, give the windows film or
  laminated glass, and keep the charge back with a slider, the glass, walls and people re-colouring
  as each moves. Illustrative.
- **The charge from the damage, in the app:** mark buildings by hand or load a survey, and watch
  the charge's place and yield firm up. A district from File ▸ New District now knows where it is
  on the map, so a survey in longitudes and latitudes lands on it by itself. Illustrative.
- **The explorer keeps up on Beirut and reads worst as worst,** with a warm colour scale and a
  legend under each layer.
- **A live preview nearer the full estimate, and more honest:** each façade is read in three bands
  by distance, and the drag now says how many buildings it left to the CPU's preview, and why.
- **A barrier wall now shelters the façades close behind it,** in the quick estimate by default.
- The earlier barrier terms, the one-band façade reading and the marks' independent weighing stay
  selectable by name; the live drag's earlier networks are not kept.
- **Worker protocol unchanged (19):** Macs on 0.7.2 to 0.9.0 can share work.

## New

**Best for a Budget…,** in the explorer's Protect the buildings and in the workbench's What if: a
sheet asks for a budget and finds the barrier walls, the film or laminated glass building by building,
and the stand-off that leave the fewest people hurt (or the least glass thrown). It searches through
the learned response, in 0.3–2 s on the made-up square and street and up to 4 s on Beirut on a Mac Studio,
drawing its best walls in the view as it goes. The full estimate then judges its best few plans, as on
a let-go, and the best by it is put in the scene as a what-if. On the square at $1M, that is a 120 m
stand-off and two walls, a quarter fewer people hurt. In all 25 cases judged, the pick was the
best by the full estimate of the hand-placed plans of the same budget set beside it. The costs are illustrative: an order of magnitude from open price guides, not a quote, and
editable in the sheet. The plan's standing is the estimate's: illustrative ([protective design](../protective-design.md#the-best-protection-for-a-budget-3-november-2026)).

**Protect the buildings, live:** the explorer's new section, and the workbench's What if, can put up a
barrier wall and drag it, give every window film or laminated glass, and keep the charge back with a
stand-off slider. The glass, the walls and the people re-colour as each moves, in 2–7 ms a frame on
the made-up square, 3–15 ms on the street and about 25 ms on Beirut within 1.5 km (timed on one façade band) on a Mac Studio, and the full
estimate follows on let-go. The people can now be counted as hurt by the glass thrown into their
rooms (Bowen et al.'s glass missiles, 1956), which shows film helping: 86% fewer hurt on the square under that option.
It is an option. Kummer's estimate by the panes broken stays the default, as it fits Beirut's record
better. A barrier wall's own shelter, fitted on fine runs of walls in front of a building, takes
the load off façades close behind it. Both are illustrative ([people](../people-in-the-scene.md#hurt-by-the-glass-thrown-2-november-2026),
[the barrier](../protective-design.md#a-barrier-you-can-push-around-2-november-2026),
[the explorer](../explorer.md#protect-the-buildings-2-november-2026)).

**Mark the damage on buildings and watch the charge firm up.** Simulation ▸ Read the Charge from the Damage… opens a panel beside the view: click a building to mark its glass broken or whole and its walls undamaged, damaged or heavily damaged, and the chance of where the charge was is painted on the ground with its likeliest half and nine tenths outlined, its yield drawn beside, and both said in words that VoiceOver reads. Each mark shows within a frame (under 1 ms to weigh, a 7 ms frame). Mark from the Damage Shown marks every building a run or the quick estimate judged, so the solver can referee: on a made-up district the network never saw, 30 marks put the charge about 90 m off and its yield within a factor 2 (median), every building 30 m and a factor 1.4, though then far too sure. Move the Charge There puts the scene's charge at the reading. Standing: illustrative, as the quick estimate's chances it learned from ([charge inference](../charge-inference.md#marking-the-damage-in-the-app-2-november-2026)).

## Changed

**Read the Charge from the Damage now takes a survey, keeps its marks with the project, reaches the far field, and is less over-sure with many marks.** **Load a Survey…** reads a marks CSV, GEER's façade shapefile, its structural tables, or a folder of them. It puts them on the scene's buildings and says how many matched and how many did not. On Beirut, GEER's survey marked 1,354 buildings, and its 50 stone buildings read 0.70 kt (90%: 0.21–2.16 kt) with Warehouse 12 inside the place's regions. The marks are saved with the project (older projects open as before), and New District's far buildings can be marked too. The marks are now weighed with an error they all share, a factor 1.65 in yield and 12 m in place, because neighbours judged by the same estimate err together. On a made-up district held out, at 30 marks the yield's 90% interval held the true yield 98–99% of the time, against 84–85% before, and with every building marked 100% against 19%, the median errors no worse. Past 100 marks the panel warns that the answer is likely still too sure: with every building marked the place's 90% region held the truth 70% of the time there, and on Beirut only 28%. Stage 2's weighing stays selectable as "independent". Standing: illustrative ([charge inference](../charge-inference.md#marks-that-err-together-surveys-and-kept-marks-2-november-2026)).

**A district from File ▸ New District now remembers where it is on the map.** Load a Survey… in Read the Charge from the Damage puts a survey's longitudes and latitudes on such a district's buildings by itself, near and far, with no corner to type. Projects saved before open as they did: the panel says why a survey in longitudes and latitudes can't be placed there, and still takes a typed corner ([charge inference](../charge-inference.md#marks-that-err-together-surveys-and-kept-marks-2-november-2026)).

**The explorer keeps up on Beirut and reads worst as worst:** dragging the charge over Beirut's port
now shows the blast wave and the estimate within 20–22 ms a step on a Mac Studio (46–48 ms before),
the wave worked out on coarser cells while dragging and in full on let-go. The explorer colours the
glass, walls, people and fire on a warm scale from pale to a loud dark red, with a plain legend under
each layer, so the worst-hit buildings no longer look untouched; the workbench's colours are
unchanged ([the explorer's polish](../explorer.md#polish-beirut-at-30-frames-a-second-a-warm-scale-the-new-weights-2-november-2026)).
The charge's marker in the explorer is now a bright aqua ball in a dark ring, left clear of the
wave, so it stands out among the red buildings.

**The drag preview now reads each façade in three bands by distance, so a charge kept back from
long façades previews much closer to the full estimate:** on the explorer's square, people hurt
were 11–73% above the full estimate with one strip for each façade, and are now within 3.3% of it,
at 2–7 ms a frame, as the user decided on 8 November 2026. The glass networks were retrained on
sides read this way, and the budget planner's ranking on the square agrees with the full estimate
on 98–100% of the pairs it separates. The networks follow their own CPU preview less tightly than
before: within 1.4% on the square and 0.8% on Beirut, a limit of about 1.5% set from the spread
between two trainings, and Beirut reads 1–2% under the full estimate. The preview as it was stays
available as "Each façade read by its nearest strip (before 8 November 2026)" in Quick Estimate; the
networks were not retrained for it
([learned models](../learned-models.md#adopted-8-november-2026)).

**The drag no longer colours buildings from inputs its networks have never seen:** where a building
lies outside what the learned response was trained on, its preview while dragging is the SDOF's and
draws, as for a construction the response cannot read, and the drag says how many and why, as the
user decided on 3 November 2026. The training's ranges now ship with the weights, so the drag and
the check of the built-in scenes read the same ones. The presets on medium cells (the office tower,
the transport hub and the process plant) are now previewed by the SDOF throughout; on the explorer's
square and street a few buildings nearest a large charge are (under 1%), at no cost to the frame
rate. The explorer's Beirut stays on the learned response for now, with a note: on the SDOF its walls'
drag would fall to a few frames a second
([learned models](../learned-models.md#what-lies-outside-the-training-on-the-cpu-3-november-2026)).

**Far fewer buildings fall back to the SDOF while the glass is dragged on Beirut:** the network for
the glass thrown into rooms has been retrained on four times the data, as the user decided on
3 November 2026. The new data takes in Beirut's whole district on 4 m and 6 m cells, more squares, and
the glass network's own kinds of district. On the glass layer, Beirut's whole district on 4 m cells
now leaves about 80 buildings a frame to the CPU's preview, where it left about 220. The explorer's
Beirut counts about 22 outside, where it counted about 120. On every held-out scene the network now
agrees more closely with the CPU's own answers. On Beirut's port on 4 m cells, 98.0% of sides come
within 0.02 of the CPU's share hurt, against 95.5% before. Against the full estimate the glass thrown
and the people hurt stay within 0.1 everywhere it was checked
([learned models](../learned-models.md#the-glass-thrown-trained-wider-3-november-2026)). These counts and
shares are that network's on one façade band. On three bands, as now, the explorer's Beirut leaves 7–9 of
about 2,870 buildings to the CPU.

**The live colours on districts of small cells:** the learned response behind the live drag is now
trained on made-up districts of every kind on 2 m cells (perimeter blocks, grids, towers,
industrial estates and squares, with charges of 50 kg to 100 t), not squares alone. On a held-out
district of a style it never saw at 2 m, its walls' chances are within 0.1 of the CPU's own for
98% of buildings, against 91% before; the explorer's square, street and Beirut move by 0.3 points
or less, and a frame costs what it did
([the built-in scenes checked](../learned-models.md#the-built-in-scenes-checked-and-round-6-with-the-pools-2-m-pieces-3-november-2026)).

**A barrier wall now shelters the façades close behind it:** the quick estimate takes a wall's own
shelter by default, as the user decided on 3 November 2026. Held out on fine runs of walls in front
of a building, the peak comes within ×1.5 of the runs' on 98% of the façade strips behind a wall
(85% before) and the impulse within ×1.25 on 98% (68%). Close behind, the load is no longer read
about ×1.8 too high. It credits about a sixth too much shelter on the median strip, and rests on one
family of made-up layouts. Only scenes with a barrier change; "Barrier walls as buildings crossed" in
Quick Estimate gives the terms before
([protective design](../protective-design.md#adopted-3-november-2026)).

## Fixed

**The live drag on a scene without a charge:** on the process plant's preset, a gas cloud with no
charge of TNT, the learned response was handed a scaled distance of infinity and no load, inputs it
was never trained on. Such a scene is now left to the CPU's preview, which gives it no load, as the
full estimate does, and the app says why
([the process plant's scaled distance](../learned-models.md#the-process-plants-infinite-scaled-distance-3-november-2026)).

## Known limitations

**Best for a Budget's dollars are illustrative.** The cost table is an order of magnitude from open
price guides, partly sellers' own, and not a quote; change it in the sheet before reading much into
a plan. A plan takes one charge. It puts up at most two walls, none nearer the charge than
Z = 2 m/kg^(1/3), since the model's walls are rigid where a real wall that close would be thrown
down. Glass is one kind per building, not side by side, and wall retrofits are not costed. The
workbench offers walls and glass only: a stand-off needs one of the explorer's two lines
([what it lacks](../protective-design.md#what-it-lacks)).

**People in the scene stays illustrative and relative.** Read it for where people would be hurt and
for a change against a baseline, not for counts. The default model, Kummer's by the panes broken,
barely moves for film or laminated glass, since they hold the glass without stopping it breaking;
the glass thrown (Bowen's) is the option under which film counts
([people](../people-in-the-scene.md#hurt-by-the-glass-thrown-2-november-2026)).

**The barrier's shelter rests on one family of made-up layouts,** credits about a sixth too much
on the median strip, and holds for Z of 2 to 8
([adopted](../protective-design.md#adopted-3-november-2026)).

**The charge from the damage stays too sure with hundreds of marks.** It is calibrated up to about
30 marks; past 100 the panel warns of it. With every building marked, the place's 90% region held
the truth 70% of the time on the made-up district, and 28% on Beirut. It knows no shielding, reaches
charges within 500 m of the scene's reference from 100 kg to 3.3 kt, and inherits the quick
estimate's biases, the glass's above all
([what it does not do](../charge-inference.md#the-charge-from-marked-damage-learned-1-november-2026)).

**The live preview is a preview.** The three-band networks follow their own CPU preview within 1.4%
on the square and 0.8% on Beirut, inside a limit of about 1.5% set from the spread between two
trainings, not the 0.5% first asked. The square and the street are now in the glass networks'
training, so that check is not independent of the training on those two scenes; Beirut's explorer
scene and a made-up district remain held out. The one-band reading, kept by name, reads sides
differently from those the networks now learned. The full estimate still follows every let-go
([adopted](../learned-models.md#adopted-8-november-2026)).

**The explorer's Beirut stays on the learned response even where its walls lie outside the
training,** with a note: on the SDOF its walls' drag would fall to a few frames a second
([what lies outside](../learned-models.md#what-lies-outside-the-training-on-the-cpu-3-november-2026)).
