Thirteenth build of BombCAD, an interactive blast simulator for Macs.

- macOS 15 or later, Apple silicon (arm64), Metal GPU
- Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, with the ticket stapled: it opens without
  Gatekeeper warnings
- Version 0.7.3, build 18, from commit COMMIT

**Install with Homebrew:** `brew install emmettl/tap/bombcad` installs the app and links its
headless runner as `bombcad`.

**The main changes**, each in full below:
- **Far façades read higher, the safe way round.** Beirut's earlier reference runs had a 320 m
  domain top that let the far loads drain away over the hills, so the far field and the glass
  chances were fitted to loads that were too low. Quick Estimate, the glass chances and the far
  field are now refitted on runs whose top clears the hills, and New District raises its top to
  match (608 m on Beirut).
- **The protection planner's plans now meet their target on a run, not only on average:** a margin
  for the scatter between sides, judged on runs at 0.5–50 t.
- **Concrete walls' chances pass their calibration test again**, narrowly, with their impulse
  ×0.95.
- Every earlier setting stays selectable by name, to reproduce earlier estimates and plans.
- **Worker protocol unchanged:** Macs on 0.7.2 and 0.7.3 can share work.

## Changed

**Quick Estimate, the glass chances and the far field read the far façades higher, and New
District's top is raised.** Their façade terms are now refitted on runs whose top clears the far
façades, and so is the scatter the chances draw. Far fields that New District makes carry the
run's match flat beyond the hand-over, with no trend with distance. New District's domain top is
at least the highest ground plus a fifth of the radius; on Beirut's 2.5 km that is 608 m, against
400–488 m before.

Against Beirut's clear-top runs:
- Quick Estimate's far loads at 0.5 kt are 0.82–1.03 of the run's, where they were 0.72–0.92.
- The glass chances' blown out passes the protective-design test (Brier 0.027 against 0.038).
- The far field's error in the share of sides blown out halves at every yield.

Some glass broken still reads low at 0.2 kt beyond 1.75 km. Concrete walls' chances keep their
peak ×1.15 and take their impulse ×0.95, and pass their tenths test narrowly
(see "Quick Estimate's concrete walls' chances now pass their tenths test" below).

The earlier terms stay by name, to reproduce earlier estimates: Envelope Damage ▸ "Façade terms of
23 October", headless `--surrogate-terms fitted`, and `--terms fitted` in the benches. Projects
saved before keep their far field's trend, and Far field ▸ "Carry the run's match flat" changes it.
Details are in [the surrogate](../surrogate-first-look.md#adopted-25-october-2026) and
[the hybrid far field](../hybrid-far-field.md#adopted-the-clear-top-terms-the-match-carried-flat-and-a-higher-top-25-october-2026).

**The protection planner leaves a margin for the scatter between sides, so its plans meet their
target on a run, not only on average.** The planner and Simulation ▸ Protective Design now take
the clear-top façade terms with a flag margin at 2.5 standard deviations by default. A side whose
plan passes on its chance, but not with its load 2.5 standard deviations of the terms' scatter up,
is given the next dearer glazing with a smaller chance. The planner was judged on the sparse
made-up district's runs at 0.5–50 t, with two seeds. Its plans now meet the target in all 16
cases, the worst side at 9.4%, for 1.01–1.34 times the earlier cost. The earlier plans met it in
7 of 16, the worst side at 43%. The planner still buys the stand-off before film, and no wall. The
margin was set holding masses out, on one made-up district and one target, so its standing is
illustrative. The earlier planner stays by name: `scenebench protect --terms fitted --margin none`,
or Protective Design ▸ "Planner of 23 October". Details are in
[protective design](../protective-design.md#adopted-the-clear-top-terms-with-the-flag-margin-25-october-2026).
`--option-table` writes each side's chances under each option, for tracing.

**Quick Estimate's concrete walls' chances now pass their tenths test.** Their peak keeps ×1.15,
and their impulse takes ×0.95 where it took ×1.15, as with the learned field models. Against
Beirut's near runs at ten yields, on air refined to 2 m, Brier skill goes from 0.599 to 0.601 for
walls damaged moderately or worse. Every tenth of chance is now within 0.1 of the share of runs,
but only just: where the chances say 0.84, 0.94 of the runs' buildings were damaged, the unsafe
side. Before, that tenth was 0.11 under. Heavy damage, which is illustrative, goes from skill 0.51
to 0.54. Held out yield by yield, the refit picks this pair in nine folds of ten. Roofs, stone and
the field models' walls don't change. Details are in
[the surrogate](../surrogate-first-look.md#the-walls-impulse-095-25-october-2026).

## Known limitations

**The far field and the glass chances still read some glass low beyond about 1.5 km at the
smallest yields, the unsafe side:** judged again on Beirut against runs whose top clears the hills
(8 m air refined to 4 m, a 640 m top), where the 4 m runs they were first judged on had a 320 m top
that drained the far loads. On the terms adopted with this release (see "Quick Estimate, the glass
chances and the far field read the far façades higher") the hybrid far field's façade peaks are
0.82–1.05 of the run's to 2.5 km, and its glass with some broken is within 0.05 of the run's to
1.5 km at 0.2 kt, to 2 km at 0.5 kt and to 2.5 km at 1.1 kt, 0.13–0.50 under beyond at 0.2 kt. The
glass chances' thrown past 3b and some broken still fail the protective-design test. The standings
say so ([the hybrid far field](../hybrid-far-field.md#judged-again-against-a-clear-top-reference-25-october-2026)).
The full-length peak refit of 23 October, which passed the rule it was judged by, was overtaken by
the refit on clear-top runs. Developers: `surrogate-judge`, `hybrid-judge` and `glass-calibrate`
take `--surrogate-dx` for refined references, and `hybrid-judge` takes `--coefficients`.

**A building's collapse is still carried by the elements' stabilisation, and the element that
would hand it to the concrete is too soft once cracked:** on the large-panel block most of the
hourglass control's 60–76% share of the elements' work is elements warping in modes the one-point
element cannot see, not the floors' hinges, and moving its cap changes nothing. The element that
bends in its cracked concrete (`assumedStrainCracking`, an option) makes the work the concrete's
and brings the twelve-storey bay down as far (7% further by 3.5 s), a second sooner, at five
times the cost, but sends struck beams 20% further than their tests. Collapse stays illustrative
([whole-event simulation](../whole-event.md#7-hinges-two-elements-deep-25-october-2026)).
Developers: `blastbench wholeevent --breakdown <checkpoint>` sorts a saved run's work by part and
state, and `blastbench hinge-sections` bends the block's sections on the CPU reference.
