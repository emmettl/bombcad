Tenth build of BombCAD, an interactive blast simulator for Macs.

- macOS 15 or later, Apple silicon (arm64), Metal GPU
- Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, with the ticket stapled: it opens without
  Gatekeeper warnings
- Version 0.7.0, build 15, from commit 133685e0

**Install with Homebrew:** `brew install emmettl/tap/bombcad` installs the app and links its
headless runner as `bombcad`.

**The main changes**, each in full below:
- **Drag the charge and the quick estimate follows it**, in about half a second a frame on
  Beirut's 14,000 buildings, with the hazard rings and stand-off lines moving with it.
- **Chances for walls and roofs** beside the glass's in the quick estimate, and **close calls
  refined by the solver** within a time you choose. **Concrete walls' chances are higher** than in
  earlier builds: runs refined to 2 m showed the 4 m runs they were fitted on read the walls' peaks
  13–19% low, so concrete walls' loads now take ×1.3.
- **Run the blast on a Mac nearby and watch it here**, the run kept afterwards like one made here.
- **Replay keeps every layer as it came:** vent panels, envelope damage, the thermal paint and the
  cloud.
- **People in the scene**, illustrative and relative: where people would be hurt, and how a change
  moves it against a pinned baseline.
- **A learned field model for ground-floor peaks**, an option, off by default and illustrative,
  its weights downloaded only when you ask.
- **Raised charges dig smaller craters**, their height now measured to the charge's centre.
- **Macs that share work all need 0.7.0** (worker protocol 16).

## New

**Drag the charge and the quick estimate follows it.** With a quick estimate on screen and Place
Charge on, drag the charge across the ground. While you drag, a preview colours the buildings by
what the view shows: the damage, the glass's chances or the walls' and roofs'. It judges each
building side by its component nearest the charge, on a coarser table of panes than a full
estimate's, and the sidebar marks it as a preview. When you let go, the full estimate is made where
the charge stopped. On Beirut's 14,000 buildings a preview frame takes about half a second for every
colouring (the glass's chances 0.43 s, the damage 0.69 s, the walls' and roofs' chances 0.53 s),
and under a second with the Mac heavily loaded. Against the full estimate, its glass chances were
within 0.1 on 88–93% of sides and its walls' and roofs' on 98.9–100% of buildings, and the damage's
colour was the same for 99.7%. The quick estimate itself now lays the district out once, and works
out the glass's sight lines about twice as fast
([dragging the charge](../surrogate-first-look.md#dragging-the-charge-20-october-2026)).

**The hazard rings follow the dragged charge.** The K24, K40 and minimum-distance rings move as the
charge does. With Safety distances and injury on, the viewport also shows the scene's stand-off
lines, amber when the charge crosses onto their guarded side, and the Hazards section gives the
distance to each line. Before a run, amber and dark-red injury rings show the bare-TNT surface-burst
reference for a person in the open: they leave out shielding, reflections, terrain and the charge's
height, and are not drawn for several charges. After a run, the injury contours still come from the
simulated field.

**Chances for walls and roofs in the quick estimate.** Beside the glass's chances, each building
now gets three, drawn over the yield, the loads' scatter and an assumed strength scatter, and the
view can colour by them:
- that its walls are damaged (moderate or worse);
- that its walls are damaged heavily or worse, illustrative for concrete;
- that its roof is damaged (moderate or worse).

Against Beirut's runs at ten yields on air refined to 2 m, each held out:
- **Concrete walls**, moderate or worse (skill 0.59, 0.66 with the learned field model), are
  calibrated: each tenth of chance is within 0.1 of the runs.
- **Roofs** (skill 0.74) read 0.10–0.18 low in the middle.
- **Stone walls** read high, by up to 0.18.
- **Failure** gets no chance.

Headless, `--surrogate` writes them as `walls`
([glass by chance](../glass-probability.md#walls-and-roofs)).

**Close calls refined by the solver.** Once the quick estimate is in, the app can run the air on the
nearest buildings whose chances lie between 0.2 and 0.8 for the colouring shown. It runs them on a
scene cut round them, with finer air along the way to each, within a wall-clock budget you choose
and can cancel. Each building's run result replaces the estimate's as the blast passes it, outlined
in green in the view and marked *refined* in the list. On Beirut at 0.5 kt the first answer came in
13 s and eight in 22 s.

**Run the blast on a Mac nearby and watch it here.** With a Mac paired (Settings ▸ Macs nearby),
**Run on** in the Run tab sends the blast to it: its GPU runs the air and the structures and sends
the scene as this Mac draws it, the field every few cells and every structure's mesh, and the
viewport shows each frame as it arrives. The run comes back kept, with the frames as its recording,
to scrub, replay and compare like a run made here. If the link drops, the run carries on there:
**Keep Watching** picks it up again, or **Fetch Result Later** collects it when it suits. Measured
with the Studio running and the mini watching over Wi-Fi, the city block's frames (every 4th cell,
about 1.2 MB) nearly all arrived, one every 0.2 s, about 0.2 s behind the run, for 4–6 ms of the
Studio's time each. What it leaves out is under Known limitations
([running on a Mac nearby](../run-comparison.md#running-the-blast-on-a-mac-nearby),
[measured](../distributed-computing.md#the-thin-client-in-the-app-21-october-2026)).

**People in the scene, illustrative and relative.** Once a quick estimate has the glass's chances,
the sidebar's People in the scene section places people in each building by its use and the time of
day, and shows where they would be hurt as shares, by building and by distance. Pin a baseline, then
move the charge, change the time of day or protect a building, and it gives the change, such as
"−38% hurt against the pinned baseline", draw by draw over the same assumptions. Colour Buildings by
People Hurt colours each building by its share hurt, and follows a drag. It rests on published
models from other events and hazards: Hazus's people by time of day and its earthquake casualty
rates, Kummer's glass injury, and Eisenberg's probits. The counts and their band are behind Show
counts, under the warning that on Beirut 2020 they read 4 to 12 times the injuries and deaths
recorded; where it puts the hurt agrees better ([people in the scene](../people-in-the-scene.md)).

**The quick estimate can take its ground-floor peaks from a learned field model** (an option, off by
default). Under the envelope damage, **Learned field model for ground-floor peaks** has a network
trained on BombCAD's own 4 m district runs give the peak at each ground-floor façade strip, for the
strip's damage, its glass and the walls' chances; the impulse, the roofs and the upper storeys stay
on the surrogate. It runs on the GPU and adds about 0.2 s to a drag frame on Beirut's 14,000
buildings. On Beirut's 50 runs, a city it never saw, the estimate's glass by side is exactly right
on 0.942 of sides against the surrogate's 0.916. Against the runs refined to 2 m, with its walls'
peak taken ×1.15 and impulse ×0.95, concrete walls moderate or worse score Brier skill 0.66
against the surrogate's 0.59. Its standing is illustrative. Its weights (15.7 MB, from the
repository's `field-model-1` release) download the first time the option is turned on, and only
when you click Download: the app says where the file comes from and what it is for, checks it
against the SHA-256 it expects, and keeps it in Application Support. A failed or cancelled download
keeps nothing and leaves the option off, saying why. A weights file named by `BOMBCAD_FIELD_MODEL`
or the setting that is corrupt, cut short or does not fit the network its header describes is
refused with a message, and the estimate stays the surrogate's. The release's notes give the
model's training data, its licences and its standing
([in the app's quick estimate](../learned-models.md#in-the-apps-quick-estimate-21-october-2026),
[how the weights ship](../learned-models.md#how-the-weights-ship-21-october-2026)).

**Ground points know when the ground's own wave outruns the blast.** Each point now takes the time
the ground's wave from the charge first reaches it through the soil's layers (the direct wave, or
the head wave along a deeper, faster layer such as saturated soil under a water table), and is
labelled outrun wherever that comes before the air, however fast the air's front still is. The
ground shock chart names the followed point's regime, in orange with the lead in milliseconds where
it is outrun; `--ground-results` gives `groundArrival`. Under Prairie Flat's 500 t the label finds
the outrunning limit between 400 and 560 ft, where Murrell found it at about 560 ft; the front's
speed alone never did ([Prairie Flat](../ground-shock.md#against-prairie-flat)). *Illustrative:*
against Prairie Flat the column and the estimate are within a factor of two at under half the
gauges inside that limit (too low near the surface), and the half-space carries the motion ahead of
the blast past it within a factor of two; the standing now bands that motion, with the half-space
chosen, at 0.33 to 3 times the measured. On the soil measured at Watching Hill
(`Fixtures/PrairieFlat/soil-profile.json`, which `blastbench groundmotion --profile` reads) the
column's peaks come within a factor of two at 26 of 34 gauges, but its stress then arrives late:
one loading stiffness cannot give both.

**"Arching between returns" for envelope masonry walls,** an option in the construction editor,
off by default. Unreinforced masonry walls held at both upright edges resist by arching across
their width once cracked, the abutments taken as not giving. With it, the terraced row's solid
one-brick fronts stand, cracked, from 16.9 m under 96 kg, as the detailed shells' do, where
without it the envelope graded them fallen to 26 m. The damage references' brick walls come
within 0.79–1.30 of the detailed rotations (0.82–2.54 without), and freestanding walls are
unchanged. Its standing is illustrative
([building envelopes](../building-envelopes.md#arching-between-returns)).

**Tools and the record.**

- **`blastmodel`, the CPU studies on Linux.** A command-line tool that builds without Metal, on a
  Mac or on Linux, and runs the studies `scenebench` and `blastbench` run on the CPU: the façade
  surrogate's judge and extract, district glass and glass calibration, protective design, charge
  inference, and the soil column and the half-space's calibration. The same code backs both, and
  gives the same answers. GeoTIFF elevations compressed with Deflate are read on Linux by a decoder
  written in Swift ([on Linux](../linux.md)).
- **Two benchmark cases have reproducible input decks and history readers.** The KB free-field and
  UFC room controls now fix the initial state, source model, mesh, boundaries and measurement
  rules. They keep raw histories and provenance and can export predictions for the existing
  scorer. They use a declared ideal-gas approximation and leave the historical entry unchanged
  ([protocol](../open-benchmark.md#reproducible-two-case-controls-21-october-2026)). A second
  code, pyro2, has been run to these decks, in one dimension (spherical) in place of their 3-D
  meshes, and scored 0.96 on the free field and 0.60 on the room
  ([to the decks' contract](../open-benchmark.md#to-the-decks-contract-21-october-2026)).
- **Training targets generated on an NVIDIA PC** (for developers). `scenebench field-export
  --cuda-job --cuda-source generated.cu` exports the initial air, fixed obstacles, species and
  receiver histories, and `Scripts/cuda/remote.py` deploys CUDA kernels derived from the solver's
  over SSH and returns compatible training arrays, keeping hot air, afterburning, the recommended
  radiative cooling and static terrain cut cells. A resumable batch runner checks each case against
  its Metal reference before accepting it. Physics it does not carry, such as refinement and
  moving structures, is refused. The app's Metal solver and its defaults are unchanged
  ([the CUDA backend](../cuda-backend.md)).
- **The first-phase recorder's pressure, traced** (for developers). Field exports can capture the
  signed pressure the first-phase recorder takes in, its seeded start included, and chosen
  receivers at further heights: `--trace-pressure` with `--trace-columns` and `--first-phase`. The
  trace is separate and versioned, replays the recorder exactly and has an explicit sample budget;
  check that plain and traced outputs agree before using one to diagnose the training labels.

## Changed

**Concrete walls' damage chances are higher.** The quick estimate's chances of concrete walls being
damaged were calibrated on Beirut's runs on 4 m cells. Runs refined to 2 m showed those read the
walls' peak loads 13–19% low at 400–600 m of a 0.5 kt blast, and damage about twice as many
concrete walls. Recalibrated against the refined runs, concrete walls' loads now take ×1.3
(×1.0 before). With the learned field model for ground-floor peaks on, its walls take the peak
×1.15 and the impulse ×0.95. Stone walls and roofs are unchanged. Against the refined runs, concrete
walls moderate or worse score Brier skill 0.59 (0.66 with the field model), each tenth of chance
within 0.1 of the runs ([walls and roofs](../glass-probability.md#walls-and-roofs)).

**The quick estimate loads streets and close-in façades harder, as fine runs do.** A façade whose
line to the charge runs down a street now takes the fine canyon runs' gain. At tonne-scale yields
between 15–30 m walls that is up to 1.4–2 times the peak; at Beirut's yields, a few per cent.
Below Z 4.6 m/kg^(1/3), to 1.5, the loads come from a close-in branch: Kingery–Bulmash's base times
the ratio open façades take on 0.5 m cells, 1.6–1.9 times what the district terms gave at
Z 2.5–4.6. On Beirut's near boxes the peaks at Z 3–5 are now 1.11 of the run's, against 0.66.
Protective design's sides close in and in streets are loaded harder, and its plans buy more for
them. The glass across Beirut's districts is unchanged, side for side
([the quick estimate](../surrogate-first-look.md#shielding-streets-and-the-close-in-branch-20-october-2026);
`blastbench canyon --offset`, `blastbench barrier`).

**The quick estimate's roofs on their own terms, and walls judged on uncapped loads.**
- **Roofs.** They now take their own fitted terms. Of the roofs either the estimate or a run calls
  damaged on Beirut's near boxes, held out, 29–64% take the run's level (6–42% on the façades'
  terms), and 99–100% come within one level.
- **Walls.** Judged again against loads saved without the old 65.5 kPa cap: 15–47% of the concrete
  and 33–41% of the stone take the run's level (the capped reference had said 0–10% for the
  concrete).

See [the first look](../surrogate-first-look.md#walls-and-roofs-20-october-2026).

**Replay shows vent panels, envelope damage, the thermal paint and the cloud as they came, and
recording no longer holds the run up.** A run's recording now keeps, with each frame where they
change, which vent panels have opened and which envelope panes have broken, each envelope
building's damage colour (glass broken from the frame its first pane breaks), the thermal
receivers' paint as far as reckoned then, and the cloud from the frame it was handed over in. A
replayed vented street turns each building to glass broken at the frame its first pane broke,
rather than showing the run's end throughout (0.6.0's known limitation). 0.6.0's recordings still
play, those layers as the run left them. The blast's field for each frame is now cut out on the
GPU by the batch that passes the frame's time, so a frame of 21 million cells takes about half a
millisecond of the main thread instead of 9 ms. Structures are recorded the same way, copying only
the motion and damage a replay needs: short office-tower comparisons took 1.19 ms a frame against
2.42 ms in a release build. The run is the same to the bit with recording on or off
([using the app](../using-the-app.md#replaying-a-run)).

**The buildings' damage is judged sooner when a run ends.** The judgement of each envelope
component's glass and structure now takes about 2.3 times less time with the same results. On
Beirut's 465,671 recorded façade loads it took 28–30 s where it took 63–81 s. The quick estimate
and the charge's drag use the same judgement.

**The terraced-house sample is now "Terraced houses, pre-1919 solid brick":** the terraced row
with solid one-brick walls, as most British houses standing in 1940 had; the 1930s cavity-walled
row stays as a second sample, "Terraced houses, 1930s cavity walls". Under the wartime bombs'
96 kg its houses grade within a category of the Ministry of Home Security's surveys at every
distance by 0.3 s, their fronts standing by arching between the party walls, where the cavity
row's front leaves go over, one or two categories too severe beyond 11 m. The standing of collapse
is unchanged ([validation](../validation.md#solid-one-brick-walls-20-october-2026)).

**The cloud's top to five minutes, in measured air.** Church's (1969) temperature and wind profiles
for each of his 23 shots are now transcribed (`Samples/Church1969/church-1969-profiles.json`), and
followed through them, the cloud's top stays with his measured tops to five minutes, 0.88 to 0.98
of them on average from three minutes on (0.68 at five in one lapse rate before), within 3% on the
17 shots profiled to 600 m ([the comparison](../fireball-rise.md#in-churchs-own-air-20-october-2026)).
The fall behind after two minutes was the one lapse rate standing in for layered night air. The
standing says so: at night, give the cloud a sounding. *Illustrative:* shot by shot the tops still
scatter by 27–55% after two minutes, as much as Church's repeated shots scatter among themselves,
and the band stays to two minutes.

**Raised charges dig smaller craters, such as a car bomb's or a charge on a table.** The crater's
height of burst is now measured to the charge's centre, as both Coopers measure it, not to its
underside. A sphere resting on the ground digs about 0.62 of the radius it did, and a charge higher
up less again. Against H. F. Cooper's 40 raised high-explosive craters (1976), the radius now reads
0.90 of BombCAD's rather than 0.57, and charges on the surface still read 1.01. Glacial soil
(Suffield) now digs by Cooper's own wet-soil law, so that the Suffield 500-ton shots stay within
the crater's band: Prairie Flat 1.17, Dial Pack 0.96, Snowball 0.79. The blast is unchanged: the
air's share on soft ground still tapers from the underside. The crater's standing stays an
approximation ([cratering](../cratering.md#the-centre-datum-22-october-2026)).

**The standing of beams struck by a falling weight is lowered to low.** An element that bends in
its cracked concrete, without hourglass control, was built as an option
(`StructureModel.assumedStrainCracking`, off) and matches section analysis. But it sends Saatci's
heavy drop 20% further than the test, so today's close agreement on struck beams leans on the
elements' hourglass control. The app's standing for structural response now says so; the results
themselves are unchanged ([validation](../validation.md#hourglass-control)).

**Macs that share work all need 0.7.0 (worker protocol 16).** A worker, a Mac watching another's
run, and a Mac running one for another must be on the same version; an older one is turned away
when it connects.

**Weak-wave diagnostics, for developers.** `blastbench acoustic-phase` records a 1, 10 and 100 Pa
planar pulse and its reflection against a linear-acoustic reference (`--fine` and `--cfl` for finer
cells and a second Courant number), and a standalone CPU Euler reference compares single, double
and mixed precision on the retained inputs. Neither changes the GPU solver or its defaults.

## Fixed

**Quick estimates are reproducible.** The same scene now gives the same glass, wall and roof
chances every time, to the last digit. Each building's random draws were keyed by an identifier
that a map's buildings are given anew each time a district is laid out, so two estimates of one
district differed slightly. The draws are now keyed by each building's geometry; the chances moved
once, by no more than their sampling noise.

**Ground-shock points near the charge feel the blast's front again.** Since the mapped start became
the default (18 October), the grid's clock begins when the one-dimensional solution's shock reaches
its radius, up to 64 cells or 6 m/kg^(1/3) out, and a ground point inside that radius took its
first load after the front had passed: under Xia et al.'s 1 t on 0.5 m cells, the soil column at
21 m read 0.068 m/s at 0.5 m deep instead of about 0.23. The frames before the clock now come from
the one-dimensional solution itself, in the app, `BombCAD run` and `blastbench groundmotion`, and
that column reads 0.249 m/s, within 5% of what Kingery–Bulmash's pulse gives it; points beyond the
radius, the air and the run's digests are unchanged. Illustrative, as the ground-shock models are
([ground shock](../ground-shock.md#before-the-mapped-starts-clock)).

**Corrupt plan and terrain files are refused, not crashed on.** Each of these stopped the reader
before: a DXF arc with a NaN or enormous angle, or a bulge between a vertex and its repeat; an SVG
arc whose radii overflow, or groups nested thousands deep; an ESRI grid whose `ncols` or `nrows`
is not a whole number from 2 to a billion; and a BigTIFF whose 64-bit offsets lie past a signed
64-bit integer. Blocks that insert one another many times over, and SVG `<use>` copies, past
5,000,000 pieces, and plans of tens of thousands of layers, no longer run without end. A DXF or
SVG with a NaN or infinite coordinate, and a grid's infinite cell size, corner or elevation, are
refused rather than read as walls at infinity, and a DXF number that does not parse (`1e`, `--1`)
is refused with its line, where it was read as 0. A grid whose first value is `nan` reads it as no
data instead of shifting every value by two. An SVG or .osm file in invalid UTF-8, which trapped
Linux's XML parser and which macOS's already refused, is refused before it is parsed. Found by
fuzzing the readers ([Linux](../linux.md#sanitizers-and-importer-fuzzing-20-october-2026));
each case is a test in ImporterRobustnessTests or ElevationFileRobustnessTests.

## Known limitations

**A charge on sloping or shaped ground starts as a balloon, not from the mapped burst.** Found
after this build was made: the recommended mapped start doesn't recognise uneven ground under the
charge, so such a charge falls back to the balloon start, whose result depends on the cell size.
On Beirut's terrain at 4 m cells the impulse reads about 9% low, and less in finer cells; peaks
are a little low too. Flat ground is not affected. Beirut's district runs, the 2 m references
behind the concrete walls' ×1.3, and the learned field model's training runs all started this
way. A fix is on the way for 0.7.1, with the walls re-judged on fixed references.

**The learned field model is illustrative, and off by default.** It gives only the ground-floor
peaks; the impulse, the roofs and the upper storeys stay on the surrogate. It was trained on 4 m
district runs of made-up layouts and judged on one real city's runs, Beirut's, and it needs a
15.7 MB download before it can be turned on
([learned models](../learned-models.md#in-the-apps-quick-estimate-21-october-2026)).

**People in the scene is illustrative and relative.** Its models come from other events and
hazards, and on Beirut 2020 its counts read 4 to 12 times the injuries and deaths recorded, so the
counts are behind Show counts with that warning; read it for where people would be hurt and for
the change against a baseline, not for numbers ([people in the scene](../people-in-the-scene.md)).

**A run on a Mac nearby carries the air and the structures only.** The fragments, the thermal
radiation, the ground points and the envelope damage run only on the Mac showing the scene, so they
are left out of a run sent elsewhere; pause, edits mid-run and **Continue from here** are not
offered for it yet. The Mac sharing the run needs a signed build, such as this release: its
firewall lets a Developer ID-signed BombCAD take connections without asking, but asks about an ad
hoc build (`make app`, `swift build`) anew each time it is rebuilt, which wants someone at that Mac.
Over Wi-Fi a mini sending or receiving through Network.framework carried 4–7 MB/s, so every 4th
cell of a city block is the size that fits
([measured](../distributed-computing.md#the-thin-client-in-the-app-21-october-2026)).

**The quick estimate's walls and roofs are still estimates.** They are not to be relied on for a
building's level; stone walls' chances read high by up to 0.18, and failure gets no chance. Streets that do not
point at the charge are still read as a row crossed, and their lee walls up to ×1.6 low
([the first look](../surrogate-first-look.md#walls-and-roofs-20-october-2026)).

**Barriers and buildings in front still barely change the quick estimate's loads.** A barrier
wall takes 2–3% off the peak behind it, so protective design rarely chooses one. Fine-cell runs
(1 t, 0.5 m cells) take half or more off within one or two of its heights behind it, and little
beyond ten. A shielding factor fitted on them held on the fine layouts pooled, but over-credited
barrier walls by 20% and is absent from the 4 m district runs, so it was not adopted. The
surrogate's barriers are on the safe side: a plan may buy glazing a close wall would have made
unnecessary ([protective design](../protective-design.md#stage-1-shielding-as-a-factor-stopped)).

**Bulk training data on the NVIDIA PCs is still gated.** Full-size terrain and Beirut pilots show
isolated differences from their Metal references, so bulk CUDA generation waits on resolving them
([the CUDA backend](../cuda-backend.md)).

**Full Keyboard Access and VoiceOver have not been checked on a real screen.** The labels and
keyboard paths were reviewed offscreen, which cannot test focus order or speech
([native interface review](../native-interface-review.md)).
