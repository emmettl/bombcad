Fifteenth build of BombCAD, an interactive blast simulator for Macs.

- macOS 15 or later, Apple silicon (arm64), Metal GPU
- Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, with the ticket stapled: it opens without
  Gatekeeper warnings
- Version 0.7.5, build 20, from commit 9a069d5b

**Install with Homebrew:** `brew install emmettl/tap/bombcad` installs the app and links its
headless runner as `bombcad`.

**The main changes**, each in full below:
- **Fire after the blast:** for hours after the blast, each building's chance of catching fire
  from its neighbours, drawn on the quick estimate or a run's damage. Illustrative.
- **VoiceOver and the keyboard:** every control named, the 3D view described and moved from the
  keyboard (View ▸ Camera), and a building selected without clicking the view.
- **A map's wood tags make a building timber.**
- **A large-panel block's collapse settles:** with its debris's displacements kept precisely its
  pile comes to rest, and the range is quoted on a ground with friction and in live air, read at
  5 s, the less damaging end with a fifth fewer failures.
- **The steel column's web and its frame against their tests' paper** (for developers).
- Every earlier setting stays selectable by name, to reproduce earlier estimates.
- **Worker protocol unchanged (19):** Macs on 0.7.2 to 0.7.5 can share work.

## New

**Fire after the blast in the app: a layer on the quick estimate, and on a run's damage.** Below
People in the scene, the sidebar's Fire after the blast section draws the fire spread over the
envelope buildings for 3 to 24 hours, through the glass the blast broke and the walls it failed.
- **Where fires start:** buildings picked by clicking them in the view (or the selected building's
  switch), every building near the charge, so many at random in a band of distance, or a run's
  surface-heating flags.
- **The wind,** its speed and where it comes from, and every building timber-clad as a contrast.
- **What it shows:** each building coloured by its chance of catching from a neighbour by the
  hour on a slider, or of burning then from a neighbour's fire: of the draws in which it was not
  itself a starting fire, the share in which it caught. So random starting fires do not colour the
  buildings they happen to land on. A building that starts a fire in every draw (picked, near the
  charge or flagged) is drawn in the top colour, certain, under its outline; with fires picked by
  hand the map is the chance of catching at all. Beside it: the buildings alight, burnt out and the
  floor reached, median and 5–95%.
- **Its colours** are on a log ramp, so that small chances separate: up to 1%, 2%, 5%, 10%, 20%,
  50%, 90% and above, labelled in the legend. A building that no draw counted sets alight is the
  neutral grey the close calls give settled buildings, and the legend says so. On Beirut at 12
  hours, with 20 fires at random within 1.5 km, 7,018 of the 9,708 buildings are grey.
- **The selected building's panel** gives its chance by the measure shown and the draws it was a
  starting fire in.
- **Other colourings stay by name:** Caught ▸ "At all, seeds with it" colours by the chance of
  catching at all, the building's own starting fires counted; Scale ▸ "Linear, in eighths" is the
  chance legend's linear ramp.

The fire is drawn away from the main thread: a 40-draw preview while the charge or a slider moves,
then the full 200 draws, with their progress shown; a change cancels the draws under way.

**Standing: illustrative,** shown first in the section, as for the people. See
[Fire spread](../fire-spread.md#the-app-stage-3-31-october-2026).

**Fire after the blast, building to building over hours, headless: `scenebench fire` and
`blastmodel fire`.** You give it a district's inputs, seeds and a wind.
- **Seeds:** the surface heating's ignitions, buildings near a point, map ids, or ignitions drawn
  at random.
- **What it gives:** the buildings alight and burnt out each hour, over a seeded ensemble, and each
  building's chance of burning, at all and from a neighbour (over the draws in which it was not a
  starting fire), with the draws it was a starting fire in, as text, JSON and CSV. It prints the
  app's map counted by colour and distance, by `--map-chance neighbour|caught` and
  `--map-scale log|linear`.

It is the NZ Fire Service's post-earthquake model (GNS Science and Victoria University of
Wellington, Reports 27, 44 and 80), adapted from cells to buildings and from earthquakes to blasts.
It works by radiation from a burning face, sparks carried by the wind, and fire through windows
that the quick estimate's broken glass opens. It reproduces its reports' flux table and critical
separations.

**Standing: illustrative,** and judged only qualitatively, on Beirut 2020. Twenty ignitions in its
concrete and stone take about a hundred buildings and die out within hours, where the same streets
in timber burn by the thousand. See [Fire spread](../fire-spread.md).

**VoiceOver and the keyboard reach the view, its legends and chart, and name every control.** The
3D view reads as "3D view" with a summary of the scene (its size, the charge, what is in it, the
time and what the surfaces show), and VoiceOver's actions orbit and zoom it; **View ▸ Camera** does
the same from the keyboard, ⌥⌘ and an arrow to orbit in 15° steps, ⌘= and ⌘− to zoom. The colour
scales read as their range, the chart below the view by what it plots and as VoiceOver's audio graph,
and each gauge's peak with its name. Every slider now reads its title and the value shown ("TNT
equivalent, 50 kg"), not its bare number; fields that read their name twice read it once; each
kept run's buttons in Compare Runs say which run; and each building in Envelope building damage has
**Select This Building**, so its chances and its fire switch are reached without clicking the view.
Checked offscreen by reading the app's accessibility tree, which tests now hold; VoiceOver's speech
on a real screen is still to be checked. See the
[native interface review](../native-interface-review.md#offscreen-accessibility-pass-1-november-2026).

**The steel frame under a blast, compared with its tests** (for developers). `blastbench dinu
--frame` builds the bunker specimen of Dinu et al. (Procedia Engineering 210, 2017) as the frame it
was: the shell column, its IPE220 beams as members on extended end plates, their lateral restraints,
and the web's strain gauges read every way. With the gauges 80 mm off the web's centreline, as the
drawing gives them, the elastic shots read 0.66–1.17 of seven measured peaks. The web rings at
0.82 ms against the test's 0.62, its fillets left out. The 1.8 kg shot at 0.2 m breaches the web
over 0.45–0.54 of the measured length at the measured tearing strain, shorter than the column with
its joint held still. Under a weight on the column (`--axial`), the frame holds in all five
cases the paper's own model collapsed in: the lighter charges do not breach its web, and its freed
flanges stay straight. Nothing changes in runs; details in
[the frame as built](../validation.md#the-frame-as-built-1-november-2026).

## Changed

**The steel column web case, judged against its test paper** (for developers). The bunker tests'
own paper (Dinu et al., Procedia Engineering 210, 2017) measures the web's breach at almost 500
and 600 mm, not the 0.3 m that 0.7.4's notes compared with, and gives the column as 1.99 m on
pinned ends with the beams' joint 0.3 m above the charge; `blastbench dinu` now builds that. At
the measured 27% tearing strain the breach is 0.65–0.78 of the measured length on 10 mm shells and
1.15–1.38 on 5 mm shells, and 20 mm shells hold. A tearing strain of 23% on 10 mm shells and 15% on
20 mm reproduces it: a rule that scales with the element's size, proposed, not adopted. Nothing
changes in runs; details in
[against the test paper](../validation.md#against-the-test-paper-30-october-2026).

**A map building tagged wood is timber.** File ▸ New District, a map's buildings added to a scene,
and the headless district benches now read OpenStreetMap's `building:material=wood`,
`timber_framing`, `timber`, `wood_frame` and `log` as a timber construction: walls and roof of
cross-laminated timber 0.105 m thick (three lamellae), the one timber the envelope damage model
has, its thickness assumed. Such a building's walls and roof are judged as timber, and in the fire
after the blast it burns from outside and its roof is not fire-rated. Before, a wood tag said
nothing and the building took the default concrete. Beirut's map carries no material tag at all,
so its estimates do not move; districts with wooden buildings do. The earlier rule stays by name,
to reproduce earlier estimates: New District ▸ "Wood tags not read (earlier estimates)", headless
`--wood-tags ignore`. See
[Building envelopes](../building-envelopes.md#buildings-from-map-data) and
[Fire spread](../fire-spread.md#the-app-stage-3-31-october-2026).

**A large-panel block's collapse is now quoted with its debris's displacements kept precisely,
and its pile at rest is quiet.** Whole-event runs keep each node's displacement together with the
rounding error its float leaves. Kept as a float, the debris that had fallen 16–26 m moved in steps
of 1.9 µm, and its walls went on failing at rest, 1,700 a second on twelve storeys. Kept
precisely, they fail 23–33 a second, and the pile is still by 6 s. What comes down does not change:
twelve storeys lose 237–309 m² of floor, 11–12 storeys past 15%. The collapse's standing, still
illustrative, now quotes 18,000–51,100 elements failed and 27–76 t of rubble, not 22,600–52,400 and
34–78 t: today's element fails a fifth fewer, and assumed strain 2% fewer. Today's debris reaches
further, 99% of it within 9.1 m, not 7.4 m. Three storeys, whose debris falls less than 8 m, are
not rerun. Every other scene is the same to the bit. For developers, `blastbench wholeevent
--float-displacements` gives the earlier runs' floats. See
[adopted: displacements kept precisely](../whole-event.md#adopted-displacements-kept-precisely-1-november-2026).

## Known limitations

**A large-panel block's collapse is quoted on a ground with friction, in live air, and read at
rest, at 5 s.** Whole-event runs take Coulomb friction 0.6 on the ground and keep the air awake by
default (the structure solver's own frictionless ground and sleeping air are unchanged elsewhere).
The collapse's standing, still illustrative, quotes its range between today's element and assumed
strain on them: twelve storeys lose 237–309 m² of floor, 11–12 storeys past 15%, with
18,000–51,100 elements failed and 27–76 t of rubble; three storeys lose none to 19% of their floor,
with 861–8,059 elements failed. The debris comes to rest, 99% of it within 1.3–9.1 m of the plan
(5.4–9.1 m on twelve storeys, 1.3–3.0 m on three), where the frictionless runs ended at up to
16.9 m and still creeping. On the frictionless ground three storeys lost 15% of their floor with
today's element; with friction their broken ground-floor wall holds at the foot and the block
stands. In sleeping air the frozen field pushed the falling bay: assumed strain took 74–81% of
three storeys' floor down, against 19% in live air. Run to 10 s, twelve storeys' collapse is over
by 3.5 s and holds; results are read once the block is at rest, at 5 s, and no later failure count
or rubble mass is quoted. For developers, `blastbench wholeevent --ground-friction 0` and
`--sleeping-air` give the earlier runs' ground and air, `--outcome <run folder>` names the row to
quote, and `--dump-split` writes the census split node by node. See
[ground friction 0.6](../whole-event.md#adopted-ground-friction-06-30-october-2026),
[awake air](../whole-event.md#adopted-awake-air-31-october-2026),
[the 10 s run](../whole-event.md#the-10-s-run-31-october-2026) and
[read at rest](../whole-event.md#read-at-rest-and-the-walls-that-fail-at-rest-31-october-2026).
