Twelfth build of BombCAD, an interactive blast simulator for Macs.

- macOS 15 or later, Apple silicon (arm64), Metal GPU
- Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, with the ticket stapled: it opens without
  Gatekeeper warnings
- Version 0.7.2, build 17, from commit COMMIT

**Install with Homebrew:** `brew install emmettl/tap/bombcad` installs the app and links its
headless runner as `bombcad`.

**The main changes**, each in full below:
- **A run on a Mac nearby now draws what a run here draws**, as it goes and in its recording: the
  fragments, the thermal paint, the ground points, the vent panels and the envelopes' damage
  colours, which 0.7.1 listed as a known limitation. The envelopes' damage comes back in the
  recording, where before it did not come back at all.
- **Every Mac that shares work needs 0.7.2** (worker protocol 19): a worker, a Mac watching
  another's run, a Mac running one for another, and the iPad viewer built from the source. A Mac
  on 0.7.1 is turned away when it connects.
- **A collapsing building's rubble no longer leaves grams of steel flying:** broken bars go with
  their rubble.
- **`BombCAD run` and `watch` keep the run of a project with its own thermal radiation or cloud**,
  where they could fail at its end.

## New

**A run on a Mac nearby now draws its fragments, thermal paint, ground points and envelope damage
as it goes, and keeps them in its recording.** Each frame from the other Mac carries what the
viewport draws over the scene, each part sent only when it has changed, as a run's recording
stores it: the fragments and tracers in flight and their strikes, the thermal paint, the ground
points' colours, the freestanding objects, the vent panels and panes opened and each envelope
building's damage colour. The live view draws them as a run here does, and replaying or scrubbing
the kept run shows them frame by frame as a recording made here does, envelope damage included,
which before did not come back at all. The last frame comes once the run and the models fed
alongside have finished, so it holds the damage judged at the end. What it costs the Mac running
it, measured on the Studio on 24 October 2026: 0.2 to 0.4 ms a frame, where reading the scene view
takes 2.5 to 7 ms. Compressed, the city block's frame grows by 91 kB (8%), and a district's with
thermal radiation by 349 kB, nearly all of it the thermal paint; on the sealed socket's 40 MB/s that
is 2 to 9 ms a frame. See
[Running the blast on a Mac nearby](../run-comparison.md#running-the-blast-on-a-mac-nearby) and
[what is drawn over the scene](../distributed-computing.md#what-is-drawn-over-the-scene-24-october-2026).

**Tools and the record** (for developers).

- **`BombCAD watch --as-app --on here`** measures a run on a Mac nearby on this Mac alone:
  `--as-app` runs the project as Run on does, its fragments, thermal radiation, ground points and
  cloud included, and `--on here` sends it to a worker process on this Mac
  ([what is drawn over the scene](../distributed-computing.md#what-is-drawn-over-the-scene-24-october-2026)).
- **The hybrid far field's judge counts the strips the near run does not hold.** On Beirut, 7,855
  of the 26,131 façade strips inside 1 km that `hybrid-judge` compares have no matching near-run
  strip, because the bench's two layouts project the map about their own corners and so sit
  buildings 0.48 m apart east-west. `district-hybrid` already gave them the far field; `hybrid-judge`
  left them out. It now judges them on the far field too, apart from the rest, and `district.json`
  counts them. They matter little: their peaks are 1.02–1.03 of the full run's, scattered more,
  and nothing beyond the hand-over moves. In the app no strip needs a match
  ([strips the near run does not hold](../hybrid-far-field.md#strips-the-near-run-does-not-hold-24-october-2026)).
- **The structure shaders' NaN guards hold under fast math.** Metal's fast math keeps NaNs and
  infinities, and its `isnan` and `isfinite` test the bits, so the guards in Structure.metal and
  Shell.metal stay in. `FastMathGuardTests` compiles each guarded path with the library's options and
  feeds it NaN and infinities; each test fails with its guard taken out. No shader code changed, so
  results are the same to the bit ([performance](../performance.md#fast-math-and-the-build)).
- **The run clock's short steps.** A step under 7.6 µs, as fine cells near a charge take, is cut
  to a whole tick of 2^-40 s, so the run's clock falls behind the steps' sum by under 0.9 ps a step
  (under 10 ns over 10 ms of 1 µs steps), still the same however the run is batched. Only the
  comment in Solver.metal changed.
- **Learned field model, round 5: no field model 3.** One levels network weighted toward the ground
  floor was trained in three settings and judged on Beirut's full-length 2 m boxes, to let the app
  need one file again. None matches field model 2 (the best gives ground-floor glass 0.932 exactly
  right against its 0.963), so field model 2 stays as it is
  ([round 5](../learned-models.md#round-5-one-levels-network-weighted-toward-the-ground-floor-24-october-2026)).

## Changed

**Macs that share work all need 0.7.2 (worker protocol 19).** The frames from a run on a Mac
nearby now carry what is drawn over the scene, so a worker, a Mac watching another's run, a Mac
running one for another and the iPad viewer must be on the same version; an older one is refused
when it connects, with a message saying it runs a different version of BombCAD. The iPad viewer
is not part of this download: build it again from this version's source
(`Scripts/build-ipad-viewer.sh`).

## Fixed

**A collapsing building's rubble no longer leaves grams of steel flying.** With failed concrete
kept as rubble particles (an option), bars that broke stayed on nodes that held nothing else. Their
steel now goes with the rubble their concrete became, with its momentum, and a bare element pulled
far out of shape (inside out, or stretched past twice its volume or squeezed below a quarter of it)
is removed with its steel. On the twelve-storey large-panel block, bars-only nodes used to fly off
at 50 to 200 m/s; now none is thrown off, and only a few of a gram or two among bare bars on the
ground pass 50 m/s for a moment. Runs without rubble are unchanged, to the bit
([whole-event simulation](../whole-event.md#6-steel-that-breaks-goes-with-its-rubble-24-october-2026)).

**`BombCAD run`, `BombCAD watch` without `--as-app` and sweeps no longer fail to keep the run of a
project that has its own thermal radiation or cloud.** Such a run started the project's radiation
and cloud as the app does, then tried to keep the run before they had caught up ("The thermal
radiation is still being reckoned"). It now leaves them, as it already left the project's
fragments and ground points: the headless way to ask for them is `--thermal` and `--cloud`, and
`--as-app` runs the project as the app does, its radiation and cloud included.

## Known limitations

**The learned field model is illustrative, and off by default.** Field model 2's walls pass the
calibration test on the refitted façade terms, but it gives only the façades' peaks: the impulse
and the roofs stay on the surrogate. It was trained on BombCAD's own runs of made-up districts and
judged on one real city's, Beirut's, and it needs a download before it can be turned on
([learned models](../learned-models.md#field-model-2-in-the-app-23-october-2026)).

**The hybrid far field reads high beyond 1.5 km at 1.1 kt.** On the refitted façade terms its peaks
there are 1.12–1.19 times the full run's (0.95–1.19 across the yields judged), on the safe side:
the surrogate's own over-read at that yield
([the hybrid far field](../hybrid-far-field.md#on-the-refitted-surrogate-23-october-2026)).

**People in the scene is illustrative and relative.** Its models come from other events and
hazards, and on Beirut 2020 its counts read 4 to 12 times the injuries and deaths recorded, so the
counts are behind Show counts with that warning; read it for where people would be hurt and for
the change against a baseline, not for numbers ([people in the scene](../people-in-the-scene.md)).

**A run on a Mac nearby: what is not yet carried or measured.** The fragments' strikes are drawn as
the run goes but, as for a run here, not recorded. **Continue from here** is not offered for a run
elsewhere. The thermal paint is most of each frame's added bytes, and a laptop's link has not been
measured with it. The Mac sharing the run needs a signed build, such as this release
([running on a Mac nearby](../run-comparison.md#running-the-blast-on-a-mac-nearby)).

**The iPad viewer has been tried in the Simulator only.** It is not yet measured on a real iPad:
its Wi-Fi, its GPU drawing the field, and its memory with a 250 MB recording
([the spatial viewer](../spatial-viewer.md)).

**Whole-event runs are not offered.** The large-panel block carried past its collapse with rubble
reaches 3.5 s with its energy budget closing and, since this version, no node thrown off, but
hourglass control does 60–76% of the elements' work, so collapse stays illustrative and the run is
not offered in the app ([whole-event simulation](../whole-event.md)).

**The quick estimate's walls and roofs are still estimates.** They are not to be relied on for a
building's level; stone walls' chances read high by up to 0.18, roofs' middle chances low, and
failure gets no chance ([walls and roofs](../glass-probability.md#walls-and-roofs)).

**Barriers and buildings in front still barely change the quick estimate's loads** (as in 0.7.0;
[protective design](../protective-design.md#stage-1-shielding-as-a-factor-stopped)).

**Full Keyboard Access and VoiceOver have not been checked on a real screen.** The labels and
keyboard paths were reviewed offscreen, which cannot test focus order or speech
([native interface review](../native-interface-review.md)).
