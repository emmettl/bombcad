Sixteenth build of BombCAD, an interactive blast simulator for Macs.

- macOS 15 or later, Apple silicon (arm64), Metal GPU
- Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, with the ticket stapled: it opens without
  Gatekeeper warnings
- Version 0.8.0, build 21, from commit COMMIT

**Install with Homebrew:** `brew install emmettl/tap/bombcad` installs the app and links its
headless runner as `bombcad`.

**The main changes**, each in full below:
- **The explorer, for someone new to blast:** a new window opens on a simpler mode (View ▸
  Explore). Put a charge in a made-up town square or high street, or in Beirut's port as a
  download, watch the wave, read what happens to the glass, the walls, people and fire in plain
  words with how sure each is, and try one change against a pinned picture.
- **The blast wave, live:** its front, strength and shadows drawn over a district before any run,
  following the charge as it is dragged, and refined by a learned model within about 1 km.
  Illustrative.
- **A live drag and instant what-ifs:** with a quick estimate on screen, dragging or resizing the
  charge re-colours Beirut's 14,169 buildings at 40–50 frames a second, and every building's
  glass, walls or roof can be changed at once, by a learned response on the GPU.
- **The fire after the blast keeps up** with its wind sliders and a drag.
- **Replays play at an even pace**, on the Mac and on the iPad viewer.
- **The charge read back from marked damage**, in milliseconds (headless for now).
- **A large-panel block's collapse carries its dust** (for developers).
- Every earlier setting stays selectable by name, to reproduce earlier estimates.
- **Worker protocol unchanged (19):** Macs on 0.7.2 to 0.8.0 can share work.

## New

**The explorer, for someone new to blast.** A new, blank window now opens on the explorer (View ▸
Explore, ⌃⌘E; View ▸ Workbench, ⌃⌘W, for the full app; the choice is remembered). Choose a scene:

- **the square,** a made-up town square of 398 buildings;
- **the street,** a made-up high street of 781 buildings;
- **Beirut's port** within 1.5 km, a download of 0.8 MB offered under one prompt together with the
  live wave's refinement, and opening with a short factual line about 4 August 2020.

Click to put the charge, or choose a named place; its size comes in steps of 50 kg, 500 kg,
5 tonnes and 500 tonnes of TNT, with a slider between, and its place is shown by a small red
sphere. The blast wave plays by itself with captions, then a strip steps through the glass, the
walls, the people and the fire, each in plain words with a "How sure?" line taken from its
standing. **Try something** pins the picture, draws its reach as a faint ring, and makes one
change: twice the charge (how much further the windows break, and why it is about a quarter, not
twice), the charge behind the brick terrace, or security film on every window (less glass thrown
into the rooms). Each building is then coloured better (blue), about the same (a warm grey) or
worse. People are shown only as where they are hurt and how much a change helps, never as counts.
Opening a built-in scene leaves the window's project unchanged. The explorer rests on the live
models already in the app (the quick estimate, the learned response, the live wave, the what-ifs,
the fire after the blast); their standing is unchanged, and the full estimate follows each change.
The learned response is now trained on small scenes as well (made-up squares on 2 m cells, charges
of 50 kg to 5 t), so the square's and the street's walls and roofs follow the full estimate as
closely as the CPU's preview or more: within 0.1 on 94–100% of the street's buildings and 82–100%
of the square's, with Beirut and the held-out district unchanged within 0.3 points. See
[the explorer](../explorer.md),
[stage 3](../explorer.md#stage-3-the-street-beiruts-port-and-the-ring-2-november-2026) and
[small cells and close charges](../learned-models.md#small-cells-and-close-charges-2-november-2026).

**Watch the blast wave sweep a district live, and drag the charge to move it.** Simulation ▸ Show
Live Blast Wave (⌥⌘B) draws the front before any run: when it arrives, how strong it is, and the
shadows behind buildings. A bar under the view has play, scrub and speed controls, and the wave
follows the charge as it is dragged, in tens of milliseconds. It is an estimate, not a run:
Kingery–Bulmash along the shortest paths round and over buildings, fitted to BombCAD's own 4 m
district runs. **Learned Refinement**, on by default in the bar, then corrects it cell by cell
within about 1 km of the charge, with a network trained on BombCAD's own district runs, easing to
the plain wave by 1.2 km. On Beirut's runs, which neither was fitted or trained on:

- 96% of cells in sight and 70% behind buildings come within ×1.25 of the runs' peaks, against 91%
  and 68% for the plain wave;
- close in behind buildings, the peaks go from ×1.7 high to about right;
- arrivals come out closer (the plain wave's beyond 200 m were 0.4–5% out on average), and the
  pulse carries the runs' impulse.

The refinement adds about 20 ms to a drag step on Beirut, so it runs while you drag. Its weights
(15.7 MB, the `live-wave-model-1` release) are fetched by **Download Refinement…** in the bar, only
once you agree; until then, or with the checkbox off, the wave is the plain one. Standing:
illustrative ([the live wave](../live-wave.md),
[the learned refinement](../live-wave.md#adopted-as-the-default-2-november-2026)). Headless,
`scenebench field-export --arrival` records a run's arrivals (now run over all 200 runs of the
learned field's list), `blastmodel live-wave-features` writes the refinement's training pairs, and
`blastmodel live-wave-judge` and `scenebench live-wave-judge --learned [--blend near,far]` judge the
wave against them.

**A live drag and instant what-ifs.** With a quick estimate on screen, dragging the charge colours
Beirut's 14,169 buildings at 40–50 frames a second on a Mac Studio (20–25 ms a frame, against
0.6–0.8 s), and holding the charge's size slider does the same, about 25 ms a frame; letting go
still makes the full estimate. A new **What if every building** group under Quick Estimate sets
every building's glass (toughened, filmed, laminated), walls (concrete, blockwork, masonry,
sheeting, timber) or roof and re-colours at once, then makes the full estimate; the yield's spread
does the same. A what-if is for the estimate only and never changes the project; it builds the
fire's buildings too, so timber walls burn and roofs that are not fire-rated spread, as they would
on a project built that way. It rests on Quick Estimate's new default **Drag preview: Learned
response (GPU)**, by the user's decision of 1 November 2026: small networks, trained on BombCAD's
own SDOF and chance code over the envelope model's constructions and glazing on Beirut and four
made-up districts, stand in for each component's SDOF and the chances' draws, and the sight lines
and loads are worked out on the GPU. Against the full estimate it is as close as the CPU's preview
on every measure and on each control moved, the glass closer: blown out within 0.1 on 96.8–96.9% of
Beirut's sides (85.8% before), the walls' and roofs' chances on 98.8–100.0% of buildings, the
damage colour the same for 99.5%. On a made-up district it never saw, the damage colour matched on
98.3–98.4% of buildings, against the CPU preview's 99.6–99.7%. The CPU's preview stays as **SDOF
and draws (CPU)**, and a quick estimate on the learned field model still uses it. See
[the live drag's learned response](../learned-models.md#the-live-drags-learned-response-1-november-2026)
and [instant what-ifs](../learned-models.md#instant-what-ifs-2-november-2026).

**Read the charge back from marked damage, in milliseconds (headless for now).** `blastmodel
charge-from-damage infer` (also `scenebench`) takes buildings marked glass whole or broken and
walls undamaged, damaged or heavily damaged, and gives the charge's likely place and yield with
their spread in a few milliseconds; each further mark adds under half a millisecond. A small
network learned from the quick estimate's own chances over Beirut and four made-up districts gives
it. On charges drawn from those chances its 50% and 90% regions hold the truth as often as they
claim, it comes within about 10% of the full posterior's yield, and it does as well on a district
it never saw. On Beirut's surveys it inherits the quick estimate's biases: GEER's glass reads tens
of tonnes, not the 0.3–1.1 kt the arrivals give. The network is built in, so the commands need no
weights file; the app has no panel for it yet. Standing: illustrative
([charge inference](../charge-inference.md#the-charge-from-marked-damage-learned-1-november-2026)).

**A large-panel block's collapse now carries its dust, as a passive cloud** (for developers).
`blastbench wholeevent` makes fines from the elements' fracture and crushing work and from a share
of the rubble's impact losses. The air carries them in two classes, 10 and 100 µm, each settling at
its own speed, and counts them as they settle on the ground, under the pile or on the block, or
leave the domain. Each sample writes the cloud's airborne mass, its height and reach, and maps of
the mass over each column of air. On twelve storeys, 46 kg of fines are made by 7 s and 30 kg are
in the air at 3.3 s; the cloud fills the 31 m domain, so its reach is the domain's, and 19 kg settle
on the pile and the block. Standing: illustrative, since the energy per kilogram of fines is
rock's, and every mass scales as its inverse. Whole-event runs carry the dust by default;
`--no-dust` leaves it out, and a run saved without it carries on without it. With the dust on, the
collapse is the same to the bit. See [dust, stage 1](../whole-event.md#dust-stage-1-1-november-2026)
and [adopted: the dust](../whole-event.md#adopted-the-dust-1-november-2026).

## Changed

**The fire after the blast keeps up while you move things.** With the fire shown, its wind sliders
now redraw its 40-draw preview in about 8 ms on Beirut's 14,169 buildings on a Mac Studio, against
45 ms, and dragging the charge holds about 25 ms a frame with the fire following. The district's
buildings and their neighbours are kept between frames, and only each building's glass and walls
are refreshed from the live estimate. The fire is the same as before, to the bit
([fire after the blast](../fire-spread.md#a-fast-path-for-the-preview-2-november-2026)).

**Replays play at an even pace, on the Mac and on the iPad viewer.** Play used to step a frame each
1/24 s, whatever the simulated time between frames. A run's frames are uneven in time (a street
run's gaps swing from 0.02 to 0.89 ms), so the wave's speed jumped by up to 40 times from one frame
to the next. Now a clock of simulated time moves on with each refresh of the display, and the frame
shown is the last recorded at or before it. A run plays in about as long as before. On the street
run, the simulated time shown per quarter second now varies by 1.12 times (90th over 10th
percentile, measured on an iPad Pro), against 1.5 to 1.6 before. The Mac shows the same frame for
any time as before, and its crater tail still plays in real time. On the iPad the view draws 60
frames a second while playing. While a run is still arriving, it plays up to the frames already in
([the viewer's notes](../spatial-viewer.md#on-the-ipad-pro-measured-2-november-2026)).

## Fixed

**A run on a Mac nearby that finished while its connection was down is taken up again whole.**
When the app reconnects to a run the sharing Mac kept going, that Mac hands over the run's scene
view, progress and, if it has finished, its result as soon as the session opens. If those arrived
before the app had asked for the run, as they could on a busy Mac, they were dropped: the view came
back without its scene, and a run that had already finished never delivered its result, so the app
waited for it until its silence limit gave up. The app now keeps what arrives for the run it
reconnected to until it asks for it.
