Ninth build of BombCAD, an interactive blast simulator for Macs.

- macOS 15 or later, Apple silicon (arm64), Metal GPU
- Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, with the ticket stapled: it opens without
  Gatekeeper warnings
- Version 0.6.0, build 13, from commit 27fb9be7

**Install with Homebrew:** `brew install emmettl/tap/bombcad` installs the app and links its
headless runner as `bombcad`.

**The main changes**, each in full below:
- **Replay a run and scrub through it**, without running it again. **Continue a run from any saved
  state**, the same to the bit as running it straight through.
- **A district in minutes:** File ▸ New District runs the air near the charge and judges the
  buildings beyond on the façade surrogate, with automatic hand-over. Click any building, near or
  far, to see its damage.
- **Refine toward the buildings you choose**, at a fraction of refining everywhere. A colouring
  shows which buildings finer cells could change.
- **Protective design in the app:** the cheapest glazing, film, stand-off and barrier changes that
  meet a glass hazard target, and a "new or tested glass" option.
- **Charge inference in the app**, from arrival times and, illustratively, the scene's broken glass.
- **Buildings from DXF and SVG floor plans**, with angled and curved walls and pitched roofs.
- **Glass and openings chances** in the quick estimate; **collapse** as a result with its own
  (illustrative) standing.
- **Macs that share work all need 0.6.0** (worker protocol 15).

## New

**Steel connections.** A steel beam that stops at a column, a through member or a shell now
meets it through a hinge: plastic at the connection's moment, letting the beam go at its fracture
turn. Welded unreinforced flanges, reduced beam sections, fin plates and bolted end plates (flush
and extended). *A check, not a validation:* under a column's removal, the welded, reduced-section
and fin-plate assemblies of NIST TN 1669 and Weigand and Berman fail 5–27% past the measured drop
at 1.07–1.16 of the measured load, their fracture turns taken from those same tests. Bolted end
plates take Culache's 40 ms tests, which a hinge from slow tests would read 13–41% too strong and
1.5–1.7 times too ductile; the welded and fin-plate joints, from slow tests, may be too ductile
under blast.

- **The office on fin plates collapses:** under the kerbside 1 t its front ground-storey columns
  are lost, and 334 of its 480 fin plates fracture by 1 s as the floors above drop about 650 mm
  (with rigid joints nothing moved more than 745 mm). By 3 s 428 have fractured, 34–40 on every
  floor: beams freed at both ends have dropped 7.5–8.5 m, the slabs sag between the columns, which
  stand, and the perimeter leans out up to 1.5 m, the side façade peeling away. The run stops with
  the floors still coming down; the collapse is illustrative, compared with nothing measured
  ([office](../office-tower.md#on-fin-plates)). Under gravity alone no fin plate fractures.
- **The transport hub's portal joints are bolted extended end plates,** as British frames are;
  none of its 46 fractures.

**New, as options.**

- **Structures' outlines as cut cells** (`structureCutCells`, off): a cell a structure's elements
  cross holds the air it has, its faces open as far as they are, as the terrain's cut cells do,
  with the ground, blocks and terrain cut cells taken as what they are
  ([structural model](../structural-model.md)). A free wall off the lattice takes its impulse
  within 2.6% of the line between its lattice values, where the staircase steps 6%, but its peak
  force overshoots 12% a tenth of a cell off it; 1.23 times the air's cost a cell. Merging small
  cells made the load 15% low and was dropped. Off until the peak is right.

**Easier by keyboard and VoiceOver** (from a real-screen review, [native interface
review](../native-interface-review.md#on-the-real-screen-labels-and-keyboard)):

- Menu items for what only toolbar and panel buttons did: File ▸ Import Model… (⇧⌘I) and Export
  Results as CSV…; Simulation ▸ Keep Run… (⌘K), Compare Runs… (⇧⌘K) and Parameter Sweep….
- Fields and buttons say which they are: Extent X, Y and Z; "Remove Block 1", "Remove gauge …";
  a box's or gauge's fields with its name; the advisor's target as its picker reads.
- Sheets open in their first field (Export for Rendering, whose interval is now a field beside its
  stepper, the parameter sweep, the run comparison and the import), and Tab no longer stops on the
  3D view (ContinuumKit 0.1.0-alpha.28).

**The record.**

- **Beirut against three more sources** ([Beirut](../beirut-2020.md)): the shock arrives at
  0.94–1.06 of Stennett et al.'s six observers' times; the grain silos, as a block, shade the
  peaks as Denny et al.'s silo tests measured at 2–3 m/kg^(1/3) and recover more slowly than their
  Viper::Blast farther out; the JRC's simulation (Valsamos et al., 2021) matches Kingery–Bulmash
  with a charge tuned to it, the figures quoted from it before being its Kingery–Bulmash values.
- **Footings on a slope** ([structural model](../structural-model.md#footings),
  [validation](../validation.md#footings-on-a-slope)): footings stay level, stepped into the
  slope, and read the ground round them from the terrain: each side buried as deep as the surface
  stands over the base, Coulomb's earth pressures for ground rising or falling away, and the
  bearing capacity cut by Vesić's (1 − tan β)², 0.88–1.39 of Meyerhof's charts on a slope's face
  and 0.85–1.14 near a 20° crest. Judged on closed forms only. A new sample, "Two-storey frame on
  a hillside".

**Click a building to see its damage.** Once buildings have been judged (a run's envelope damage, a
quick estimate, or a district's far field), clicking one in the view outlines it. The sidebar's
**Selected building** then shows its damage, what it is built of, and its sides' glass chances,
and says which judgement it is: the run's, the quick estimate's, or the far field's estimate for a
building beyond the air. The view also zooms out far enough to see a district's far buildings
([using the app](../using-the-app.md#the-view-and-the-sidebar)).

**Three parameters calibrated against 21 comparisons at once (a study).** The afterburning time,
the concrete's tension and the glass's flaw constant are calibrated together, a Kennedy–O'Hagan
posterior with a discrepancy for each regime over emulators of 169 runs. Today's afterburning
time and tension lie well inside their posteriors. Today's glass strength lies at the
posterior's weak edge, its safe side. No default changes. `BombCAD ensemble --design` runs
validation cases at chosen parameter values
([calibration](../calibration.md)).

**Charge inference from the glass on the scene's own buildings (headless).** The charge's yield
and place can now be inferred from which building sides' glass broke. Each side is loaded by the
façade load surrogate from the place being weighed, with the shielding and facing that place gives
it, rather than by the free field. On a made-up district, against BombCAD's own runs on 4 m cells,
the glass alone held the true yield inside its 90% interval for all four charges, three of them
within 15%, where the arrivals alone read 1.6–2 times high. On 2 m cells it held two of four, and
says only "large" where nearly all glass breaks. A run of BombCAD near the estimate corrects the
surrogate's loads as it corrects the arrivals (`--passes`). With the arrivals, that finds all four
yields within 2% on 2 m cells; on the glass alone it does not help, as the glass places the charge
20–70 m off. `scenebench infer-charge-study --forward-damage`
([charge inference](../charge-inference.md#damage-on-the-scenes-own-buildings)).

**In the app, the survey on the scene's own buildings.** Simulation ▸ Infer Charge from Arrivals…
offers **On this scene's buildings** under Broken glass, where the scene has buildings whose glass
the app judges. Each surveyed façade is put on the side of a building it lies on, and weighed by the
surrogate from the charge being searched. Illustrative, and circular on Beirut
([in the app](../charge-inference.md#in-the-app)).

**Infer the charge from what was seen after an explosion:** `scenebench infer-charge` takes the
shock's arrival times at observers (Rigby et al.'s table, or any CSV of places and time windows)
and gives the TNT yield and the place, and if asked a height and a clock offset, each with its
credible interval, in about a second ([charge inference](../charge-inference.md)). It rests on
Kingery–Bulmash's arrivals with the air's temperature, optionally corrected observer by observer
by a BombCAD run of the scene, and a sampler whose intervals hold synthetic charges as often as
they claim. *Approximation:* on Beirut it finds 0.50–0.54 kt (90%: 0.36–0.85) and Warehouse 12 to
within 32 m from Rigby's 31 observers, and 0.66 kt from Stennett et al.'s six, their own estimates
by the same curves (0.50 and 0.64 kt). Broken glass can be added from GEER's façade survey, but it
biases the yield low (the glass model breaks far more than the survey saw), so it is
*illustrative* and off by default. GEER's façade directions are read in degrees, and an arrival
placed before the explosion by a clock offset now makes a charge impossible rather than a
likelihood that is not a number, which could leave the search on a false, narrow answer.

**Continue a run from a moment of its replay, at full detail.** A recorded run saves its full
state every eighth of the way (Settings ▸ Replay, "Space for saved states", 512 MB a run) and
wherever you choose Simulation ▸ Save State Here (⌥⌘S). Replaying it, the slider marks those
states and **Continue from …** takes back the one at the time shown or just before, then runs on
from there, the same to the bit as the run went, structures, envelopes and venting included; Keep
Run keeps the states with the run ([continuing a run](../using-the-app.md#continuing-a-run-from-a-saved-state)).
Runs with fragments, ground points or thermal receivers, or whose delayed charges have begun to
fire, neither save nor continue yet, and a kept run continues only in its own scene and settings.

**Charges fired one after another.** Each further charge now takes a delay after the main one: in
the sidebar's **More charges** (which also adds, places and removes further charges), with
`BombCAD run --charge <kg>@<x>,<y>,<z>+<ms>`, or as `delay` in a layout. The run lays each down
when its clock reaches it: with the fine start, from its own one-dimensional solution where the
air about it is still until that solution is due, otherwise as a ball of hot gas, and the run says
which and why. Gauges, peaks, impulses and the injury plane take each charge's records at its own
times. Against the same two charges run without interruption on cells four times finer, a charge
delayed 1, 5 or 20 ms reads as the undelayed one does: the delay adds no error of its own
([validation](../validation.md#delayed-charges-19-october)); no open test of delayed charges
was found to compare with. See [a finely resolved
start](../air-blast-model.md#a-finely-resolved-start) for what is approximated.

**Which buildings finer cells could change.** After a run, the envelope damage section's "Colour
by" offers "Could finer cells change it". It colours each building settled, in doubt or likely
different, judging its loads again at the ends of the band within which cells half as large have
read district façades. On Beirut and a 150 m block it found 95–100% of the buildings that finer
cells changed in three of four comparisons, while flagging a fifth to a third of them. Those are
the buildings to refine toward. Changes that come from the façades' own outline on finer cells
are beyond it ([refinement steering](../refinement-steering.md#which-buildings-the-cells-could-flip)).

**Each building side's chance of broken, blown-out and thrown glass, in about a minute.** Quick
Estimate (and `BombCAD run --surrogate`) now draws the first look a hundred times over the yield,
each building's glass strength and the estimate's own scatter, and colours each side by its chance
of glass broken, blown out or thrown past 3 m (GSA's condition 3b); "Yield known to within" sets
the yield's spread, or `--surrogate-yields` takes an inferred charge's samples. Against Beirut's
full runs at 0.2, 0.5 and 1.1 kt the chances are calibrated (each tenth of chance within 0.1 of the
share of runs, for all three); against GEER's survey they are not, sharing the glass model's
over-breaking, so they read as a run's spread, not the event's.
Walls and roofs are given no chances ([glass by chance](../glass-probability.md)).

**Glass strength: the evidence for a decision.** Every judged glass comparison was rerun with the
panes 1.2, 1.4 and 1.6 times E1300's strength: Monk and Clubley's panes, E1300's threshold,
Osnes's laminated panes, Beirut against GEER, the façade surrogate, and protective-design plans.
Today's strength is the only one on the safe side everywhere. Stronger defaults leave plans at the
edge of the damage up to 46% cheaper, but unsafe if the glass is weathered. The default is
unchanged ([glass strength](../glass-strength.md)).

**A district in minutes: the air near the charge, the surrogate beyond (File ▸ New District, and
headless as `scenebench district-hybrid`).** The air runs in 3-D only out to a hand-over radius.
Beyond it, each façade's load comes from the façade load surrogate, its peak and impulse matched to
the near run where both hold and carried on the near run's trend with distance. On Beirut, handing
over at 1 km, the near run took 135–335 s on the CI mini against the full run's 1,604–2,464 s, and
laying out and judging all 14,169 buildings about a minute more on the Studio. Inside the hand-over
the near run's loads are the full run's. Beyond it, sides with some glass broken come within 0.04
of the full run's to 1.5 km at every yield (to 2 km at 0.5 kt, 2.5 km at 1.1 kt), and arrivals come
2–4% earlier. It holds less at 0.2 kt beyond 1.5 km (0.16–0.28 under) and for blown-out glass in
some bands, and walls and roofs beyond the hand-over keep the surrogate's standing. In the app, New
District weighs the district against this Mac: with the cells left to the advisor, it runs the
whole district in the air when it fits on 4 m cells within an hour, and otherwise hands over at
12 m/kg^(1/3), brought in by tenths (to half, and no nearer than 50 m) until the near run fits; the
sheet says which, and why. The buildings beyond
are kept beside the project, never voxelised, and saved with it. When the run ends they are judged
in seconds and painted with their damage, labelled a far field estimate with its match and
standing. The app carries the trend to 1.5 times the hand-over and holds it from there; at 0.2 kt
that brought the share broken at 1.75–2 km to 0.40 against the full run's 0.61, from 0.33. After the
run, the far buildings' sides get glass chances drawn over the far field's scatter and each
building's glass strength, and the view can colour by them ([a city in
minutes](../hybrid-far-field.md#using-it)).

**Infer the charge from arrivals, in the app.** Simulation ▸ Infer Charge from Arrivals… reads
observers' arrival times (a CSV, or Rigby et al.'s table) and works back to the charge's yield and
place with their 68% and 90% intervals, and a clock offset or the burst's height if asked, off the
main thread with progress. A plan draws the observers and the place's credible region; the standing
is the table's, illustrative once the optional broken-glass survey is used; and Place Charge at
Median puts the scene's charge there, given the scene corner's longitude and latitude
([using the app](../using-the-app.md#inferring-the-charge-from-arrivals)).

**Steel members buckling under a sudden end load, checked against tests.** Kenny's drop-hammer
tests on steel and aluminium strips (Dalhousie, 2001) are now a benchmark (`blastbench kenny`).
The final deflection of the peak buckle comes within half to twice of measured in 13 of 16
series with the default Eurocode bows, and in 15 of 16 with Kenny's measured imperfections. The
slenderest steel strips read 2.5–3.7 times too far with the default bows. The model's buckle
lies near mid-length, where the tests' lay near the clamp. The steel needs its strain-rate law:
without it the strips read 1.3–7 times too far ([buckling](../shell-model.md#buckling)).

**Materials look like what they are** in the viewport and in snapshots, read from each material's
own data: concrete warm grey, steel blue-grey with a highlight of the sun, profiled sheeting light
metallic, glass tinted and see-through, timber and CLT warm brown, brick red-brown, blockwork buff,
terrain earth. Exposure-only buildings are drawn by their walls' construction. Each is restrained,
so the blast's field and the damage colouring read over it. Failed elements are rubble in a dark
grey, not the material darkened: tinted by brick or timber, rubble read as the heaviest damage's
dark red to someone with protanopia. Under simulated protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia, every
material stays at least ΔE 15 from each stage of damage and from its rubble, and the heaviest damage
at least ΔE 10 from rubble (`MaterialAppearanceTests`); a wall of each, rendered, reads nearest its
own colour (`MaterialRenderingTests`).

**New or tested glass, a construction's option.** A building's glass can be judged as designed
(weathered, E1300's strength, still the default) or as new or tested glass, 1.55 times as strong:
the least of Osnes et al.'s tested panes. Set it under **Glass** in the construction editor. It
applies to envelope damage, the façade glass chances and protective design, where re-glazing with
new glass is also a change a plan may make, at your cost. Its standing notes that new glass
weathers in service, and that at a little more strength (1.58) one of Osnes's cracked panes would
hold ([glass strength](../glass-strength.md#the-option-new-or-tested-glass)).

**Freestanding objects can share the air with a deformable structure** (stage 1, for the
experimental object drivers on the coarse grid). An object's cells stay solid and moving with it
while a structure beside it bends, and the structure's cells stay its own while the objects move.
In a closed test, a box beside a plate struck by 1 kg changes the gas by what the plate alone does
(16 g of 54 kg). Stage 2 adds contact: thrown into a wall in the air, a box strikes the wall's
nodes at the structure's own step, and the wall's reaction is within 3% of the same throw without
air. **Compute Motion now takes a deformable structure** in the scene. The crop takes in the
structure, the air is uniform (0.1 m), and the section gives the impulse the objects struck it with.
The structure is drawn as placed. `rigidboxdemo --car-wall` blows a saloon into a concrete wall
([freestanding objects](../freestanding-objects.md#limitations-and-future-work)).

**BombCAD's validation as an open benchmark another blast code can run and be scored on.** Ten
cases in a solver-neutral JSON format (`Benchmarks/open/cases`), each with its scene, charge and
stated TNT equivalence, gauges, and the measured or reference values with their uncertainty and
full citations: Kingery–Bulmash in the open and on a wall, ANFO before pipe columns, a blast wall's
shielding, two close-in reflected loads, a closed room's gas pressure, slabs bent by hung charges,
laminated panes in a shock tube and ground motion under 1 t. A standard-library scorer
(`Scripts/open-benchmark/score.py`) gives each gauge's ratio, each case's score within its stated
factor and a summary. BombCAD's own entry, quoted from the record without new runs, scores 0.65 on
average (83 of 123 values within their bands), the shortfalls those the record already names. No
other code has run the cases yet; what is missing is listed ([open benchmark](../open-benchmark.md)).

**A chance that a building side's openings are blown out, fitted to Beirut's survey.** The quick
estimate's glass chances gain a fourth, beside some glass broken, blown out and thrown past 3 m:
that a map building side's windows, doors and frames are nearly all blown out, from a fragility on
the side's peak load fitted to GEER's survey of Beirut (median 18.5 kPa at 4 m cells). It is the
only chance now judged against that survey, whose classes turned out to be openings photographed
two months on rather than glass broken: 0.31 at 0.75–1 km where GEER saw 0.27, where the glass's
blown out gave 0.91. Fitted to one event, so illustrative elsewhere. "Colour by" in the damage
section shows it, and `--surrogate` writes it; see [glass by
chance](../glass-probability.md#why-it-over-breaks-against-geer-19-october-2026).

**Pitched roofs.** Shells can now be laid at a pitch, so a roof can have real slopes, a ridge and gables. The structure editor's *Add Pitched Roof* sets the pitch, the ridge's direction and the covering. It puts two raftered slopes over the structure from the tops of its walls, raises the end walls to gables, seats the slopes on the walls under them and hangs the tiles on their battens. On import, *Roof slabs at a pitch as shells at their pitch* brings an IFC roof slab in at its pitch; without it, such a slab stays a rigid obstacle. The terraced row now has a 40° roof with purlins, and its tiles hang on their nibs instead of sliding off. The seats' and nibs' laws are assumed from how roofs are built, not measured. No pitched roof under blast was found to compare with, so the standing table lists *Shells at a pitch* as an approximation (docs/shell-model.md#shells-at-a-pitch).

**Floor plans with walls at an angle, curved walls, and plans drawn turned.** The DXF plan
importer now raises walls at an angle to the plan, and the chords of curved ones (arcs and
polylines' bulges), as walls turned in plan: each a shell in its own plane, upright, tied to the
walls and slabs it meets. A plan drawn turned is first turned square to its walls, so that they
lie square to the air's cells. The IFC import does the same for whole walls turned in plan, with
**Pitched roofs and angled walls as shells in their own planes** on. The example house drawn
turned by 17° reads as drawn square; with a curved bay in its front wall, the bay's six chords
share their corners and, under a 20 kg charge, hold (13 to 119 mm) where the flat leaf beside them
breaks. The seams of mitred corners are read as joints, not walls. Windows in walls at an angle
are left open, and the floors over them are the outline's rectangle; see [From architectural
plans](../using-the-app.md#from-architectural-plans).

**Buildings from DXF floor plans, in the app.** **Import Model…** now takes an ASCII DXF plan and
raises it into a building without a BIM program: walls drawn by both faces (read as the regions
their lines enclose) or by centrelines, joined at corners and junctions; doors and windows from
blocks, groups of lines or gaps; the storeys by repeating the plan, with each layer on every
storey or one; and flat floors and roof, seated inside the outer walls as a BIM model lays them
(or over the whole outline). A sheet maps the drawing's layers to walls (each with a construction
such as a brick-and-block cavity wall), doors, windows and floor outlines, asks for the units
where the drawing does not say, and previews what was found. The building then goes through the
same review as an IFC import, as shells and panes or rigid obstacles. Walls at an angle and
curved walls are read too (the next entry); [From architectural
plans](../using-the-app.md#from-architectural-plans) describes both this route and the IFC one. On the example
house it gives the same building as the FreeCAD route, and under a 20 kg charge the same front
load and the same broken front wall; on plans cut from buildingSMART's Duplex Apartment it finds
the outer walls whole and every door and window the cut shows.

**Floor plans from SVG.** **Import Model…** now takes an SVG floor plan as it takes a DXF one, so a
plan from a drawing program, or a PDF opened in one and saved as SVG, can be raised into a
building: lines, polylines, polygons, rectangles, circles, ellipses and paths (curves and arcs in
pieces of 15° at most), each on its group's layer, with their transforms; symbols placed with
`<use>` are single doors or windows. The page's size gives the units where it is in millimetres
or other real units, and the sheet takes the plan's scale (1:100) and shows its size in metres.
The example house as SVG at 1:100 reads as its DXF does; see [From architectural
plans](../using-the-app.md#from-architectural-plans).

**Buildings from architectural floor plans, by way of IFC.** A new guide,
[From architectural plans](../using-the-app.md#from-architectural-plans), takes a 2D plan
through FreeCAD's BIM workbench to IFC and into BombCAD as shells and panes. Its worked example,
a two-storey house with cavity walls, is in `Samples/Plans`: the plan as DXF, the FreeCAD script
that raises it, and the IFC. Two importer fixes came from it. A wall's material layers are now
read by what each layer is made of, where the IFC names the layer by its place ("Outer leaf").
Before, such walls fell back to the default material. A cavity wall's second leaf, whose stiffness
is still left out, now keeps its mass on the leaf that is meshed. FreeCAD 1.1 writes walls
without their layers, so the example adds them after export with IfcOpenShell.

**Polyurea liners on masonry walls.** A building's unreinforced masonry walls can be lined with
spray-on polyurea in the construction editor. The liner holds the masonry once it cracks and
carries the load as a membrane until it tears. In one-way damage a lined wall stays (heavy)
where a bare one falls. It is judged against one lined and one bare blockwork wall in the
University of Ottawa's shock tube (Ciornei, 2012), so its standing is illustrative:
- past the wall's thickness, 1.3–1.9 times the measured deflection;
- the liner tears late;
- the masonry underneath does not arch, as the tests' did.

FRP, steel-stud liners and fabric catchers are not offered, for lack of open tests
([liners](../building-envelopes.md#polyurea-liners-on-masonry)).

**Protective design: the cheapest changes that meet a glass hazard target, in the app and
headless.** Simulation ▸ Protective Design… (and `scenebench protect`) takes a threat (a charge,
weighted places, or an inferred charge's samples), a target for each side (no pane worse than a
GSA condition except with an accepted chance), and your costs for the changes allowed: security
film, toughened or laminated glass, filling a window in, the scene's stand-off lines and barrier
walls. It ranks the plans that meet the target, cheapest first, from the façade load surrogate's
draws. Choosing a plan colours each side by its residual chance, and Apply Plan writes its glazing
into the project as each side's own glazing mix. Glazing mixes now carry a glass type, so a plan
is an ordinary envelope construction that the envelope damage assessment judges and a run can
confirm (`scenebench protect --confirm`). On the design prototype's own problems
(`Scripts/protective/`) the search finds the same plans, and stand-off comes first in every
cheapest one. `Scenario.addBarrier` builds a barrier wall on the ground as a fixed block the air
sees; the app warns when a charge stands inside a stand-off line. *Plans are illustrative until a
run confirms them*, and the surrogate, fitted on buildings only, takes little off for a barrier
(about 2% of the peak behind a 10 m wall), so a barrier rarely earns its cost until the refit
with barrier runs ([protective design](../protective-design.md),
[using the app](../using-the-app.md#protective-design)).

**Refine toward the buildings that matter.** Click a building in the view and turn on **Refine
toward this building**, for as many as matter: the run sharpens the shock only along corridors from
each charge to them, and the rest of the scene stays on the coarse cells. On Beirut each chosen
building came out as refining everywhere gives it, where the coarse cells got two of three wrong,
for about 0.4 of the refined GPU time. The sidebar gives the advisor's estimate against refining
everywhere; saved runs keep the choice, and `scenebench district-compare --refine-toward` does the
same headless ([refinement steering](../refinement-steering.md#refining-toward-chosen-buildings)).

**File ▸ New District offers refined air, and over a city it now pays.** The window can lay a district's air out as a coarser base refined to half its cells where the shock is. The advisor takes it where it is finer at the façades and no dearer than the uniform cells that fit this Mac, and the window shows the other kind's cost beside the one chosen. Refinement stops at a ceiling over the ground (`SolverConfiguration.refinementCeiling`): the height of all but the tallest 2% of the buildings, plus four base cells for the shock's top. Over Beirut's 1 km box, uncapped refinement had filled the column to the 320 m top. Capped at 72 m, 8 m refined once to 4 m kept the 4 m cells' glass and arrival times in 0.44 of their run time (74 s against 170 s on the CI mini), with façade peaks 2–12% lower. A tower above the ceiling takes its upper floors' load on the coarse cells, and refined air takes the ground as a staircase of whole cells for now ([Beirut, 2020](../beirut-2020.md#what-cell-size-the-glass-and-the-damage-need); [using the app](../using-the-app.md#a-district-from-a-map)).

**Refinement confined to chosen regions, and a corridor to one building.**
`SolverConfiguration.refinementConfinedTo` refines the air only inside the boxes it lists. On
Beirut's 1 km box, `scenebench district-compare --corridor x,y` refines a corridor from the charge
to one building. That gives the fully refined run's answer for the building at 2–9% of its refined
work, and about a third of its GPU time. Steering a whole district by the buildings whose outcome
the coarse cells leave in doubt (`--steer`) saved nothing, because those buildings fill a domain
drawn to the damage's reach. Refining only beside a building read its façade lower than coarse
cells ([refinement steering](../refinement-steering.md)).

**Replay a run, and scrub through it, without running it again.** A run records what the view
draws as it goes, up to 240 frames of the blast's field, the structures, the fragments in flight and
the freestanding objects; Replay under the view plays it back, steps it and drags to any moment, with
no physics. Keep Run keeps the recording in the project, so a reopened project replays its kept
runs. Recording takes at most 2% of a run's time and, by default, 256 MB a run (Settings ▸ Replay);
vent panels, envelope damage, the thermal paint and the cloud replay as the run left them
([using the app](../using-the-app.md#replaying-a-run)).

**Security film and toughened glass as envelope constructions.** A building's panes can carry
security film, 4–14 mil, daylight, wet glazed or mechanically attached, and its glass can be
toughened, set in the construction editor. Once the glass cracks, the film holds the fragments as
a membrane until its anchorage lets go. Against 67 open GSA tests of filmed 1/4" panes:
- wet-glazed panes stay, condition 2, as 18 of 24 were graded, the model never worse;
- daylight film's sheets stay while pushed and go on the rebound, one class lighter than most tests;
- mechanical attachments, judged on a vendor's capacity, pull out, never better than a test.

See [security film](../building-envelopes.md#security-film).

**Save a run part way and carry it on later, the same to the bit** (headless: the air, its
refinement and its clocks, structures, envelopes' recorders and venting, exposure planes and
deflagrations). `blastbench snapshot --checkpoint-at T --checkpoint-out DIR` saves
the solver's state at the first 64-step boundary from T, `--resume DIR` carries a run on from it,
and `--from DIR` draws it as it was saved ([checkpoints](../checkpoints.md)). On the CI mini, 24
runs (refined once and twice, on cut terrain, with gravity, on the recommended air, up to 50
million cells) carried on from a checkpoint ended exactly as the same runs straight through. 50
million cells take 78 MB stored early in a run, saved in 4 s and taken back in 0.6 s. Struck walls in
solids and shells, glazed buildings with their recorders and vented panes, and a gas-filled room
carry on to the bit too. In the app, a recorded run saves its state every eighth of the way, within
Settings ▸ Replay's "Space for saved states" (512 MB a run), and wherever you choose with
Simulation ▸ Save State Here (⌥⌘S); replaying, the slider marks them and Continue from… carries the
run on from the one at or before the time shown, the same to the bit as the run straight through
([continuing a run](../using-the-app.md#continuing-a-run-from-a-saved-state)). Runs with
fragments, ground points or thermal receivers, or with a delayed charge already fired, are refused
with the reason; a saved state is read only by a build whose saved parts keep the same versions.

**A quick estimate of a district's envelope damage in seconds, without running the air.**
Simulation ▸ Quick Estimate of Envelope Damage (and `BombCAD run --surrogate`) predicts each wall,
roof and façade strip's load from its distance, facing and the buildings in its way
(Kingery–Bulmash corrected by terms fitted to BombCAD's runs of two made-up districts), then
judges it with the run's own damage model. Over Beirut's 724,000 components it takes about half a
minute, against 25–28 minutes for each full run on the CI mini (27–41 minutes when timed again for
the hybrid comparison). Judged against Beirut's full runs, which it was not fitted to, each
building side's glass state agrees with the run's for 75–84% of sides and the share broken by
distance comes within about 0.1 to 2 km; walls and roofs are not to be relied on. It is shown as
an estimate with that standing, never as a run ([first look](../surrogate-first-look.md)).

## Changed

**Defaults.**

- **Every charge starts from a finely resolved burst,** not only a lone one: each is solved in one
  dimension and mapped to its own radius (0.8 of the way to its nearest solid, pane or face, half
  way to the nearest other charge), and a charge on a reflecting face, a model's mirror plane or
  corner, is mirrored as one on the ground (2, 4 or 8 times its mass). Two 10 kg bursts 30 m apart:
  peaks within the mapped radius 98–115% of Kingery–Bulmash where the balloon gave 52–69%, impulses
  within 8% of the curves superposed. The chamber's four charges now take it too, moving its
  results 3% or less. A lone charge in the open is as before, bit for bit.
- **Structures on the terrain:** the clamp and the ground's connection also hold a base within
  half an element of the terrain under it, not only at z = 0, and a structure's loose nodes,
  shell nodes and rubble land on the terrain instead of the floor. Scenes without terrain are
  unchanged.

- **An envelope's load history from the blast's arrival starts at 500 Pa at the component,** not
  when the air around it wakes, which is a tile ahead of the shock: Beirut's windows had opened
  0.13–0.35 s before their load, and on refined air most of a half-second window closed before it
  came. Options saved before keep the old rule.

**The record.**

- **Beirut refined:** after the two refined-air fixes and the 500 Pa windows, the district on 8 m
  cells refined twice loads its façades as 3 m cells do. On the ground cut through the cells, a
  ringing at the finest level ran ahead of the shock, and six of Rigby's observers read arrivals
  25–52% early. With the ground as a staircase of whole cells, refined air arrives at 0.97–1.03 of
  Kingery–Bulmash at his observers 250–1,075 m out and gives the 3 m cells' façade loads, glass and
  stone, the concrete more damaged (its peaks higher, 0.79–0.86 of the curves). Refined up to the
  domain's top it took 3.2 times their run time; capped at a ceiling over the ground it now pays
  (New District, under New). Refined air takes the ground as a staircase for now.
- **The slab, wall and Keys comparisons off the balloon start:** Wu, Wang and Yao's slabs,
  Chiquito's, Alsubaei's walls, Keys's walls in the open, Mendonça's studies on today's air and the
  Kasun cubicle now start a lone charge from the one-dimensional solution, as the app does, at the
  open faces' clearance. Against the balloon in the same domains: Chiquito's P7 left 111 → 121 mm,
  Keys's debris 1.60 → 1.75 m out, Wang's P1 peak 10% lower, Yao's T4 underside damage 29% more,
  the rest within 6%; no standing moved. The chamber followed with the mapped start for every
  charge (Defaults, above).
- **Keys's debris judged by its side:** in the open it lies too far out, the safe side for its
  reach; in the shock tube it is thrown half as far as measured, the unsafe side; and the walls
  fall as one column where the tests' folded, so how they come apart is wrong either way.

**Compute Motion warns when a car's gap to the ground lies between cell faces.** Beside a
deformable structure the motion run moves cars on uniform 0.1 m cells, which take a saloon's
0.15 m underbody gap as 0.1 m. That gap can decide whether the car overturns or rocks back. The
standing and the Freestanding objects section now say so for each such car, as they do on a slope
or for a tilted car: whether it overturns may differ on finer cells. Beside a structure the
section offers uniform cells that resolve the gap, with their cost (about 3.2 times the time on
0.075 m cells), or says that the scene is too large for them
([freestanding objects](../freestanding-objects.md#which-outcomes-the-whole-cell-outline-flips-20-october-2026)).

**Collapse has a standing of its own: illustrative.** Wherever a scene has a structure, a storey
lost or a structure down is a result apart from its members' damage. It has no error band, so it
no longer takes the local damage's band, as the large-panel flats coming down did. The
structure section shows its badge. Collapse has been compared once for slabs, blind, and missed,
and for brick houses only by survey category; no frame or panel block has been compared falling
([standing](../standing.md#collapse)).

**The quick estimate, the hybrid far field and charge inference take their standing from the
standing table**, as runs do: their figures against Beirut's full runs and BombCAD's own charges
are in [validation.md](../validation.md#estimates-beside-a-run), and the lines each prints are
worded from them and count in the table's identity. The quick estimate's far loads now read 1.1–1.6
times the run's on the median, as its page has it, not "up to ×1.6–2". `scenebench infer-charge`
prints its standing and writes it into `--out`'s JSON.

**The fireball cools as it radiates.** With afterburning and hot air, the fireball's gas now loses
the heat it radiates, by default (**Fireball cools as it radiates**; projects run before keep
their gas's heat). Against a 41 kg test (Clough and Clubley) the fireball now grows to its measured
12.5 m and fades within the second, its flux at 4 m falling from 175 W/cm² at 100 ms to 19 by
500 ms, where before it held near 2,500 K with a flat flux, and the fluences it leaves are about
half. Gas pressures in closed rooms come out 2–4% lower (94–105% of UFC 3-340-02's curve), and
open-air peaks and impulses move by 0.4% or less. The run advisor prices its memory, some 1.2 GB
on a grid of a hundred million cells. A fireball still radiates more than TNT's measured 4–7%,
and reaches its size later than measured; the thermal radiation stays illustrative
([against the 41 kg fireball](../thermal-radiation.md#against-clough-and-clubleys-41-kg-fireball)).

**The fireball's first flash shows.** The thermal volume's hot air now absorbs as equilibrium air
does at its temperature and density (Kivel and Bailey's predictions, as NASA TN D-1031 correlates
them; `hotAirAbsorption`, on by default). The fireball's frames from the one-dimensional start keep
the products' soot within the products' own gas, and the volume marches them through the
solution's own spherical shells rather than voxels. A strong shock's layer of air at 6,000 K and
more, which absorbed 0.1/m and was hidden behind a cooler sooty skin and smeared over its voxel, is
now opaque within centimetres and shows: Dial Pack's first maximum at 1.25 ms comes three-fifths as
bright as measured, from a quarter, and falls away over the next milliseconds as measured. Cooler
gas, the fireball from tens of milliseconds on, is as before, and so is everything measured against
Clough and Clubley's 41 kg. The thermal radiation stays illustrative ([before the grid's
clock](../thermal-radiation.md#before-the-grids-clock)).

**Freestanding objects' motion runs are about twice as fast in crowded scenes, and repeat to the
bit.** Moving objects through refined air now remaps only the cells that open or close, side by
side on the CPU's cores, so the coupling, which cost more than the air in the car park, room and
pen, takes a quarter to a third of its time: the car park's 0.5 s run took 771 s against 1,417 s
on the CI Mac mini ([freestanding objects](../freestanding-objects.md#populated-scenes)). The
gas, outlines and walls are the same to the bit. Two faults went with it: a box in the corner of
an L of others was opened by their remap and closed by its own (in the furnished room the pieces
now move up to 5 mm differently), and the air's loads were summed in an order the GPU decided, so
the same run could part after 0.05 s; both are fixed.

**Refined air among many buildings runs faster.** A newly refined block now checks only the
buildings near it, rather than every building in the scene, when it works out which of its cells
are solid. The results are the same to the bit. On Beirut's 1 km box, a run refined everywhere
takes about 12% less GPU time, and a corridor refined towards one building about 25% less
([performance](../performance.md#where-a-refined-runs-gpu-time-goes)).

**Refined runs of a structure standing on terrain cut the terrain through the cells.** Until
now, refined air under a structure on a slope kept the ground as a staircase of whole cells.
Refined levels now shut in the thin cut cells under a structure, as the unrefined grid does, and
the closed air keeps its gas to 2–5e-8. On "Two-storey frame on a hillside" the frame's largest
displacement comes within 6% of fine uniform cut cells refined once and 9% twice, where the staircase gave 2.7 and 1.6 times
as much. Such runs take 26% more GPU time refined once and 8% twice, the cost of cut cells over
the staircase; the cap itself is 2% of it ([terrain](../terrain.md#cut-cells)). Saved runs
keep the setting they were recorded with.

**A structure settled under its own weight once is not settled again.** Running the same building
again, after changing the charge, the air or nothing at all, takes its settled state from the
last run instead of settling it afresh: the office tower starts its blast about 3 minutes sooner
(176 s to 7 s), the twelve-storey tower 34 s sooner, and the run is the same to the bit. A change
to the building, its materials or settings, or a new build of BombCAD settles afresh
([settling](../structural-model.md#settling-under-its-own-weight)).

**Profiled sheeting's buckled flanges keep their folds by default.** A flange that has buckled is
pulled straight in tension at the strength of its fold's plastic hinges, not at its yield.
ADBLAST's cladding now ends nearer level after the blast: the 1.5 mm sheets end level or lifted,
as tested. `--no-fold` in `blastbench langdon` and `adblast` restores the old behaviour
([profiled sheeting](../profiled-sheeting.md#what-is-left-scoped)).

**The short comparisons now run on the mapped start.** The envelope walls and slabs, Nian's wall,
the façade, the anchorage wall, Tyndall's CLT building, the hub's roof glass and the terraced
houses were rerun on the default start; each still takes `--balloon-start`. They moved by 0–4%,
with every damage level the same. The exception is Tyndall's heaviest test, whose broken front
now goes 1.9 times the measured distance (1.5–1.6 on the balloon); its standing's evidence says
so ([record](../validation.md#off-the-balloon-on-19-october)).

**Structures' outlines as cut cells have a standing of their own.** With `structureCutCells` on
(unrefined air), the standing lists "Structures' outlines as cut cells" as an approximation for the
air's results and the structure's: a wall's impulse follows its place within a cell, but its peak
force runs up to 12% high a tenth of a cell off the lattice, and coarse air reads its faces higher
than whole cells do. A gauge beside such a structure carries a note instead of the reflected bands,
which were derived on whole cells ([structural model](../structural-model.md#coupling-to-the-air)).
Off, nothing changes.

**Gauges read at the wall have a standing of their own.** With `gaugesReadWallPressure` on, the
standing lists "Gauges read at the wall" as an approximation for peaks and impulses, and a gauge it
reads at a wall carries a note instead of the reflected bands, which were derived from the cell
beside the wall: the read brings peaks nearer Kingery–Bulmash but overshoots the close-in impulse
([air-blast model](../air-blast-model.md#reflected-peaks-at-a-wall)). Off, nothing changes.

**The error bands' input spreads and Wu's band rechecked on today's defaults.** The validation
ensemble, which samples each case's uncertain inputs, was rerun on the mapped start with the open
faces at the clearance rule ([the model's spread](../validation.md#the-models-spread-from-uncertain-inputs)).
The structural cases spread as before, and Wu's slab 4%. The close-in gauges now spread 7%,
against 8%. A wall's reflected impulse spreads 2%, against 1%, and
on the mapped start it reads 11% above Kingery–Bulmash, so it no longer falls inside the model's
spread. Wu's band for slabs under a charge close in is now 0.90–1.19 of the measured peak, from
runs in a domain that meets the rule. Its low end was 0.91, read in the harness's tighter box.

**Macs that share work all need 0.6.0 (worker protocol 15).** A worker, a Mac watching another's
run, and the Mac that shares a sweep or a run's companions must all be on this version: the
fireball's frames now carry the hot air's absorption and the 1-D shells, delayed charges travel
with the scene, and the materials' colours travel to a watched view. A nearby Mac on an earlier version
is listed as incompatible and is not sent work. A run kept with collapse's new standing cannot be opened by an app from
before this release.

## Fixed

- **A structure standing on cut terrain went non-finite as the blast arrived:** cut cells less
  than half air under it were shut in and their columns' differences came back to them magnified.
  They are now solid for the run, as under a block. Refined runs with such a structure keep the
  terrain as a staircase until the same rule reaches the refined levels.

- **Refined air beside a building lost pressure when its patches were released.** A coarse cell
  cut by a block's finer outline averaged its fine cells' air over its own share, not the air's,
  and came out short by the block's share (half its fine air's pressure in the test); released
  patches left that deficit behind. Terrain-only scenes are unchanged. Suspected of Beirut's
  negative precursor at 1 km, which is being rerun.

**A charge inside a block releases nothing on every grid, as the sidebar's warning says.** A charge inside a block, a wall, a map building, the ground or a structure used to go off on uniform cells, its balloon laid in whatever air lay within two cells (the air round a building's block), and vanish on refined air, whose finer balloon found none: a district whose charge sat on a map building broke 43% of its glass on 4 m cells and none refined. Such a charge now releases nothing on either, `scenebench district-compare` refuses the scene with a message, and a charge just outside a block whose fine cells hold no air within the balloon is laid on the coarse cells instead of being lost ([using the app](../using-the-app.md#the-view-and-the-sidebar)).

**A building with slabs on columns is built the same by every launch.** The order in which slab
nodes were tied to column heads (and, for a node near two heads, which head it went to) and the
numbering of the punching rings followed an order that changes from one launch to the next. The
structure handed to the GPU therefore differed between launches, and a run could differ in its
last bits, which grow once something fails. The heads are now taken in order: the twelve-storey
and office towers' structures are the same, byte for byte, in every launch.

**Air over terrain no longer gains gas where buildings stand in a slope.** A cut cell between a
building's face and the rising ground took a ghost beyond the building built before the ground's
mirror was set, so gas passed through the building's face as the blast went by: a street over a
hill in a closed box gained 0.34 kg and 0.28 MJ in 60 steps, refined or not. The ghost is now built
in order and the box keeps its gas to round-off ([terrain](../terrain.md)). Scenes whose
buildings stand on flat ground, or on terrain without cut cells, are unchanged.

**Cells beside a charge on a slope are no longer drained to nothing.** A strong expansion over the
terrain's small cut cells could leave a cell's redistributed difference larger than what the cell
held, on coarse cells and refined alike, and the pressure floor then added the energy back. Now
a cell takes no more than leaves it a tenth of its density and pressure, and the rest goes on up
its column to cells that can take it ([terrain](../terrain.md)). A closed box now keeps its
energy to the joule where the floor had added 1.2–5.4 kJ, and the impulse near the charge moves
by under 0.05%. Runs in which no difference asked a cell for nine tenths of its pressure are
unchanged to the bit (84 of `blastbench digest`'s 112 cases; the rest are charges over terrain).

**A charge placed on a building now goes off: the building over it is cleared.** File ▸ New
District and the Map buildings section placed every footprint, so a charge on a building lay in its
solid block and released nothing. **Clear the building over the charge**, on by default, now leaves
open ground under the charge's cell, as for Beirut's Warehouse 12, which the map no longer holds.
The sheet and the standing say the building was cleared: its walls neither confine nor vent the
blast. Off, the building stands and the sheet warns that the charge is buried ([using the
app](../using-the-app.md#a-district-from-a-map)).

**The thermal record starts at detonation again on the fine start.** With the fine (mapped) start,
the grid's clock begins only once the charge's shock has spread to the mapped radius: some 9 ms
for 1 kg on 0.5 m cells, 200 ms for 500 t on 4 m cells. Until now a run's fireball, and so its
thermal radiation, began there (0.5.0's known limitation). The fireball before then now comes
from the same one-dimensional solution, laid onto a lattice finer than the grid's cells. Against
Dial Pack, the first maximum of the radiation pulse is half as bright again as the balloon start
gave, but still a quarter of the measured; the thermal radiation stays illustrative
([before the grid's clock](../thermal-radiation.md#before-the-grids-clock)).

**Far-field faces conserve the gas.** The recommended air's open faces built their ghost state one
way for the cell at the face and another for the cell next in, so the face between those two cells
was fluxed twice over: a tube drawing air in through such faces took in about 10% more mass than
its oxygen accounted for over its first steps. Both cells now build the ghost alike
(`farFieldInflowConserved`). Results move only once the blast reaches the open faces, and then
little: Kingery–Bulmash's farthest incident impulse in `blastbench validate` goes from 106% to 105%.

**A structure on footings now settles under its own weight before the blast**, where before it
never did and started the blast unloaded. Its footing joints chattered at rest, a few cm/s at
microns: the joint was worked out from the scene's positions, tens of metres rounded to microns,
and its stiffness turned that rounding into a fresh traction every step. It is now worked out from
displacements ([footings](../structural-model.md#footings)), and the frame on a hillside
settles in 0.18 s. Every footing moves a little: FoRDy's shaken events by up to 2.4 mrad in peak
rotation, FoRCy's slow test by under 0.004 in its moment ratios, Alsubaei's walls on footings
226 → 228 mm and 356 → 362 mm at the top ([validation](../validation.md#perimeter-walls-under-field-blasts)).
A body still moving at the 1 s limit whose displacement has stood still over its last 5 ms also
counts as settled now; bodies that settled before are unchanged.

**A torn laminated interlayer no longer holds cracked glass together across one axis.** Once its
film had torn, a laminated pane in the structure model still kept a little shear capacity across
its second axis, set by the film's few megapascals; it now has none either way.

**On the mapped start, glass breaks and envelope histories are timed from detonation** (also in
0.5.1). The mapped start begins a run some time after detonation, but each pane's and wall's clock
in venting, and the clock the envelope component histories are binned by, still started at zero:
a pane broken by the blast recorded its break that much early, and a history's window start and
bins ran behind the run's time. They now start with the run's clock, as the GPU's own clock
already did; runs on the balloon start are unchanged ([building envelopes](../building-envelopes.md)).

**A Mac nearby that stops answering no longer holds a sweep or a pairing for ever.** Connecting
to a `nearby:` worker, TLS handshake and all, must finish within 20 seconds, and each message of
the first exchange within 30, or the connection is dropped with a message saying which Mac stopped
answering; checking a pairing code may take up to 5 minutes. Once a session has started, a run
takes as long as it takes. A Mac sharing its GPU now starts taking sessions with a new pairing's
key before it tells the other Mac the pairing holds, so a session opened straight after pairing no
longer reaches the listener being replaced.

**A locked Mac can save its pairings.** A worker Mac usually serves while locked, and while it is
locked macOS refuses to create files in the strictest protection class, so saving a pairing failed.
The pairings file is now protected from the first login after start-up, still readable only by its owner.

**Floor plans as offices draw them.** The plan importer now places doors drawn with their leaf and
swing (it dropped them before), closes walls whose faces stop together or a few millimetres short,
finds a turned plan's angle exactly (a tenth of a degree off left walls read as centrelines),
passes over furniture blocks on wall layers and short wall ends at an angle, and refuses a DXF cut
off part way rather than reading half of it. A corpus of 25 small DXF and SVG plans, each trying
one such thing with its expected answer, checks it (Fixtures/FloorPlans, `scenebench plan-check`);
hatching alone is not read, a recorded limitation.

**A refined run that runs out of memory while its pools grow carries on as it was.** When the
first level of twice-refined air could not grow its pool for want of memory, it had already moved
its share of the shared buffers, and the second level was left in the old ones, its cells read from
the wrong place. Growth now changes nothing until every buffer it needs has been made, so a run
short of memory keeps its pools and refines no more than they hold, as intended. Runs whose pools
grow are unchanged.

**Refined air over terrain no longer stirs the still air beside buildings ahead of a blast.**
Where a refined patch's face met coarser air beside a building standing in cut ground, the finer
level gave the coarser cell the pressure on the whole face though that cell had counted only the
terrain's open share of it, and the difference pushed small cells beside the building every step:
hundreds of pascals to kilopascals in still air, most with two levels, and in Beirut's district an
early arrival about 0.7 kPa ahead of the shock at the south-east gauges. The push now goes through
the face's open share only; the air ahead of a blast stays within a pascal
([terrain](../terrain.md)). Runs over terrain without buildings standing in it are unchanged.

**Broken panes open the refined air where they are.** (Also in 0.5.1.) On refined air with venting (the default), a
mismatch between the solver's and the GPU's layout of the panes' lookup left the two highest pane
cells never opened on the fine grid, and opened the fine cells of the corner cell at the origin,
ground or not, whenever the first pane broke. The layouts now agree, and a test holds them to it.
Runs without refinement or without venting are unchanged.

**Saved façade loads keep close-in peaks above 65.5 kPa.** `district-compare --save-loads` no longer clips façade pressures above 65.5 kPa. The strips were stored as 16-bit floats in pascals, so every close-in peak read as 65,504 Pa in `district-glass`, `surrogate-extract` and `surrogate-judge`. They are now stored over a power-of-two scale recorded in `strips.json`, at the same relative precision. Folders saved earlier still read as before, clipped peaks and all.

**A restarted run starts its steel connections, screwed sheeting and spring supports whole
again.** (Also in 0.5.1.) A shell structure kept its fractured hinges, pulled-through screws and yielded spring
supports across a restart, so a rerun of the office on fin plates began with its fin plates
already gone.

**A concrete or masonry shell keeps its own rule through the thickness beside timber.** (Also in 0.5.1.) Any
timber in a model, a glulam beam or a timber floor, put every shell on the timber's
Gauss–Lobatto points, so a concrete slab's cracking moved when a timber beam was added beside it.
Each shell now integrates by its own material's rule ([shell model](../shell-model.md)): models
without timber shells are unchanged to the bit, and mixed ones, as the terraced row's brick walls
beside its timber floors, move a little. A joint's friction exponent must also now be below 1,
where its friction force still grows with pressure (the neoprene pad's is 0.20).

**Parameter sweeps and ensembles no longer keep every run's memory,** 0.5.0's known limitation of
sweeps. Building a solver left it in an autorelease pool that a worker's or a command-line thread
never drains, so runs made one after another kept all their memory: six solvers of 584 MB built in
turn held 2.8–3.2 GB, and `blastbench validate` reached 43 GB on the 36 GB Studio. The app's
parameter sweeps and headless workers, and the ensembles' runs, now build their solvers in a pool of
their own, and six built in turn hold 0–7 MB. Reading a run's cells piled up the same way: each read
of a cell's state, mask, peak or impulse autoreleased its buffer, so a sweep, digest or export
reading every cell added an entry per read until the pool drained (a digest peaked at 445 MB, now
327 MB); the solver now takes those addresses once. Results are bit for bit the same
(`SolverMemoryTests`).

**A steel connection a hinge cannot use is refused** when a document is opened or a structure is
built: a fracture rotation that is not positive (which broke every hinge on the first step) or not
a number (which never broke one), or a strength ratio that is not positive. Objects beside two or
more structures laid out in tiles no longer write past the structures' wall-velocity buffer.

## Known limitations

**Full Keyboard Access and VoiceOver have not been checked on a real screen.** The labels and
keyboard paths were reviewed offscreen, which cannot test focus order or speech
([native interface review](../native-interface-review.md)).

**Barriers barely change the quick estimate's loads.** The façade load surrogate's shielding was
fitted on buildings, so a barrier wall takes about 2% off the peak behind it and protective design
rarely chooses one; a refit on fine-cell runs with barriers is under way
([protective design](../protective-design.md)).

**Past 1.5 times the hand-over, the far field holds the near run's trend rather than fading it.**
Fading it toward the surrogate over any length was judged on Beirut's full runs at 0.2, 0.5 and
1.1 kt, and none gained overall, so the trend is held as before

**How far reflected peaks fall short at a wall, by cells per charge length.** A reflected peak read
beside a wall comes out low wherever the cells there are coarse against the charge: at 1 m/kg^(1/3)
it is 38–40% of Kingery–Bulmash's with 19–20 cells across W^(1/3), and 62–69% with 37–50, and at
4 m/kg^(1/3) 83% with 19 and 95% with 37 ([air-blast model](../air-blast-model.md#reflected-peaks-at-a-wall)).
Most of it is the cell beside the wall reading the reflected shock smeared over it, not the
incident wave. Gauges can read the pressure at the wall instead (`gaugesReadWallPressure`,
`--wall-read`, off), which brings peaks to 82–96% close in on 0.02 m cells and 69–93% on 0.25 m
for 100 kg, but overshoots the close-in impulse by 13–31% on 0.05 m cells and 19–43% on 0.02 m, so it is for
peaks alone.
