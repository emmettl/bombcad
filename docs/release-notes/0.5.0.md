**Draft, not released.** Notes for 0.5.0, gathered from main since 0.4.0 (build 10, `9482ba7`).

Seventh build of BombCAD, an interactive blast simulator for Macs.

- macOS 15 or later, Apple silicon (arm64), Metal GPU
- Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, with the ticket stapled
- Version 0.5.0, build —, from commit —

What you can now do, each with its [standing](../standing.md) (how far the result has been
compared with measurement):

- **Open six new sample buildings.**
  - *A twelve-storey office of steel and glass:* a concrete core, a steel frame, composite floors
    and a glazed curtain wall, a van bomb at the kerb and two envelope buildings across the street
    ([office tower](../office-tower.md)). *Showcase:* nothing compared as a building; its steel
    members as below, its damage illustrative.
  - *A large-panel block of flats* (Ronan Point type), as two presets: a town gas explosion in a
    corner flat, and a street charge, both collapsing progressively through the joints
    ([large-panel flats](../large-panel-flats.md)). *Illustrative:* the gas flat peaks at 12 kPa
    where the inquiry put 21 kPa on the flank wall, the unsafe side; collapse has been compared
    once, blind, and missed.
  - *A vapour cloud explosion at a process plant,* after Texas City 2005, beside its control room
    and trailers ([process plant](../process-plant-explosion.md)). *Illustrative:* the flame is sped
    up by a fitted factor, and no open, congested cloud has been compared.
  - *A portal-frame warehouse clad in profiled steel sheeting* ([profiled
    sheeting](../profiled-sheeting.md)), its sheets screwed to their purlins by screws that pull
    through. *Approximation* for the sheeting as shells: ADBLAST's screwed cladding's first deflection
    is 0.90–1.35 of the measured on all ten tests, though what is left after is not right, and
    Langdon's corrugated panels' permanent sets are within half to twice on 10 of 13. The one-way
    envelope method for sheeting is an approximation too: against the same ten shots, on the middle
    strip of its ribs, it reads 0.65–1.09 of the measured deflection.
  - *A transport hub:* a train shed with a long-span steel and glass roof beside a glazed concourse,
    with laminated glass that holds together once cracked, glass falling from overhead and slender
    steel members that buckle ([transport hub](../transport-hub.md)). *Showcase:* nothing compared
    as a building; its members, bowed by default, buckle at 0.97–1.02 of Eurocode 3's curves.
    Laminated glass is in the structure model too, as panes that fragment whole and hang on their
    interlayer, and the hub's roof can be laminated (an option). *Compared:* ten of Osnes's fifteen
    shock-tube panes crack or hold as measured, the cracked ones 0.80–1.28 of the measured
    deflection; six of Hooper's eight bonded panes leave their silicone at 0.87–1.24 of the
    measured speed.
  - *A row of three 1930s terraced houses* of brick cavity walls, wall-tied, with joisted timber
    floors seated in pockets and tiled roofs thrown as debris ([shell model](../shell-model.md#the-terraced-houses)).
    *Illustrative:* against the Second World War's surveys of bombed houses, every house is in the
    surveyed category or the one below at 0.1 s; by 0.3 s its cracked front walls go over, where the
    surveyed houses mostly stood.
- **See the crater** each charge digs in earth or rock, shaded into the ground, with the energy the
  ground takes; below a water table its walls slump ([cratering](../cratering.md)). *Approximation*
  for the diameter (five of six measured craters within the scaling's scatter), *illustrative* for
  the depth and lip.
- **Give a charge a casing** and see its fragments: Mott's masses from the casing, a measured drag,
  where they land, what they penetrate (concrete, steel, glass) and the hazardous fragment
  distance as DDESB counts it ([fragments](../fragments.md)). *Approximation* when the casing's
  thickness and diameter are given (Cooper's examples; penetration within 1–2% of UFC 3-340-02's
  and THOR's own examples), *illustrative* otherwise.
- **Read the blast's hazards to people:** the K-factor safety distances, the minimum safe distance
  and whether the blast or the fragments set it, and how far the blast injures someone standing in
  the scene, drawn in the view ([hazards](../hazards.md)). The minimum safe distance never falls
  below the published one: K40, a cased charge's default fragment distance, or for a charge inside a
  building the structure debris distance. *Approximation:* published methods,
  checked against their own distances, not against injuries; not for triage or casualty estimates.
- **Envelope buildings that open.** With "Vent broken glazing" (the default), panes that break
  open in the air, refined or not; walls, roofs and slabs that fail by their one-way damage open
  too, and so do fragments' perforations and close-in breaches by UFC 3-340-02's curves
  ([building envelopes](../building-envelopes.md)). Freestanding walls are judged as cantilevers,
  masonry walls rock against the masonry above them, and map buildings' façades hold a window a
  storey. Envelopes can now be of profiled sheeting or cross-laminated timber too. Each wall, slab
  and roof is judged on its equivalent uniform load, its faces weighted by the shape it would fail
  in. *Approximation* for glass, concrete panels and sheeting (Alsubaei's walls 5 of 7 outcomes as
  measured, the 125 kg blockwork short of failing where it collapsed, the unsafe side; Wu's one-face
  slabs 0.91–1.56), *illustrative* for brick, CLT and the inside.
- **Debris from failed walls, roofs and panes,** thrown at each one's speed at failure (UFC 3-340-02)
  over the shape it fails in, flown and judged beside the fragments, its landings joining the
  minimum safe distance ([hazards](../hazards.md#debris-from-failed-envelope-components)). For a
  charge inside a building that distance is never under DoD's structure debris distance (DESR
  6055.09). *Illustrative:* Keys's brick walls' mean reach as measured but the far tail short;
  Kasun III's launch speeds 0.77–0.94 of measured, and the Kasun cubicle's hazardous debris to
  129 m where the tests found 290–315 m, the unsafe side, which DoD's 268 m covers. No piece size,
  drag or launch angle alone closes it: the tests had a spread the model has not.
- **Steel members compared with blasted beams and columns** (Nassr, W150 and W200 under 50–250 kg
  of ANFO), bowed by default so that they buckle as Eurocode 3's curves say (0.97–1.02 of them),
  their yield rising with strain rate by Cowper and Symonds's law by grade (mild steel's constants
  to 300 MPa, ERDC's measured A992 and A572 grade 50 above). *Compared,* unfitted: beams 0.57–1.12
  of the measured deflection, columns 0.30–1.61; a column bent about its weak axis 1.6 times;
  their connections not compared.
- **Build in timber:** softwood, glulam and CLT presets with a timber editor (grain, lamellae,
  strengths), meshed as shells and beams ([shell model](../shell-model.md#timber)). *Compared:*
  against the Ottawa shock tube's CLT panels (the elastic shots 0.72–1.21 of the peak) and glulam
  beams (1.06–1.30), its strength calibrated on their static tests, and Tyndall's full-scale CLT
  building: with the front's load within 5% of the report's, the first storey's inbound peak is
  0.62–0.64 of measured under the two lighter charges (too stiff) and 1.6 times under the heaviest
  (too weak once cracked), the second storey 1.1–1.4 times; the connections, modelled rigid, are
  the first suspect.
- **Burn hydrogen or town gas** as well as methane and propane, and put congestion below the
  grid, pipes and bars too small for the cells, that folds a flame ([deflagration](../deflagration.md),
  [congestion](../congestion-model.md)). Lean hydrogen's flame responds to its diffusion (1/Le,
  nothing fitted). *Illustrative:* vented hydrogen reads either way, 1.24–2.96 of FM Global's
  chamber tests (the safe side) but 0.19–0.45 in HySEA's containers vented through their roofs (the
  unsafe side), their doors within the repeats' scatter; in a closed sphere K_G is 0.89–0.99 of the
  measured. Town gas was compared only in a closed sphere. Against MERGE's rigs, congestion's finer
  pitches come within 0.4–1.2 but the coarser three to five times too high.
- **Ground shock from a half-space**, its soil yielding near the charge, as well as the layered
  column ([ground shock](../ground-shock.md)). *Illustrative:* against one measured 1 t burst the
  vertical motion is far too high (the safe side) and the horizontal off by 2–11 times.
- **Watch a run on another Mac** from a laptop: `BombCAD watch <project> --on <host>` runs the
  blast on a large GPU and draws it here, over Wi-Fi ([distributed
  computing](../distributed-computing.md#a-thin-client)). A headless prototype.
- **Lay out a district from your own map** with File ▸ New District: an OpenStreetMap export or
  GeoJSON footprints, optionally a heights raster and a terrain, the charge placed on a map of the
  footprints, the open faces at the clearance rule, and the finest cells this Mac can hold by the
  advisor's estimate, with the run's cost shown before the project is made ([a district from a
  map](../using-the-app.md#a-district-from-a-map)). The app fetches nothing. *Illustrative,* as
  Beirut's comparison above stands.
- **Neighbourhoods from open data:** building heights from an elevation grid (BigTIFF too) and
  bare ground from a surface model ([buildings from map
  data](../building-envelopes.md#buildings-from-map-data)).

**Defaults changed.** Results differ from 0.4.0's for the same project:

- **The air starts from a finely resolved burst,** solved in one dimension and mapped onto the
  grid, instead of a balloon of hot gas a few cells wide ([air-blast
  model](../air-blast-model.md#a-finely-resolved-start)). Incident peaks from 1 m/kg^(1/3) come
  to 63–117% of Kingery–Bulmash where the balloon gave 11–84%, the impulses within 5–15% on every
  grid, refined or not, and the reach of lung injury at standing height to 0.65–0.80 of the free
  field's (0.50–0.69 before). Reflected peaks close to a wall are no better. Its mixing was fitted
  once, on 10 kg. In a short run the solve stops at half its duration. The record's comparisons
  of structures, envelopes, rooms and districts were all run on the balloon, and their harnesses
  keep it until each is rerun ([still on the balloon
  start](../validation.md#still-on-the-balloon-start)).
- **Structures settle under their own weight before the blast,** instead of starting to sag as
  the shock arrives. The record's comparisons with self-weight moved by 2 mm or 4% at most; the
  office tower's motion off rest before the shock falls from 12.9 mm to 0.13 mm.
- **Broken glazing vents** in refined air too, and failed walls, roofs and slabs of envelopes
  open with it.
- **Walls, slabs and panes thinner than a cell** take a layer of cells in the air; before, a
  0.2 m wall on 0.5 m cells and every 6 mm pane let the blast through.
- **Terrain is cut everywhere,** a deflagration's air included.
- **Concrete envelope walls are judged whichever way they went furthest,** pushed out by a charge
  inside or by their rebound as well as pushed in (UFC 3-340-02 §4-26.2), and vent either way. The
  office tower's two concrete neighbours have 15 of 40 components a level worse; the record's
  walls and slabs are unchanged.
- **A bare element pressed flat on hard ground is removed,** which stopped the large-panel block
  blowing up at 0.18 s.
- **A shock caught at its peak** by the ground-shock column is no longer notched, which had cut its
  averaged peak by a fifth.

**Fixed.**

- **Deflection read the sag under the structure's own weight** before the blast arrived: a settled
  warehouse showed 40 mm with nothing yet loading it. Deflections are now measured from the
  settled shape, as a test's gauges are zeroed, and the sag is shown on its own ("Sag under own
  weight"). The record's comparisons already measured from rest and are unchanged.
- **The intentional-detonation distance (K328)** was shown below the standard's 381 m minimum for
  charges under about 25 kg (353 m for 20 kg). It and its circle in the view now keep the
  minimum, saying what the K-factor alone gives.
- **Map buildings' walls were weaker the coarser the grid:** a façade strip spanned as many metres
  as the cells cut it, 8 m on 8 m cells, and a masonry strip's resistance falls with the square of
  its span. It now spans its storey. An earlier match of Beirut's stone collapses came from this.
- **A refined run under buildings could go wrong without saying so.** Air carried up a column
  that a building stopped went back into a sliver of a cut cell, magnified, and the NaN wave speed
  that followed was taken as the step's largest: Beirut's district refined twice ran on, wrong,
  from 0.875 s. Such air now goes to the last cell at least half air, and a wave speed that is not
  a real positive number stops the batch as unstable. The same run is sound, its peaks 0.85–0.92
  of Kingery–Bulmash to 500 m.

- **Open faces too near a structure moved its late response:** a far-field face takes each wave
  head on, so a blast grazing it loses half its pressure there and the reflection drives the
  structure after the positive phase. On Tyndall's CLT building, test 1, a tight domain gave 29 mm where
  18 mm is converged. The app now warns when an open face is nearer than 4 m/kg^(1/3) to the charge
  or a structure, and so does the structural standing ([open faces near a
  structure](../air-blast-model.md#open-faces-near-a-structure)). Peaks and impulses are unaffected.
  The warehouse, transport hub, deformable wall and terraced row samples meet it. The others keep
  their domains, which meeting it would take 1.6 to 32 times the air for, and leave the choice to
  the user: **Widen to the rule**, beneath the warning, grows the domain's far faces out to it
  without moving anything in the scene, showing the advisor's estimate of the extra air and time
  first, and names any face at the origin that only moving the scene would clear. The warning
  says when the faces matter least: for what the positive phase decides, such as glass, and for a
  charge inside its walls.

**The record.**

- **Terraced houses against wartime bomb damage** (Gilbert's chapter 5): right at a tenth of a
  second, then one or two categories too severe as the cracked front walls go over. Rocking would
  not hold them either: SBEDS's resistance takes in 10–60 J/m² where the blast gives about 600, and
  a bomb that burst partly buried, which Gilbert's charge sets aside, would have loaded them less.
  Fixing it showed that each brick leaf had taken the whole blast; a cavity's two leaves now share
  it.
- **Beirut, 2020,** the record's first comparison at a city's scale: the port explosion over
  14,169 map buildings ([Beirut](../beirut-2020.md)). From 400 m out the shock arrives within
  0.83–1.10 of Rigby's observed times, 25 of 27 within 10%. The glass, converged by 4 m cells,
  blows out the survey's share of façades beyond 1.5 km, but closer in the façades break together
  (nearly all inside 1.25 km where the survey found 0.17–0.77), and it breaks 3.8 times the army's
  count of broken glass on an assumed glazed share. Peaks are 0.68–0.78 of Kingery–Bulmash and
  still rising on finer cells. The structure is too strong and not converged: stone cracks at
  0.5–1 km but falls in 8 of 293 buildings where 7 of the survey's 13 partly collapsed, and
  concrete is hardly damaged beyond 750 m where the surveys found damage to 2 km.
- **Every tight-domain comparison rerun at the open faces' clearance rule** (one case each): Chiquito,
  Wu, Alsubaei's blockwork and Keys within 1–2%; Wang's gauge impulses 2–7% lower (76–84% of
  measured); Mendonça's blast peak 1.5% lower; the chamber's roof 47 mm, left 25 mm up; Yao's 20 kg
  slab a quarter less (126 mm, left 73), its top face still undamaged. The chamber's bench now
  uses the rule's domain by default.
- **Keys's brick walls rerun on today's air,** their record having been taken on the old one: as
  structures, their debris falls 1.60–1.64 m out where the tests' fell 0.23–0.64 m (2.10 m before),
  moved by the air's defaults and by settling, both meant.
- **Yao's large slabs:** the reflected load at the slab's centre within 11% at 1.0 and 1.26
  m/kg^(1/3), 19% low at 0.8; the 20 kg slab's top face undamaged where the test cratered, the
  unsafe side.
- **Nian's blast wall:** in front of it 1–11% below ConWep; behind it shielding less than measured
  (1.9–2.1 times the filtered peaks at 5 kg, 1.25 at 20 kg).
- **Ground motion against Xia et al.'s 1 t burst,** the record's first ground-shock comparison
  (above).
- **Refinement held about structures,** tried as an option and left off: on Tyndall's building it
  raised the front's reflected peak from 19.7 to 21.2 kPa (25.2 on uniform cells) and moved the
  walls by under a millimetre, at 13–54% more time. A lower shock threshold (0.02) gives the uniform
  grid's peak without it ([air-blast model](../air-blast-model.md)).
- **The bands follow what each scene is:** the vented-room bands only for a vented room on the
  unscaled flame, hydrogen's own band, no TNT fireball or cloud bands for a gas explosion, and
  envelope damage bands only for the constructions a scene has.

Known gaps, as the standing says, those on the unsafe side first, are as 0.4.0's (collapse missed,
punching not predicted, slabs too strong, no blast face crushed, close-in damage too little,
shear too strong), with three more: hydrogen vented through a roof reads a fifth to under a half of
the measured, a gas flat's walls are loaded below the inquiry's estimates, and a district's stone and
concrete stand where Beirut's collapsed or were damaged.

`docs/validation.md`, `docs/blind-tests.md` and `docs/validation-review.md` give the details.

It is a study of the numerics, the physics and the performance of blast simulation, not a design
tool, and nothing in it should be used to judge the safety of a real structure. MIT licence.
